Volume IX: Dictionary — Chapter 10: Loanwords from Moryn

"Their language is color. We wrote it down in letters and lost nine tenths of the meaning. The remaining tenth was enough to transform our oceanography." — Guild of Scribes, commentary to the "Deep Lexicon," +2,400 Y.E.

Related sections: → see [Volume VII, Ch.07, "Biology of the Moryn"], → see [Volume VIII, Ch.06, "Ocean Gharra"], → see [Volume IX, Ch.09, "Loanwords from Kel'vash"]

10.1 Context

The Moryn inhabit the depths of Ocean Gharra (1,200–3,000 sha; pressure 120–300 tyazh, T 2–8 °X). Their communication is bioluminescent: sequences of color, brightness, and rhythm perceived over the whole body surface (→ see [Volume VII, Ch.07]).

This makes borrowing fundamentally different from the Kel'vash case. The Kel'vash have sound — alien though it is. The Moryn have no sound at all. Every Moryn "word" in Xha'Ruun is a conventional vocalization of a light pattern, adopted by the Guild of Scribes after first contact in +2,000 Y.E.

There are 26 loans. All are oceanographic, bioluminescent, or social terms for which the Xha'ruun possessed neither the phenomenon nor the concept.

10.2 Rules for Transcribing a Language of Light

Pattern parameter Xha'Ruun correspondence
Wavelength (color) vowel: blue → i, green → e, yellow → a, red → u
Brightness vowel length: dim → short, bright → long
Rhythm (frequency) consonant: slow → m, n, l; fast → r, s, sh
Duration number of syllables
Simultaneity (2+ colors) diphthong or cluster

Hence the sonorant character of all Moryn loans: they abound in m, n, l, r and contain almost no stops. To the ear they are softer than native Xha'Ruun vocabulary — and speakers notice: Moryn loans are called "beautiful," Kel'vash loans "heavy."

Limitation: simultaneous patterns of three or more colors are untranscribable. They are rendered descriptively, in italics, and are not counted as loans.

10.6 The Untranslatable

Concept Why it was not borrowed
Simultaneous "speech" with the whole body no structural analogue — Xha'Ruun is linear in time
Distinguishing 40+ shades of blue Xha'Ruun color vocabulary distinguishes 6
The collective pattern of a shoal the Xha'ruun have no notion of speech without a speaker
"Silence as utterance" rendered descriptively (muranel is only one special case)
Speech by touch-vibration a mixed channel; the Guild declined to transcribe it

The fourth item produced the most famous linguistic controversy of the Era of Unity. Xha'Ruun is obligatory in evidentiality (→ see [Ch.07]): a speaker always marks the source of knowledge. Among the Moryn, however, an utterance may have no author — the pattern is generated by the shoal as a whole. The commission of +2,600 Y.E. ruled that in translation from Moryn a fifth, non-canonical evidential suffix -nal is admissible ("said by many, but by no one in particular"). It is the only suffix in the language that is not taught in schools and occurs solely in translated texts.

Moryn loans (26) are slightly fewer than Kel'vash ones (31), yet their effect on the language ran deeper: they added a fifth evidential suffix and forced the Guild of Scribes to concede, for the first time, that the structure of Xha'Ruun is not universal. The table of "the untranslatable" above is not a list of failures but a map of the language's boundaries — one that is still maintained and extended today.
