Volume IX: Dictionary — Chapter 8: Proper Names

"A name is the shortest sentence in the language. It already contains a subject, a predicate, and a testimony." — Guild of Scribes, "Rules of Naming," +1,400 Y.E.

Related sections: → see [Volume I, Ch.03, "The Planet Théxar"], → see [Volume VIII, Ch.09, "Political Domains"], → see [Volume V, "Mythology"]

8.1 The Principle of Naming

Proper names in Xha'Ruun are neither borrowed nor arbitrary — they are compositional and readable. Every name decomposes into roots and translates literally. A native speaker hearing a city's name for the first time immediately understands its meaning.

Three classes:

Class Structure Example
Cosmonyms root + root (hyphen or apostrophe) Khar'Vex ("Light-Giver")
Toponyms geo-root + determiner Kharak-Thar ("Light-Force")
Anthroponyms root + root, occasionally + suffix Khal-Ven ("Action-Truth")

The apostrophe in writing (Khar'Vex, Xha'Ruun) marks a sacred or self-naming status; the hyphen marks ordinary composition.

Xha'Ruun Analysis Domain

Note that the names of the deities are written with a hyphen, while the star's name takes an apostrophe (Khar'Vex). The distinction is deliberate: the star is a physical body; Khar-Vex is the Voice that sang space. Orthodoxy insists on the strict separation; folk tradition conflates them (→ see [Volume V, "Religion"]).

8.7 Personal Names

An Xha'ruun personal name consists of two roots joined by a hyphen. The first is lineal (conferred by the parents' Guild); the second is personal (chosen at majority, at 18 mormor).

Name Analysis Reading
Khal-Ven action + truth "one who acts by truth"
Ven-Thara truth + force "strong in truth"
Sharin-El small-word + unity "one who unites by word"
Rhaal-Ven archaic-level + truth "steady truth"
Xha-Morin mind + instant "quick of mind"
Vexal-Ar to create + agent "the maker"
Thel-Kharin wanderer + small-light "one who follows the light"
Rass-Xarin unity + child "child of unity"

Transparency of names is a deliberate norm codified by the Guild of Scribes: an untranslatable name counts as a scribal error, not as exotica. When in +2,000 Y.E. the Moryn species had to be named, the commission rejected twelve proposals in a row until it found a root that decomposed. This is the only known case in which the language conceded that a borrowing was unavoidable (→ see [Ch.10]).
