Volume IX: Dictionary — Chapter 11: Phrasebook

"Learn six phrases and they will understand you. Learn when to say which, and they will believe you." — From the manual for ambassadors, Keeper Guild

Related sections: → see [Volume IX, Ch.07, "Grammatical Affixes"], → see [Volume IV, Ch.03, "Grammar Overview"], → see [Volume V, "Etiquette"]

11.1 How to Read This Section

Xha'Ruun is an SOV language (subject — object — verb) with obligatory evidentiality (→ see [Ch.07]). In the phrases below the evidential suffix is set apart: change it and you alter not the politeness but the accountability of what you said.

Marker Meaning
-ven I saw it myself (direct)
-sha I sensed it (indirect)
-tor I infer it (inference)
-rass I was told (reportative)

An evidential error is the commonest mistake of learners and the most conspicuous to a native. To say "it is raining-ven" without having looked out the window is not an imprecision but a lie.

Rass-el. "Peace." Short, between intimates. rass + el

11.6 Formulas of Council and Court

Xha'Ruun Translation Context
Rass-vokh-sha khal-en-ven. "The Council of Unity is in session." opening a session
Ven-ak-thar vex-a-khar. "Let the testimonies be presented." judicial formula
Sha-ven-ven, sha-ven-rass? "Did you see it yourself, or were you told?" the key question in examination
Ven-en-ven — venar-en. "What is witnessed directly is law." legal maxim
Rass-el-mor. "May unity abide forever." closing a session
Tor-khal-torel. "Denied finally." verdict without appeal

The third formula — Sha-ven-ven, sha-ven-rass? — underlies the whole of Xha'Ruun judicial procedure. It is impossible in languages without obligatory evidentiality: there the difference between "saw" and "heard" must be extracted by questioning, whereas here it has already been uttered along with the testimony (→ see [Volume III, "The Legal System"]).

An Xha'Ruun phrasebook cannot be compiled the way phrasebooks for terrestrial languages are: here no "phrase" can be given apart from the speaker's standing. Every line above carries an evidential suffix, and to substitute it is to say something else. Hence the Keeper Guild's practical advice to ambassadors: if you are unsure of your source, use -tor ("I infer"). It is the one suffix that will never be taken for a lie.
