Volume VIII: Atlas — Chapter 8: Geological Map

"Stones remember what water has forgotten. The layers are the pages of a book written long before us. To read them is to know where we came from." — Guild of Geologists, +2,500 Y.E.

Related sections: → see [Volume I, Ch.12, "Geological Evolution"], → see [Volume I, Ch.13, "Continents"], → see [Volume VIII, Ch.10, "Resource Map"], → see [Volume I, Ch.27, "Geodesy"] Length: 15 pages

Introduction

The geological map of Théxar reflects 6.5 billion cycles of the planet's history. The ancient Kharak craton, the young volcanic plateaus of Rhuval, and the isolated massifs of Vexar — each continent bears the imprint of its tectonic fate. This chapter systematizes the geological structure, described in detail in → see [Volume I, Ch.12].

8.1 Tectonic Plates

Théxar has 10 lithospheric plates, of which 3 are continental.

Plate boundaries:

  • Divergent (rifts): the Thalas mid-ocean ridge, the Gharra rift
  • Convergent (subduction): the eastern coast of Rhuval, western Vexar
  • Transform: the Khar-Tor fault

Chapter Summary

  1. 10 lithospheric plates: 3 continental, 7 oceanic.
  2. Kharak is the most ancient craton (5.5 billion cycles); Rhuval is volcanically active.
  3. The Khar-Tor Ridge is a subduction zone, highest point — Mt. Verksan (9,537 sha).
  4. The Thash Trench is the deepest point (2,001 mo).
  5. Seismic activity is moderate, confined to plate boundaries.
