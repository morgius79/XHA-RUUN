Thexar-III: Ruun-El — Sha-El 03: Khal-Ven Rass — Economic System

"Abundance is not a gift of fate. It is the result of 3,000 years of engineering, organization, and patience. We did not find paradise — we built it." — Merchant Guild, economic report, +4 000

Ruun-ak rass: → rhu [Thexar-III, Sha-El 01, "Rass-Vokh Khal-sha"], → rhu [Thexar-II, Sha-El 09, "Khal-Ven The-El"], → rhu [Thexar-II, Sha-El 08, "Xa-Vokh The-El"] Volume: 30 sha-ruun Mor-ven: Centuries-long transition from scarcity economy → abundance economy. Key feature: post-scarcity — basic resources ceased to be limited; economic problem shifted from survival to distribution and meaning. 12 Guilds control 72% of GDP. Labor voluntary. GDP 124 T s.u.

3.1 The Post-Scarcity Economy

Basic resources available to everyone without payment.

Resource Status Provision
Energy Free Fusion + Khar'Vex
Food Free (basic) Automated agriculture
Housing Free (basic) Autonomous blocks
Medicine Free Medic Guild
Education Free Scholars Guild

Post-scarcity does not mean the abolition of economics: the market survives for differentiated goods — handcraft, art, unique services. Value shifts from material possession to quality, uniqueness, and meaning.

3.2 The Guild Economy

72% of GDP controlled by the 12 Guilds. Remaining 28% — independent producers and services.

Guilds = simultaneously economic actors and regulators: own key industries, set standards, organize training (→ rhu [Thexar-II, Sha-El 08, "Xa-Vokh The-El"]).

Energy = the foundation of abundance (→ rhu [Thexar-I, Ruun 33, "Khar-mor rass Thel-ak-mor Khal-vex"]). Practically inexhaustible and cheap energy made production automation and, consequently, post-scarcity possible.

3.4 Distribution

Basic (by need) + market (by preference):

  1. Basic distribution — energy, food, housing, medicine, education — by need, without payment
  2. Market sector — differentiated goods and services — through guild exchanges and independent producers
  3. Social guarantees — pensions, support in work and old age — through the guilds

3.5 Labor and Motivation

Labor is voluntary. Motivation shifts from survival to:

  • Professional fulfillment — guild career
  • Public recognition — contribution to science, culture, infrastructure
  • Personal meaning — creativity, research, service

Unemployment (2.8%) = not an economic problem but a period of retraining or a conscious choice of profession.
