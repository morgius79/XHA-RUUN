Thexar-VII: The-Xa — Sha-El 03: Thar-Mor Xa-Sha — Marine Ecosystems

"Land is an island. Everything else is the ocean. We call ourselves children of the earth, but our blood is salty, and the first cell from which we descend was born in darkness beneath three thousand measures of water." — Guild of Navigators, "Book of the Depths," +3,640 Y.E.

Volume: 20 sha-ruun Mor-ven: The oceans of Théxar (67% of surface) span the northern Thalas and southern Gharra, holding ~980,000 described species and most living matter. The marine biosphere preserves ancient H₂S chemosynthesis, builds reefs from a silicon-calcium composite, and is the homeland of the third sentient species, the Moryn. This digest traces marine communities from the euphotic surface to the hadal trenches.

3.2 Vertical Zonation

The orange spectrum of Khar'Vex (peak ~570 nm) is absorbed faster than blue-green, so the euphotic zone is shallower than expected.

KEY FACT — The euphotic zone extends only to 146 sha (≈120 m), a third shallower than terrestrial. Compensated by chromophyll quantum efficiency (0.92) and deep-water Thal phycobilin (absorption peak 620 nm, working to 240 sha).

3.2.1 The Dysphotic Zone and "Twilight Farms"

The 146–732 sha layer hosts diel vertical migration — the largest regular mass movement of living matter: up to 4.1 Gt of biomass rises during dark hours of the 27.3 h day and descends 300–600 sha at dawn (ascent ~4.5 h, surface stay up to 11 h, descent ~3.8 h), making an efficient organic-carbon pump.

3.3 Producers of the Ocean

Microscopic single-celled producers provide 28% of the planet's total primary production.

Silodiatoma shells are twice as thick as terrestrial diatoms (dissolved Si 0.14 vs 0.07 mmol/L); their settling forms siliceous oozes covering 31% of the abyssal floor.

3.5 Benthos and Reef Systems

3.5.1 Silicon-Calcium Reefs

Reef-builders build skeletons from a silicon-calcium composite (SiO₂·CaCO₃ ≈ 2:3), far less soluble in weakly acidic water.

Parameter Théxar reefs Note
Skeleton material Si-Ca composite Stable at pH 7.6
Maximum growth depth 190 sha Against ~50 m on Earth
Symbionts Algae and chemoautotrophs Double energy source
Growth rate 0.4–1.8 pa/cycle —
Number of reef-building species ~2,400 Of which 640 deep-water

Double symbiosis (photosynthetic + H₂S-oxidizing chemoautotrophic bacteria) lets reefs hold a positive energy balance below the light limit and survive turbidity.

KEY FACT — Field productivity reaches 12 ru/mo² — 600 times the surrounding abyss — yet fields occupy only 1.1 mo² total (<one-millionth of the floor): refugia of ancient metabolic pathways.

3.6.2 Cold Seeps and Sulfide Banks

More than 340 cold seeps (methane/H₂S seepages) are described; total area ~940 mo², nearly a thousand times the hydrothermal fields.

Seep type Depth (sha) Main substrate Number known
Methane (gas-hydrate) 600–3,400 CH₄ 186
Sulfide 200–1,800 H₂S 121
Brine pools 900–2,600 Hypersaline brine + H₂S 33

Brine pools (salinity up to 18%) hold the most extremophilic forms, with lineages that diverged >900 million cycles ago per the Guild of Natural History.

3.6.3 Carrion Communities

A Thal-mor carcass on the abyssal floor creates an ecosystem lasting 40–90 cycles across four stages: scavengers (0.5–2), gnawers (2–8), sulfophilic (8–70), and reef (70–90). The sulfophilic stage releases H₂S from bone lipids, forming chemosynthetic "stepping stones" of dispersal between divided hydrothermal fields.

3.7 Hadal Ecosystems

The hadal zone (below 8,537 sha) is 1.3% of ocean volume — isolated "pockets" with the highest endemism.

Trench Ocean Max depth (sha) Pressure (tyazh) T (°X) Endemism
Thash Gharra 12,010 ~952 46.6 91%
Khar-Tor Thalas 10,270 ~814 46.1 84%
Irkhen-Deep Thalas 9,140 ~724 46.3 76%
Koss-Trough Gharra 8,900 ~705 45.8 71%

The Thash Trench is the planet's deepest point; bottom water is 1.6°X warmer than comparable trenches (elevated geothermal flux), supporting 610 described species, 555 of them endemic.

Adaptations of the hadal fauna: silicon-organic membranes (as in Moryn and Kel'vash); piezolytes rising linearly with depth; complete loss of gas cavities including auditory; metabolism 6–9× lower than shelf relatives; lifespans up to 240 cycles at small body size.

3.8 The Moryn as an Ecological Factor

The Gharra Ocean is an ecosystem modified by the third sentient species.

Aspect Scale of impact
Cultivated chemosynthetic fields ~2.4 mo² of artificially maintained communities
Domesticated species 14 (symbiotic bacteria, 6 mollusc species, 3 worm species)
Regulated populations Gharra-Lance and 4 other apex predators
Bioluminescent "lighting" of settlements Altered diel rhythm of fauna within a radius of up to 6 mo
Reserve zones established by the Moryn 0.19 million mo² (within the Gharra Deep zone)

The Moryn are the only sentient species whose agriculture is built entirely on chemosynthesis; their "fields" are artificially widened seeps fed by H₂S. This practice, ≥40,000 cycles old, makes them the planet's oldest farmers — though they have never seen sun or plants.

3.10 Threats and Conservation

Marine ecosystems survived heavy pressure during the Crisis Era (−2,100 – −750) and the Rebirth; modern policy rests on the Law of Silence and the Law of Consent.

Threat Peak of impact Modern status
Overfishing −1,400 – +900 Sustainable catch of 24 million t/cycle
Acidification (industrial CO₂) +200 – +1,400 Reversed; pH restored to 7.8–8.2
Bottom trawling +600 – +1,900 Total ban since +1,940 Y.E.

Protected waters: 8% of ocean area has reserve status; the largest zone, Gharra Deep (0.51 million mo²), covers the southern trenches and the Moryn's main settlement area. Since +2,100 Y.E. no marine species extinction has been recorded.
