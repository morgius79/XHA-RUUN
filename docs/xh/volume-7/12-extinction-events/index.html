Thexar-VII: The-Xa — Sha-El 12: Mor-Thal Xa-El — Extinction Events

"Five times everything died. Five times everything returned. The sixth extinction — the last — we stopped ourselves. The memory of the five holds us." — Law of the Keepers of Biodiversity, +2,100 Y.E.

Recovery of diversity >10 million cycles Time to return to the pre-event level

12.2 The First Extinction: The Oxygen Catastrophe

Date: 4.0–3.8 billion cycles ago | Duration: ~200 million cycles | Cause: Free O₂ accumulation | Lost: ~40% of anaerobic species

Before the event, atmospheric O₂ was <0.02%; life ran on H₂S chemosynthesis. About 4.1 billion cycles ago the first oxygenic phototrophs appeared.

Time (billion Y.A.) O₂ in the atmosphere Consequence
4.1 0.02% First oxygenic phototrophs
4.0 0.5% Beginning of the extinction of methanogens
3.9 3.8% Oxidation of Fe²⁺ in the oceans, red oozes
3.8 12% Formation of the ozone layer, end of anaerobic dominance

Consequences: ~40% anaerobe extinction; survival in refugia (hydrothermal vents, ocean depths); ozone layer formed; aerobic respiration (18× more efficient) established. Occurred 1.6 billion cycles earlier than on Earth but was gradual enough to allow adaptation.

12.3 The Second Extinction: The Volcanic Catastrophe

Date: 1.8 billion cycles ago | Duration: ~1.2 million cycles | Cause: Super-eruption, global winter | Lost: ~55%

Peak plume volcanism 1.85–1.75 billion Y.A. formed massive traps on the Vaal-Kharak continent (predecessor of Kharak); 4.2 million mo³ of basaltic magma — 3× the largest known trap event.

Atmospheric release: SO₂ 6.1×10¹⁴ ru; H₂S 2.8×10¹⁴ ru; CO₂ 1.4×10¹⁵ ru; volcanic dust 3.2×10¹⁵ ru. Sulfate aerosol blocked 68% of sunlight for up to 800 cycles; surface T fell 18°X; photosynthesis shrank 84%.

Consequences: 62% of primary producers extinct; cascading loss to fifth trophic level; ocean pH fell to 7.2; reef systems collapsed; deep-water chemosynthetic refugia survived. Recovery: 42 million cycles.

12.4 The Third Extinction: Total Glaciation

Date: 900 million cycles ago | Duration: ~0.8 million cycles | Cause: "Snowball Théxar" | Lost: ~65%

Trigger: falling CO₂ (to 180 ppm), continents near equator, ice–albedo feedback. At maximum (890 million Y.A.) the entire surface was ice-covered up to 1,200 sha thick.

Parameter Before Maximum After
Average surface T +13.6°X −41°X +10.2°X
Ice cover 18% 100% 24%
Photosynthesis (% of normal) 100 0.4 82
Planet biomass 1,340 Gt C 31 Gt C 940 Gt C

Exit: CO₂ accumulated under ice over 18 million cycles to 6,800 ppm, triggering rapid (~80,000 cycles) deglaciation. Terrestrial species hit hardest; multicellular radiation and first large predators followed.

12.5 The Fourth Extinction: Impact

Date: 250 million cycles ago | Duration: <0.5 million cycles | Cause: Asteroid ~3 mo diameter | Lost: ~70%

An asteroid 3.05 mo fell into the Gharra Ocean. Impact energy: 8.4×10²³ J (≈200 million Mt TNT).

Consequence Scale
Crater 65 mo diameter (today the Thash Trench)
Tsunami Up to 2,440 sha high
Release into the atmosphere 1.2×10¹⁶ ru of rock, dust, water vapor
Fires 38% of the planet's forests burned
"Impact winter" 15 cycles
Temperature (fall) −22°X in the first 2 cycles

Three-stage mechanism: mechanical impact (0–10 days) → impact winter (0–15 cycles) → CO₂ greenhouse phase +8°X (15–2,000 cycles). Largest fauna extinction in planet history; 8 orders of the Thermochromadae vanished; radiation of the ancestors of the Xha'Ruun followed (240–180 million Y.A.). Only extinction of the five not tied to internal planetary dynamics — a random event that set the path to reason.

12.6 The Fifth Extinction: Anthropogenic (Incomplete)

Date: −2,100 – −750 Y.E. (Crisis Era) | Duration: 1,350 cycles | Cause: Xha'Ruun activity | Lost: ~12% (stopped)

Factor Mechanism Scale
Deforestation Agriculture, fuel 34% of forest cover destroyed
Overfishing Unrestricted fishing Marine fauna biomass −62%
Pollution Industrial runoff 18% of rivers dead
Urbanization Growth of cities 2.4% of land built over
Hunting Uncontrolled hunting 8 species of large herbivores exterminated

Turning point: in −1,200 Y.E. the Guild of Natural History warned the Council of collapse within 600 cycles. Reform sequence: −1,150 Law on Protected Territories (12% of land); −980 ban on uncontrolled logging; −840 fishing quotas; −750 Treaty of Unity (union of eight regions).

Result: extinction stopped. Since +2,100 Y.E. no species disappearance; 8 species reintroduced (including the Ghal-thass, restored +2,850 Y.E.); forest cover restored to 40% (+12% over 500 cycles). The only stopped extinction — absent from the geological record, present in cultural memory as the Lesson of the Crisis.

12.7 Patterns of the Extinctions

Extinction Cause Lost Recovery Surviving groups
I (Oxygen) Biogenic 40% 120 million Y.A. Aerobes, refugia
II (Volcanic) Geological 55% 42 million Y.A. Chemosynthetics
III (Glacial) Climatic 65% 38 million Y.A. Deep-water
IV (Impact) Cosmic 70% 24 million Y.A. Small generalists
V (Anthropogenic) Biogenic 12% (stopped) — All except large specialists

Common patterns: (1) selection against specialists; (2) deep-water chemosynthetic refugia survived all five with minimal loss; (3) large forms (>100 kh) went extinct 3.4× more often than small (<1 kh); (4) recovery time scales with severity (10–40 million cycles); (5) each event opened new adaptive radiation — Thermochromadae after IV, primate-analogues after III. "Rule of Five": one extinction per 840 million cycles — close to the frequency of Wilson supercycles.

12.8 Lessons for the Management of the Biosphere

Principle Justification
The "six steps" principle Assessing consequences 6 generations ahead
The 12% rule No less than 12% of land — reserves
The sustainable catch law Taking no more than 68% of the annual increment
The ban on destroying species Even "useless" ones — they may prove key
Reintroduction The duty to restore species extinct through the civilization's fault
