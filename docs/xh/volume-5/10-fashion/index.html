Thexar-V: Xa-Sha-Ven — Sha-El 10: Ven-Vex Khal-sha — Fashion and Clothing

"Clothing is not what we wear. Clothing is how we tell the world who we are before we manage to open our mouths. But if clothing lies — we lie." — From the code of the Guild of Artists, department "Fabric and Form," +2 800 Y.E.

Ruun-ak rass: → rhu [Thexar-V, Sha-El 01, "Sha-Ven-El"], → rhu [Thexar-V, Sha-El 12, "Ven-Rass-El"], → rhu [Thexar-III, Sha-El 06, "Xha-Mor The-Rass"], → rhu [Thexar-I, Ruun 05, "Xha'Ruun Sha-Mor"] Volume: 18 sha-ruun Mor-ven: Fashion is a projection of anatomy, climate, and history. Six limbs require a cut that does not restrict mobility; tetrachromatic vision (incl. UV) makes color visible differently to different species; pheromone sensitivity turns fabric into a chemical interface. The casteless society built clothing as a sign of function, not status — fashion was never an instrument of class division.

10.1 Functional Foundations

Anatomy and cut:

Element Function Solution
Base belt Fixing clothing Wide belt with six loops
"Veks-sleeve" Freedom of the middle hands Separate sleeve with ventilation
"Chest flap" Access to the chest heart Quick-opening fastener (two hearts)
Lower belt Support Elastic fit, free abdominal blood flow

Key principle: clothing must not restrict the lift of the veks — the middle pair of arms. A restricting sleeve is "failed" and rejected even with decorative merits.

Climate zones:

Zone Temperature Clothing
Equatorial +28…+42°C Minimal: base belt, light draperies
Temperate −2…+18°C Standard: upper cloak, belt, trousers
Polar −48…−6°C Multi-layer with insulation

Thermoregulation (heliotherapy through silicon-carbon channels): "light windows" (open back/shoulders), "shadow shields" (visors, hoods), "pheromone meshes" (large-knit weave that does not block emission).

10.2 History of Clothing

Era Material Style Function
Pre-Civilization Hides, fibers Draping, wrapping Protection
Early Civilization Woven linen, leather First belts and cloaks Ritual distinction
First Empire Silk, wool, dyes Class clothing Demonstration of rank
Dark Ages Coarse wool Utilitarian minimalism Survival
Revival Fine fabrics Regional styles Domain identity
Era of Unity Synthetic/natural blends Functional standard Guild and regional identity

The Imperial period was the only era of class clothing; the Era of Unity inherited the conscious rejection of "clothing of rank."

10.3 Modern Clothing: Standards and Variations

The basic standard (temperate): base belt (six loops), upper cloak with "light windows," veks-sleeves, lower trousers, open footwear (closed for polar).

Guild elements — a detail, not a cut:

Guild Element Color/Material
Scholars Hexagonal patch on the belt Azure
Engineers Metal belt buckle Bronze
Medics White cloak lapel White
Agrarians Green belt trim Green
Builders Reinforced veks-sleeves Gray
Merchants Narrow belt over the main one Gold
Keepers Book-symbol on the chest Azure with gold
Warriors Flame on the shoulder Red
Artists Spiral ornament Purple
Miners Crystal buckle Crystal
Navigators Star on the collar Silver
Scribes Feather-symbol on the sleeve Azure

The absence of a full uniform is fundamental: a Council member and a craftsman of the same guild wear the same mark; only quality differs.

Domain styles — from Kharak-Thar conservatism (dark tones) to Vexar-Shan transparency (many "light windows"), from Rhuval-Ven layering to Polar asceticism.

10.5 Fabrics and Materials

Natural: tar-vine fiber (light, summer), kharru wool (warm), deep-sea wool (waterproof), chromophilic threads (copper-red, golden).

Synthetic: thermoplastic (thin, warm), pheromone mesh, UV-permeable fabric (for tetrachromats), water-repellent composite.

Color palette (→ rhu [Thexar-V, Sha-El 01, "Sha-Ven-El"]): gold #c9a84c (light of Khar'Vex), azure #5b7db5 (sky of Théxar), bronze #8b6f4e (earth), copper #b87333 (warmth), purple #6b3fa0 (night, Serrin).

UV-invisible layers — part of a pattern in UV pigment distinguishable only by the Xha'Ruun: a "double image," inaccessible to Kel'vash and Moryn.

10.6 Clothing Production

Indicator Value
Production centers 240
Employed in fabric production ~1.2 million
Accessibility of the basic set 100% of the population

Guild production (Artists "Fabric and Form" + Builders "Materials") coexists with craft workshops (authorial pieces; price = payment for mastery, not a barrier) and individual tailoring. The "maternal belt" — passed from the female to children regardless of sex — is linked to the value of knowledge transmission; an old belt is resewn, not thrown away.

10.7 Fashion and Ethics

Transparency — clothing must not "lie": fake guild marks, imitation of materials ("wood under metal"), and concealment of defects are prohibited (→ rhu [Thexar-V, Sha-El 12, "Ven-Rass-El"]).

Ecological production — the Law of Silence regulates extraction; synthetic fabrics are an ecological alternative (→ rhu [Thexar-III, Sha-El 09, "Vokh-Shan Khal-sha"]).

No "disposable fashion" — clothing is passed on (older to younger, leaving a guild to entering) — a legacy of the Dark Ages.

10.8 The Fashion of Three Species

Species Materials Style
Xha'Ri Fabrics, leather The system described above
Kel'vash Stone, metal, acid-resistant polymers "Armor," plates
Moryn Bioluminescent pigments on the skin "Living light," patterns

Exchange of ornaments — Kel'vash carve Xha'Ri textile patterns on plates; Xha'Ri apply motifs from the Kel'vash "stone book" to fabrics. Light patterns — phosphorescent pigments imitating the Moryn "language of light" on ritual garments for "light meals" (→ rhu [Thexar-V, Sha-El 09, "Khal-The-El"]).

10.9 Children's Clothing

Age Features
0–3 (larva) No clothing required
3–8 Minimal; base belt, light cloaks
8–15 Full set, without guild marks
15+ Guild elements, choice of style

The guild mark appears after choosing a guild (15–20 years); before that, "neutral" clothing — the principle of "equal start."

10.10 Storage and Transmission

Practice Content

Resewing Fabric is not thrown away
Ritual burning Only on Remembrance Day — clothing of the deceased without heirs

Ruun Ven

  1. Clothing is determined by anatomy (six limbs, heliotherapy, pheromone communication) and climate (three zones).
  2. Historically, clothing went from hides to functional textiles; the imperial "class" period is rejected.
  3. Modern clothing is a functional standard with guild and domain variations; status is not marked by cut, only by details.
  4. Transparency: clothing must not "lie" — fake marks and material imitation are prohibited.
  5. The three species share no common fashion, but exchange ornaments and light patterns as a dialogue.
