Thexar-V: Xa-Sha-Ven — Sha-El 07: The-Ven Khal-sha — Science and Culture

"Science does not live in institutions. Institutions are only its home. Science lives in the way a mother explains to her child why the sky is bronze, and in the fact that the child has the right to ask: 'Did you see it yourself?'" — Keeper of Knowledge Ven-Theks-Mor, "On the People's Mind," +3 640 Y.E.

Knowledge (ven) is the highest value of the canon; culture reproduces it each generation without coercion.

7.1 Knowledge as a Cultural Value

The three pillars of ethics — knowledge (ven), harmony (rass), truth (ven-ash) — are arranged in strict order (→ rhu [Thexar-V, Sha-El 12, "Ven-Rass-El"]).

Formulation Meaning Everyday Consequence
Ven khar-vex — "Knowledge is light" Like the radiation of Khar'Vex, it does not diminish when shared Refusal to charge for an explanation
Ven-mor — "Debt of knowledge" Obligation to pass what one has learned to the archives and descendants Observation diaries are handed over to the district
Ven-ash rass-ur — "Truth is the foundation of consent" A dispute is resolved by fact, not status Citing a source in an everyday argument
Mor-ven — "Ignorance is no shame; concealment is shame" Admitting ignorance is socially safe "I don't know" does not lower one's standing

Key asymmetry: ignorance is not stigmatized, concealment of knowledge is severely stigmatized. A scientist who carried a discovery to the grave stands lower than a thief. The asymmetry formed as a reaction to the Dark Ages (−2 100 – −1 800) (→ rhu [Thexar-II, Sha-El 04, "Thar-Xa-Khal"]).

7.2 Evidentiality: Science Built into Language

The deepest manifestation of scientific culture is grammar: the language obliges the speaker to mark the source of information (→ rhu [Thexar-IV, Sha-El 06, "Sha-Ven Rass"]).

Marker Type of Evidence Scientific Equivalent
-ven Direct (saw oneself) Primary observation
-sha Indirect (heard, sensed) Instrumental data
-tor Inferential (conclusion from traces) Theoretical inference
-rass Reportative (hearsay) Citation of a source

Consequences:

  • It is impossible to tell a story without indicating the source. A child masters the distinction by age 4–5.
  • Plagiarism is grammatically visible — claiming another's observation requires a false -ven marker instead of -rass: unethical becomes falsehood, and direct lying with -ven is a grave offense (→ rhu [Thexar-V, Sha-El 12, "Ven-Rass-El"]).
  • Rumors are marked — the reportative -rass sounds like "I'm just repeating what I heard"; the mass spread of unverified information is impeded at the level of morphology.

KEY FACT

"We did not teach the people the scientific method — we inherited a language in which one cannot speak otherwise." The sequence, however, is the reverse: evidentiality formed in the Ancient period (−6 200 – −2 100) as an instrument of imperial law (→ rhu [Thexar-IV, Sha-El 11, "The-Sha-Ruun The-El"]).

7.3 Six: Number as Aesthetics

The senary system (→ rhu [Thexar-IV, Sha-El 09, "Mor-Ruun Khal-sha"]) is also a cultural tuning fork: the number 6 is perceived as "correct," "complete," "calm."

Domain Manifestation
Biology 6 limbs, 6 nucleotides of the code
Mathematics Digits mor, ven, sha, tor, vex, rass
Architecture Hexagonal plans, hexagonal ornaments
Poetry Hexameter: 6 syllables per line, caesura after the 3rd
Law Six foundational laws of the Union
Sport Hexathlon, counting in base 6

The Six Questions of the Observer (canonical didactic sequence, attributed to Vel-Shar, ~−480):

  1. What do I see? (observation)
  2. How do I know this? (evidentiality)
  3. What else could it mean? (alternative hypotheses)
  4. What must be true if I am right? (prediction)
  5. How can this be tested? (experiment)
  6. To whom am I obliged to pass this on? (debt of knowledge)

The sixth question is cultural: the method ends not with a conclusion but with an obligation to transmit.

7.4 The Image of the Scientist in Culture

The scientist (ven-sha, "truth-word") is the central figure of the cultural imagination — the place held elsewhere by the warrior or ruler.

Era Dominant Image
Early Civilization (−9 800 – −6 200) Sky-watcher, counter of days
Flowering (−6 200 – −2 100) Imperial measurer, land surveyor
Crisis (−2 100 – −750) Keeper of a surviving scroll
Revival (−750 – +850) Discoverer, disputant
Era of Unity (+850 – present) Member of a college, co-author

In the Era of Unity the heroic loner is displaced by the collective subject; a plot about a lone genius implies a violation of the debt of knowledge. The martyr of knowledge — a keeper who carried out of a burning library not gold but a scroll — is a unique legacy of the Dark Ages, the core of the theater's repertoire.

7.5 Science and Religion: From Conflict to Separation

Stage Dates Content
Fusion −9 800 – −2 100 Astronomy = temple practice; priest = accountant
Conflict −2 100 – +500 Dispute over the Empire's fall and the nature of the epidemic
Separation +500 – present Science answers "how," the doctrine — "why"

The key episode: the dispute over the Red Rot (−2 120 – −2 105). The temple explanation (dissonance in the Symphony, punishment) clashed with the empirical one (a transmissible pathogen). The empirical explanation won, but religion was not displaced — it was functionally narrowed: questions of fact ceased to be a matter of doctrine. Society is predominantly secular; conflict between science and faith is not a topical theme (→ rhu [Thexar-V, Sha-El 06, "Vokh-Shan The-El"]).

7.6 Popularization of Knowledge

Form Description Frequency
House of Questions (Ven-Vex) Open building where anyone may ask the duty master of a guild Continuous
Open College Public examination of a new result, questions from the floor Monthly
Procession of Instruments Instruments brought to the square on Knowledge Day Annual
District Diary Collective journal of everyday observations Continuous
Dispute of Six Ritualized debate of six parties over a disputed question As needed

The District Diary is a grassroots network of field observations covering all 182 districts for more than three millennia — one of the most valuable datasets in planetary science. The Dispute of Six: six participants defend six pre-assigned positions, including deliberately weak ones; no winner — the outcome is a protocol of questions left open.

7.7 Science in Art

The interpenetration derives from the aesthetic principle of transparency: a work must not hide how it was made.

Direction Content Period
Schematism Depicting an object from outside and in cross-section −4 000 – present
Spectral painting UV pigments invisible to other species +1 800 – present
Bio-art Living cultures as an artistic material +2 400 – present
Data-to-sound Sonification of scientific series +3 100 – present
Resonance installation Acoustic demonstration of physical laws +3 500 – present

Spectral painting is a consequence of tetrachromatic vision (320–780 nm, incl. UV-A); a layer of many canvases is inaccessible to Kel'vash and Moryn — a debate on "visual exclusivity" in art. Sonification relies on the auditory range 8 Hz – 42 kHz: scientific series converted to sound are perceived with a resolution unavailable to visual analysis.

7.8 The Scientific Holiday: Knowledge Day

Knowledge Day (Ven-Thex) — the 1st of Ven-Khal, the anniversary of the founding of the First Library (−4 800) (→ rhu [Thexar-V, Sha-El 11, "Thar-Mor Rass"]).

Ritual structure:

  1. Morning — the Return. Handing personal records to the district archive; the debt of knowledge (ven-mor).
  2. Noon — the Procession of Instruments. Anyone may look into the eyepiece, touch a sample, ask a question.
  3. Afternoon — the Open College. Public examination of the year's most important result.
  4. Evening — the Dispute of Six.
  5. Night — the Observation. Collective contemplation of the night sky, from the calendar priests of Early Civilization (→ rhu [Thexar-I, Ruun 25, "Sha-Ven Xa-The"]).

On Knowledge Day children may ask any master any question, and the master is obliged to answer or to admit ignorance.

The best-known image: the "Long Experiment" (Tor-mor-ven) — Kel'vash experiments outlasting a single Xha'ri lifetime; a metaphor for patience ("to begin a task whose fruits one will not see"). The reverse influence: the Kel'vash adapted the six questions to rock vibrations; the Moryn number four steps — an unsettled methodological dispute (→ rhu [Thexar-II, Sha-El 14, "Xha-Sha Xha-Mor"]).

7.11 Boundaries: What the Culture of Knowledge Does Not Allow

Three prohibitions are cultural — observed because violating them arouses revulsion, not because it invites punishment.

Prohibition Justification Historical Source
Destruction of knowledge Knowledge belongs to descendants Dark Ages, loss of libraries
Self-aware AI Creating a mind without the consent of the created Law of Consent
Interference in protected zones The intrinsic value of nature Law of Silence

The prohibition of self-aware AI derives from the Law of Consent (→ rhu [Thexar-VI, Sha-El 05, "Mor-Xha-El"]). The Law of Silence formed a scientific asceticism: in protected zones observation is permitted, intervention is not; such scientists are called "listeners" (→ rhu [Thexar-III, Sha-El 09, "Vokh-Shan Khal-sha"]).
