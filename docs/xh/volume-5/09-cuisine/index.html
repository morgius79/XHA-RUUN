Thexar-V: Xa-Sha-Ven — Sha-El 09: Khal-The-El — Cuisine and Gastronomy

"Tell me what you eat — and I will tell you where you were born. Tell me with whom you eat — and I will tell you who you are. Tell me why you cook — and I will tell you what our people will be like in a thousand years." — From the treatise "On Nourishment and the People," Guild of Agrarians, +2 100 Y.E.

Ruun-ak rass: → rhu [Thexar-V, Sha-El 07, "The-Ven Khal-sha"], → rhu [Thexar-V, Sha-El 12, "Ven-Rass-El"], → rhu [Thexar-V, Sha-El 11, "Thar-Mor Rass"], → rhu [Thexar-I, Ruun 05, "Xha'Ruun Sha-Mor"] Volume: 20 sha-ruun Mor-ven: Cuisine is one of the most complete projections of the species' physiology: six limbs determine the utensils, the requirement of sulfur in the diet and the receptor for heavy-metal salts determine a flavor palette unfamiliar to other worlds, pheromone communication builds "scent" elements into the meal. The kitchen is an institution — a zone of oral knowledge transmission and a reproduction of the civilization's history on the plate.

9.1 Physiology of Nourishment

Parameter Value Culinary Consequence
Diet type Omnivorous, protein-plant Abundance of combined dishes
Additional taste receptor For heavy-metal salts "Metallic taste" — a separate category
Sulfur requirement Mandatory component Sulfur-containing products indispensable
Metabolism Endothermic with heliotherapy Warm food in winter, light food in heat
Heat treatment Raw foods rare Fermentation and boiling are basic
Water balance High requirement Soups and drinks structural

Key fact: the mandatory presence of sulfur (a consequence of the role of hydrogen sulfide in metabolism). Table salt (NaCl) is less popular than selenium salt and sulfur condiments; a dish without a sulfur component is perceived as "unfinished."

9.2 Staple Products

Grains:

  • Thar-rice — high-protein, the staple cereal; porridges, "thar-bread"
  • Khar-millet — small golden grain; porridges, drinks
  • Ven-ear — cold-resistant Polar cereal; flour for "polar bread"

Animal protein:

  • Kharru meat — festive dishes, "long" stews
  • Thalas fish — soups, smoking
  • Deep fauna of Gharra — salting, fermentation
  • Sulfur-beans — the main weekday sulfur component

Vegetation — the chromophilic palette (copper-red to golden) sets the "red plate": selenium-herb (analogue of salt and pepper), tar-vine (dressings), hinterland roots, fermented leaves (sauces). The pigment chromophyll (absorption peak 570 nm) marks ripeness: a golden hue guarantees maturity; green fruits are rarely eaten.

9.3 Culinary Methods

Method Share Description
Fermentation ~40% of dishes The key method; "maturing" food is an art
Boiling Basic Soups, porridges, broths
Stewing Festive "Long" dishes, up to 6 hours
Smoking Fish, meat Dry processing with tar-vine smoke
Baking Hexagonal ovens Bread, vegetables
Raw serving A rarity Only fresh marine fauna

Fermentation as a worldview: ~40% of products ferment. Fermented food keeps without cold, contains sulfur compounds, and "lives." The cultural parallel is obvious: maturing is valued, speed is suspect (→ rhu [Thexar-V, Sha-El 12, "Ven-Rass-El"]).

9.4 Table Etiquette

The Six Utensils — the diner's place includes six: four hand implements (upper limbs) and two "support" implements (middle limbs, steadying the plates). Used in pairs: upper hands cut, middle hands grasp.

Meal Time Composition
First intake Morning Light: porridge, fermented drink
Second intake Noon Main: soup, grain dish, vegetables
Third intake Evening Festive or family: protein dish
Rule Meaning
Eat in the silence of conversation The table is an exchange of knowledge
Do not discuss the prey Discussing the cost is bad form
The sulfur component is mandatory A physiological and taste norm
The host tastes first The "host's debt," from the leader's poison-testing duty
Leftovers are not thrown away Into fermentation or to those in need

9.5 National Dishes

  • Kharak — "Khar-long": a festive stew of kharru meat, sulfur-beans, hinterland roots, simmered six hours in a hexagonal oven; served on Days of Renewal; a play on the "long experiment" — patience
  • Rhuval — "Rhuval heat": a soup of fermented leaves, selenium-herb, and "sulfur dew"; "burning but not scalding" — heat from sulfur compounds, not capsaicin
  • Vexar — "Vexar transparency": a cold soup of golden fruits and chromophilic leaves; the philosophy of transparency read in the layers
  • Island Domain — "Thalas salt": smoked, salted Thalas fish with sulfide-rich Thalophyta algae
  • Polar — "Polar bread": a dense flatbread of ven-ear flour with deep-fauna fat; caloric, ascetic, made in reserve for months

Alcohol culture is moderate: spirits are not a commodity of pressure or profit; public drunkenness is a social stain.

"Inherited starter" — some family starters have been maintained for over a thousand years; the oldest documented (the "khar-mother" of the Khar-Tor district) is registered by the Guild of Keepers as cultural heritage — maintained continuously since +1 900.

Holiday Dish Symbolism

Unity Day (Rass-Vek) "Dish of consent" — six kinds on one dish Six sides of unity

The "gray dish" — the rarest deliberate violation of the norm: made without sulfur, taste-wise "unfinished," remembering the irreplaceable (the Red Rot epidemic, −2 120 – −2 105).

9.9 Culinary Apprenticeship

Stage Age Content
"Helper" 6–10 Cutting, cleaning, table setting
"Carrier" 10–15 Simple dishes, caring for the starter
"Cook" 15–20 Mastering methods, fermentation
"Master of the kitchen" 20+ Authorship of dishes, running a kitchen

Oral transmission is fundamental: recipes are rarely written, and then only as "hints" without proportions; the precise tuning passes master to apprentice in cooking together. A written recipe is "incomplete knowledge" — a cultural analogue of evidentiality: a record, not an experience.

9.10 The Cuisine of Three Species

The physiologies are incompatible down to the chemistry of solvents: Xha'ri food (aqueous) is unsuitable for the Kel'vash (sulfuric acid as solvent) and the Moryn (deep-sea).

Species Medium Food
Xha'ri Terrestrial Grain, meat, vegetables
Kel'vash Acidic springs Siliceous rocks, chemoautotrophic symbionts
Moryn Depths of Gharra Chemosynthetic biota of hydrothermal vents

"Stone meal" — a diplomatic ritual: negotiations while observing each other's meal; each side "treats" not with food but with spectacle and knowledge. The "light meal" with the Moryn — an exchange of bioluminescent "toasts" (→ rhu [Thexar-I, Ruun 39, "Moryn"]). "A meal without food" entered diplomatic vocabulary.

9.11 Social and Economic Aspects

Aspect Fact
Agriculture ~12% of GDP; hydroponics, vertical farms

Food surpluses Into fermentation or "storehouses"

The "common kitchens" — public catering in every district: any meal accessible to any resident; payment symbolic or in labor; a legacy of the recovery programs after the Dark Ages.
