Thexar-II Sha-El 17: Thar-Mor The-El — The History of the Calendar

"Time is not the ticking of a clock. Time is the rhythm that the planet sets for its children." — From the "Book of Time," +2 400 Y.E.

Ruun-ak rass: → rhu [Thexar-II, Sha-El 02, "Xha-Sha Vokh-El"], → rhu [Thexar-I, Ruun 25, "Sha-Ven Xa-The"], → rhu [Thexar-I, Ruun 34, "Théxar-mor The-ven Khal-sha-ak"] Volume: 20 sha-ruun Mor-ven: Calendar = first tool with which the civilization ordered time. From observing natural cycles (moon phases, solstices) → unified planetary reckoning (0 y.e.). Modern unified year: 412.7 days, 14 months, 5 Days of Renewal (Ven-Khal). Accuracy 1 day / ~3 200 y.

17.1 Development of the Calendar

Period Calendar Basis
−10 000 – −8 000 Lunar (Serrin) Moon phases
−8 000 – −5 000 Solar-lunar Serrin + solstices
−5 000 – −2 150 Imperial 14 months
−2 150 – −750 Regional Divergent systems
−750 – +850 Pre-Unity 14 months + 5 days
0 – present Unified calendar Standard

Earliest calendars relied on the phases of Serrin — the largest moon, visible from the surface (→ rhu [Thexar-I, Ruun 25, "Sha-Ven Xa-The"]). Transition to solar-lunar (−8 000) linked to agriculture: planting cycles required attachment to seasons, not just moon phases.

17.2 The Unified Calendar

Adopted in 0 y.e. (EV-0009) as the result of centuries of standardization begun by the Unity Treaty:

  • Year lasts 412.7 local days (~10 040 standard hours)
  • 14 months of ~29.5 days, synchronized with the phase of Serrin
  • 5 "intercalary" Days of Renewal (Ven-Khal) at the end of the year; in every 5th year — 6 days
  • Accuracy: 1 day of error per ~3 200 years

Month names (Volume II reference canon): Khar-Ven, Serrin-Tash, Thalas-Mor, Ven-Khal, Rass-Ur, Sha-En, Khar-Tor, The-Nur, Gharra-Vek, Mor-Irkh, Thar-Ghal, Ruun-Shan, Serrin-Ur, Veks-An.

📝 Editor's note: the month names adopted in Volume II differ from the list in [Thexar-I, Ruun 34]. The discrepancy is recorded and subject to reconciliation in the canon.

17.3 Days of Renewal

The five Days of Renewal (Ven-Khal) — a unique element of the calendar: they belong to no month and complete the year. Not a "leap adjustment" but a full social institution — a period of celebration, renewal of obligations, and collective rituals. Every fifth year a sixth Day of Renewal is added to compensate for accumulated error.
