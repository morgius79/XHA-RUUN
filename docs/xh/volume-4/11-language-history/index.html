Thexar-IV: The-Sha-Ruun — Sha-El 11: The-Sha-Ruun The-El — History of the Language

"Language is a living organism. It is born, grows, changes, and — if not recorded — dies. Twelve thousand years of our speech — that is twelve thousand years of our memory." — From "History of the Xha'Ruun Language," Archive of the Academy

Ruun-ak rass: → rhu [Thexar-IV, Sha-El 01, "Sha-Thar The-El"], → rhu [Thexar-IV, Sha-El 07, "Sharuu Khal-sha"], → rhu [Thexar-IV, Sha-El 08, "Sha-Ven The"], → rhu [Thexar-IV, Sha-El 10, "Shaven Xha-Mor"], → rhu [Thexar-II, Sha-El 02, "Xha-Sha Vokh-El"], → rhu [Thexar-II, Sha-El 03, "Verksan The-El"] Volume: 20 sha-ruun Mor-ven: The language developed together with the civilization for more than 12 000 years (→ rhu [Thexar-II, Sha-El 01, "Xha'ri Thar-Vokh"]). Its history is a history of unification and collapse, standardization and fragmentation. Five periods coincide with the epochs of the civilization: every political turn reflected in the language, every linguistic shift — in society.

Each period left its trace: the roots of the Proto period, the grammar of the Empire, the dialectal diversity of the Classical period, the norm of the Renaissance.

11.2 Proto-Xha'Ruun: The Birth of the Language (−9 800 – −6 200)

At the beginning of Early Civilization a corpus of ~500 semantic roots took shape (→ rhu [Thexar-IV, Sha-El 08, "Sha-Ven The"]).

  • −9 780 — the invention of the proto-syllabary independently in three regions — Kharak, Rhuval, and Vexar — subsequently merged into a single system (→ rhu [Thexar-II, Sha-El 02, "Xha-Sha Vokh-El"])
  • Root-isolating, without developed morphology; meaning by word order and particles
  • Oral tradition: epic tales and genealogies transmitted by word of mouth (→ rhu [Thexar-IV, Sha-El 07, "Sharuu Khal-sha"])

11.3 Ancient Xha'Ruun: Imperial Standardization (−6 200 – −2 100)

The founding of the First Empire in −6 150 (the Verksan dynasty) made the language an instrument of governance (→ rhu [Thexar-II, Sha-El 03, "Verksan The-El"]). For the first time, a single standard covered the entire northern continent.

  • Imperial chancellery — unified norms of writing and official speech
  • Dissemination — through colonies, trade routes, and roads (→ rhu [Thexar-II, Sha-El 09, "Khal-Ven The-El"])
  • Grammatical consolidation — the foundations of the 14 cases (→ rhu [Thexar-IV, Sha-El 04, "The-El Sha-Thar"]) and of agglutinative structure (→ rhu [Thexar-IV, Sha-El 03, "Khal-sha Rass"])
  • Writing — from the three proto-traditions a syllabary took shape, the predecessor of modern Sharuu

11.4 The Classical Period: Fragmentation (−2 100 – −750)

The fall of the First Empire in −2 150 and the Dark Ages (−2 100 – −1 800) destroyed the single standard (→ rhu [Thexar-II, Sha-El 04, "Thar-Xa-Khal"]).

  • Regional variants — without central authority the norms diverged; from them the modern dialects grew (→ rhu [Thexar-IV, Sha-El 10, "Shaven Xha-Mor"])
  • Preservation of classical writing — temples and archives kept records in the classical language; thus it became "sacred" and bookish (→ rhu [Thexar-II, Sha-El 12, "Khal-Sha The-El"])
  • Technical vocabulary — the steam engine in −1 200 gave rise to the first layer of technical lexicon
  • The classical language — the period gave its name to the variant on which the main literary and scientific works are written

11.5 Revived Xha'Ruun: Restandardization (−750 – +850)

The Unity Treaty in −750 united eight regions and returned a single standard (→ rhu [Thexar-II, Sha-El 05, "Ven-Khal Thar-El"]).

  • Reform of writing — the ordering of the syllabary: 124 syllabic signs and 48 logograms fixed (→ rhu [Thexar-IV, Sha-El 07, "Sharuu Khal-sha"])
  • Unified calendar (year 0) — required a new lexicon of time reckoning (→ rhu [Thexar-II, Sha-El 17, "Thar-Mor The-El"])
  • Flourishing of the sciences — vocabulary supplemented with terms of mathematics and astronomy; the senary numeral system introduced (→ rhu [Thexar-IV, Sha-El 09, "Mor-Ruun Khal-sha"])
  • The Revived standard — based on the classical language, with simplified orthography

11.6 Modern Xha'Ruun: The Era of Unity (+850 – Present)

The founding of the Council of Unity in +850 established the unified language as the norm of the entire planet (→ rhu [Thexar-II, Sha-El 06, "Rass-Vokh Thar-El"]).

  • A unified language — the Kharak-Thar standard is taught everywhere; mass media and education sustain unity (→ rhu [Thexar-IV, Sha-El 10, "Shaven Xha-Mor"])
  • A lexical explosion — modern vocabulary exceeds 50 000 words; growth provided by agglutinative word formation (→ rhu [Thexar-IV, Sha-El 08, "Sha-Ven The"])
  • Digital writing — Sharuu is encoded and used in digital systems (→ rhu [Thexar-IV, Sha-El 07, "Sharuu Khal-sha"])
  • Dialects persist — markers of regional identity, with full mutual intelligibility
