Thexar-IV: The-Sha-Ruun — Sha-El 02: Sha-Thar Vokh-El — Phonology

"The throat of a Xha'Ruun is a musical instrument. Within it, 24 consonants and 8 vowels — 32 notes from which an infinite symphony of speech is composed." — Academician of Phonetics, Khal-Ven

Ruun-ak rass: → rhu [Thexar-IV, Sha-El 01, "Sha-Thar The-El"], → rhu [Thexar-IV, Sha-El 03, "Grammar: Overview"] Volume: 20 sha-ruun Mor-ven: Phonology = rules of organizing sounds into words and phrases. If phonetics describes the inventory, phonology describes the regularities of its use: syllable structure, stress, vowel harmony, assimilation. Syllable (C)(C)V(C)(C); dynamic stress on the root; vowel harmony by backness.

2.1 Phonotactics

Syllable structure: (C)(C)V(C)(C)

Position Possible Sounds
C₁ (initial) Any consonant
C₂ (initial) r, l, w, j
V (nucleus) Any vowel
C₃ (final) Any consonant
C₄ (final) s, ʃ, t, d, n

Constraints on C₂ (only sonorants r, l, w, j) and C₄ (only s, ʃ, t, d, n) = the key rules of phonotactics. Forbidden combinations resolved by epenthesis of the neutral ə.

2.2 Stress

Dynamic, on the first syllable of the root. Non-phonemic (does not change word meaning).

Stress serves as a word boundary and does not depend on morphological complexity: with suffixes added it remains on the root, facilitating recognition of the lexical core.

2.3 Vowel Harmony

Assimilation by backness: suffixes adapt to the vowel of the root.

Harmony = the most important morphophonological process: case and derivational suffixes have variants (front/back vowels) selected according to the root vowel. Example: the locative suffix -ven/-van is selected by the backness of the root vowel.

2.4 Assimilation

Consonant assimilation:

  • By voicing — voicing/devoicing at junctions
  • By place — assimilation of n → ŋ before velars
  • Progressive — in suffixes after voiceless consonants

These processes motivate the allomorphy of morphemes (→ rhu [Thexar-IV, Sha-El 04, "14 Cases"]).

2.5 Tone and Prosody

The tonal system (up to five tones, → rhu [Thexar-IV, Sha-El 01, "Sha-Thar The-El"]) plays a prosodic role: distinguishes grammatical forms (for example, evidentiality, → rhu [Thexar-IV, Sha-El 06, "Evidentiality"]) and marks phrase boundaries.
