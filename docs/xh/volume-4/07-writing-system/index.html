Thexar-IV: The-Sha-Ruun — Sha-El 07: Sharuu Khal-sha — Sharuu Writing System

"Sharuu is not an alphabet. Sharuu is a world. Each sign is not merely a sound, but a concept. 124 syllabic signs and 48 logograms — keys to the universe." — From "History of Writing," Academy

Ruun-ak rass: → rhu [Thexar-I, Ruun 06, "The-Sha-Ruun"], → rhu [Thexar-IV, Sha-El 08, "Lexicon and Word Formation"] Volume: 30 sha-ruun Mor-ven: One of the oldest continuously used writing systems (almost 10 000 years). Mixed character — syllabary + logograms — reflects the history of writing development (→ rhu [Thexar-II, Sha-El 02, "Xha-Sha Vokh-El"]). 124 syllabic signs + 48 logograms. Modular grid 16×16, 64 base graphemes.

7.1 Script Type

Mixed: syllabary (124 signs) + logograms (48 signs).

Component Count Function
Syllabic signs (V, CV, CCV) 124 Phonetic notation
Logograms 48 Semantic roots
Numerals (senary) 6 0–5
Punctuation 8 Separation

Syllabary covers the syllabic structure of the language; logograms encode semantic roots (often polysemantic). Numerals senary — a legacy of senary mathematics (→ rhu [Thexar-I, Ruun 34, "Théxar-mor The-ven Khal-sha-ak"]).

7.2 Direction

Right to left, top to bottom. Paragraphs arranged in columns.

Direction reflects physiology and history: the oldest texts were carved on stone stelae from top to bottom and right to left — the direction of the hand in carving.

7.3 Graphemes

Modular grid 16×16. 64 base graphemes (G-0001–G-0064), combining into syllabic signs.

Built hierarchically:

  • 64 base graphemes — the simplest elements (strokes, arcs, dots)
  • Syllabic signs — combinations of 2–4 base graphemes
  • Logograms — stylized compositions of syllabic signs

The modular grid ensures machine recognition and calligraphic flexibility.

7.4 Phonetic Writing and Tones

Tones (→ rhu [Thexar-IV, Sha-El 01, "Sha-Thar The-El"]) conveyed by diacritics above the syllabic signs. System of tonal diacritics — 5 distinctive tones.

7.5 Writing and Society

  • Literacy 100% — writing is mandatory in education
  • Sacred texts — the canon is written in classical Sharuu
  • Double notation — logograms + phonetic transcription for unambiguity
