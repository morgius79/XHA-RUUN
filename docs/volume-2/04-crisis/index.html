Volume II: History — Chapter 4: The Era of Crisis

"The Empire did not fall to an external enemy — it rotted from within. When the land ceased to bear, the people ceased to believe. And when the people ceased to believe — the walls crumbled on their own." — From "The Lament for the First Empire", −1,800

Related sections: → see [Volume II, Ch.03, "The First Empire"], → see [Volume II, Ch.05, "The Second Unity"], → see [Volume I, Ch.07, "History of Civilization"] Length: 30 pages Era: Era of Crisis, −2,100 – −750 (1,350 years)

Introduction

The Era of Crisis is the darkest period in the history of Xha'Ruun civilization. In 1,350 years it endured the fall of the first planetary empire, ecological collapse, a pandemic, and the loss of most accumulated knowledge. Yet it was precisely from this crisis that the model defining the civilization's further path grew: a federation of regions, a treaty as the basis of political order, and — in the long run — the Council of Unity.

The era falls into three phases: the fall of the Empire and the first years of disintegration (−2,150 – −1,800), the dark ages (−1,800 – −1,200), and recovery (−1,200 – −750), ending with the formation of the eight regional unions.

4.1 Causes of the Fall

The First Empire was not destroyed by external conquest — it was brought down by a combination of ecological, climatic, and social factors accumulated over centuries.

4.1.1 Ecological Collapse

The main cause of the fall was the exhaustion of the resource base. Intensive farming of the Kharak Central Plain without crop rotation led to:

  • Soil depletion — loss of ~40% of humus over 1,000 years;
  • Salinization of irrigated lands in the valleys;
  • A ~60% decline in yields by the end of the era;
  • The −2,150 famine, which claimed ~1 million lives.

The Empire, built on the agricultural production of the Central Plain, undermined its own foundation. By −2,200 grain production no longer covered the population's needs — the deficit was offset by imports from Rhuval, which drained the treasury together with it.

4.1.2 Climate Shift

A cooling of −2.5°C, lasting ~200 years (≈−2,250 – −2,050), was superimposed on the exhausted economy. It was caused by a series of volcanic winters.

Year Event T drop Consequences
−2,220 Tor-6 eruption −1.5°C 3-year crop failure
−2,180 Raal-1 eruption −2.8°C Famine, epidemics
−2,150 Crisis maximum −3.0°C Fall of the Empire

The combination of anthropogenic soil depletion and natural cooling created a double stress: yields fell below the survival level for the same population.

4.1.3 Social Factors

The ecological crisis was amplified by social stratification:

  • The gap between the elite (2% of the population) and the majority;
  • Corruption of the imperial bureaucracy;
  • Rising taxes amid falling yields;
  • Slave and peasant uprisings (−2,180, −2,160).

The elite continued to consume at imperial standards at a time when the provinces were starving. This undermined the dynasty's legitimacy faster than the crop failures themselves.

4.2 The Fall (−2,150)

The year of the Empire's fall — −2,150 (EV-0006). Events unfolded rapidly:

  1. Spring −2,150: a hunger riot in the capital Khar-Vexar;
  2. Summer −2,150: Emperor Verkmyraen V flees south;
  3. Autumn −2,150: the capital is destroyed by the rebels;
  4. Winter −2,150: the Empire disintegrates into 24 independent states.

4.2.1 Fate of the Dynasty

The last emperor Verkmyraen V was killed while attempting to flee. None of his heirs could restore power: the fall was too swift, and the dynasty's legitimacy had been destroyed by decades of crisis.

The destruction of the capital also had symbolic significance: with it perished a large part of the imperial archives. The surviving copies of the Imperial Code, taken to temples and libraries, became the core of the legal tradition of subsequent eras.

4.3 The Dark Ages (−2,150 – −750)

A period of decline lasting ~1,400 years. Its content is not the complete disappearance of civilization but fragmentation and local preservation of knowledge.

4.3.1 Demographic Decline

The population of Kharak fell from ~18 million to ~4 million (−75%). Causes — famine, epidemics, and the migration of people from exhausted lands to coastal and highland areas.

4.3.2 Loss of Knowledge

  • Writing survived in only ~40% of regions;
  • The Imperial Code was lost for 600 years (preserved only in the Irkhen Library);
  • Metallurgy regressed to bronze;
  • Trade between continents virtually ceased.

The loss was selective: economic skills (pottery, construction, breeding) survived everywhere, while administrative and scientific knowledge concentrated in a few centers.

4.3.3 Centers of Preservation

Knowledge was preserved in religious centers and monasteries:

Center Region Preserved knowledge
Thar Temple Thar Valley Writing, astronomy
Ven Monastery Serrin Plateau Medicine, botany
Irkhen Library Irkhen Island The Imperial Code
Raal School Raal Plateau Metallurgy

These four centers formed the framework from which the intellectual tradition of the Second Unity subsequently grew.

4.3.4 The "Red Rot" Pandemic

In the first decades of the decline the planet was struck by the "Red Rot" pandemic (−2,120 – −2,105). By the Keepers' estimates, it claimed ~200 million lives across the planet — for comparison, Kharak's population at that time was ~18 million. The epidemic finished off the remnants of imperial institutions and accelerated the disintegration into independent states.

The pandemic also has long-term significance: it entrenched in the civilization's cultural memory a fear of biological disasters, later reflected in strict sanitary norms and in the ethics of restricting biological research.

4.4 Recovery (−1,500 – −750)

The gradual exit from the crisis took seven centuries:

Period Process
−1,500 – −1,200 Restoration of agriculture
−1,200 – −900 Resumption of interregional trade
−900 – −750 Unification of regions into unions

By −800 8 large regional unions had formed on Kharak, which later became the basis of the Unity Treaty. Each union formed around an economic core: the Thar Valley — administration, the Serrin coast — trade, Irkhen — industry, the Raal plateau — mining, Khar-Tor — farming, Shan — energy, Vexar — technology, Serrin-Ur — culture (→ see [Volume II, Ch.05, "The Second Unity"]).

4.5 Legacy of the Era

The Era of Crisis left the civilization three important lessons:

  1. The limits of extensive growth — a resource model that ignores ecology destroys itself (a lesson applied in the Era of Unity in the Growth Commandment);
  2. The value of preserving knowledge — the keeper institutions (monasteries, libraries) became the prototype of the future Keeper guilds;
  3. Political experience of fragmentation — the memory of 1,400 years of disunity entrenched in the culture a striving for unity, realized in the Unity Treaty.

Chapter Summary

  1. The First Empire fell in −2,150 due to a combination of ecological collapse, cooling, and social stratification.
  2. Cooling of −2.5°C over ~200 years (volcanic winters) deepened the crisis.
  3. The dark ages lasted ~1,400 years; Kharak's population fell by 75% (from ~18 million to ~4 million).
  4. Knowledge was preserved in four religious centers (Thar, Ven, Irkhen, Raal).
  5. The "Red Rot" pandemic (−2,120 – −2,105) claimed ~200 million lives across the planet.
  6. By −800 the 8 regional unions formed — the basis of the Unity Treaty.
