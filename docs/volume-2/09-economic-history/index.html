Volume II: History — Chapter 9: Economic History

"From the hoe to the fusion reactor — a path of 12,000 years. From barter to post-scarcity — a path of 4,000 years. Economics is the history of our freedom." — Guild of Merchants, economic treatise, +3,800

Related sections: → see [Volume II, Ch.03, "The First Empire"], → see [Volume I, Ch.09, "Technology"], → see [Volume II, Ch.08, "History of the Guilds"] Length: 25 pages

Introduction

The economic history of Xha'Ruun civilization is the history of the successive overcoming of scarcity: from the appropriative economy of the pre-civilization era to the post-scarcity economy of the present. Each transition was linked to a technological shift and changed the social structure of society. This chapter traces the economic eras and analyzes the modern model, in which the problem of scarcity has been replaced by the problem of distribution.

9.1 The Eras of the Economy

Era Period Character Main resource
Appropriative −15,000 – −9,800 Hunting, gathering Game, fruits
Agrarian −9,800 – −5,500 Farming, livestock Grain, meat
Craft −5,500 – −2,150 Metals, pottery Copper, bronze
Imperial / crisis −2,150 – −750 Decline, regression —
Renaissance −750 – −1,200 Trade, cities Goods
Industrial −1,200 – +1,420 Machines, factories Steam, coal
Thermonuclear +1,420 – present Abundance Energy

The boundaries of the eras are determined by technological shifts: the transition to agriculture (−9,800), metallurgy (−5,500), the industrial revolution (−1,200, the steam engine), and fusion energy (+1,420). The economy always outran the political system — and the institutions (Empire, Treaty, Council) arose in response to economic needs.

9.2 Key Transitions

9.2.1 From Appropriation to Production (−9,800)

The transition to agriculture and domestication (kharru, ~−10,200) created a surplus — the economic foundation of civilization. The first accumulations appeared, and with them written accounting (−9,780) and cities (−7,800).

9.2.2 The Imperial Economy (−6,150 – −2,150)

The First Empire created the first integrated economy: unified measures, roads, a tax system, and division of labor between regions. Its fall demonstrated the limit of extensive growth: the imperial model, exhausting the Central Plain's soils, destroyed its own resource base (→ see [Volume II, Ch.04, "The Era of Crisis"]).

9.2.3 Industrialization (−1,200)

The invention of the steam engine (Thal-Ven, Shan-En, −1,200) began the industrial era. Electrification (−240) and subsequent automation completed the transition from craft production to industrial.

9.2.4 Post-Scarcity (+1,420 – present)

Controlled fusion (+1,420) made energy practically inexhaustible and cheap. Combined with the automation of extraction and production, this led to a post-scarcity economy: basic resources ceased to be limited.

9.3 The Post-Scarcity Economy

The modern Xha'Ruun economy is post-scarcity:

  • Basic resources (energy, food, housing) — available to everyone without payment;
  • Handcrafted and artisanal goods — market-based (prestige goods);
  • Labor — voluntary, organized on the guild principle;
  • Planetary GDP: 124 trillion solar units;
  • GDP per capita: 44,000 s.u.

Key features of the post-scarcity model:

  1. Near-zero cost for basic goods — energy, food, and housing are distributed by need, not by purchasing power;
  2. The market survives for differentiated goods — artisanal products, works of art, unique services;
  3. Motivation shifts from survival to meaning — work becomes a voluntary contribution to the guild and society, not a necessity;
  4. The Growth Commandment limits demographic expansion so that the resource base remains sustainable.

9.4 The Role of the Guilds in the Economy

The economic structure of the present is determined by 12 guilds (→ see [Volume II, Ch.08, "History of the Guilds"]):

  • Production and services organized on the guild principle (Engineers, Agronomists, Builders, Miners, Navigators);
  • Distribution and trade — the sphere of the Merchants Guild;
  • Accounting and administration — the Scribes Guild;
  • Knowledge and science — the Scholars and Keepers guilds.

The guilds are simultaneously economic actors and regulators: they set quality standards and ensure their observance.

Chapter Summary

  1. The economy passed through seven eras — from appropriative to post-scarcity.
  2. Key transitions: agriculture (−9,800), metallurgy (−5,500), industrialization (−1,200), fusion (+1,420).
  3. The fall of the First Empire demonstrated the limit of extensive growth.
  4. Post-scarcity economy: basic goods free, the market survives for prestige goods.
  5. The guilds are simultaneously economic actors and regulators.
  6. GDP: 124 trillion s.u., 44,000 s.u. per capita; the Growth Commandment ensures sustainability.
