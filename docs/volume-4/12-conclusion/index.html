Volume IV: Language — Conclusion

"Language is not merely a means of communication. Language is a way of thinking. Xha'Ruun grammar — with its 14 cases and mandatory evidentiality — is no accident. It is a reflection of how our people see the world: precisely, honestly, responsibly." — Keeper of Knowledge, +4 028 Y.E.

Related sections: → see [Volume IV, Ch.01–11], → see [Volume II, Ch.06, "The Era of Unity"] Length: 10 pages

Introduction

Volume IV "Language" described the Xha'Ruun language from sounds to the numeral system: phonetics and phonology, grammar with 14 cases, the verbal system, mandatory evidentiality, the Sharuu writing system, the lexicon, the senary numeral system, dialects, and the history of the language. The conclusion brings these descriptions into a single picture and defines the place of the language in culture and history.

Key Ideas

  1. Precision — 14 cases and mandatory evidentiality allow expressing an exact picture of the world: who speaks, where they know from, how it is connected (→ see [Volume IV, Ch.04, "14 Cases"], → see [Volume IV, Ch.06, "Evidentiality"]).
  2. Agglutination — the lexicon grows not by borrowing but by combining roots; ~500 roots generate 50 000+ words (→ see [Volume IV, Ch.08, "Lexicon and Word Formation"]).
  3. Unity in diversity — a single standard and five mutually intelligible dialects; the language unites without unifying (→ see [Volume IV, Ch.10, "Dialects"]).
  4. Writing as memory — Sharuu (124 syllabic signs + 48 logograms) preserved the language through centuries of crises (→ see [Volume IV, Ch.07, "Sharuu Writing System"]).
  5. Six as a constant — the senary system was reflected in mathematics, the calendar, and measurements (→ see [Volume IV, Ch.09, "Senary Numeral System"]).

Language and History

The modern language is the result of the processing of historical experience (→ see [Volume IV, Ch.11, "History of the Language"]). Imperial standardization laid the foundations of the grammar; the crisis gave rise to dialectal diversity; the Unity Treaty returned the norm. The language survived all political catastrophes precisely because it was written down: writing became a "repository" that survived where institutions collapsed (→ see [Volume II, Ch.04, "The Era of Crisis"]).

Looking Ahead

The language faces new challenges:

  • Stellar expansion — will the unity of the language survive the settlement of other stars?
  • Interspecies communication — how do the languages of the three species coexist (→ see [Volume II, Ch.14, "History of Three Species"])?
  • The digital age — how will the Sharuu writing system change in digital systems (→ see [Volume IV, Ch.07, "Sharuu Writing System"])?

The answers to these questions will be determined by the next chapter of history (→ see [Volume II, Ch.07, "The Modern Era"]).

Summary

  1. The language is agglutinative, SOV, with 14 cases and mandatory evidentiality.
  2. The Sharuu writing system: 124 syllabic signs + 48 logograms.
  3. The senary system is a cultural constant of the civilization.
  4. Five mutually intelligible dialects under a single standard.
  5. The history of the language is a mirror of the history of the civilization.
