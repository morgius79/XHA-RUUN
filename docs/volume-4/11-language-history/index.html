Volume IV: Language — Chapter 11: History of the Language

"Language is a living organism. It is born, grows, changes, and — if not recorded — dies. Twelve thousand years of our speech — that is twelve thousand years of our memory." — From "History of the Xha'Ruun Language," Archive of the Academy

Introduction

The Xha'Ruun language developed together with the civilization over more than twelve thousand years (→ see [Volume II, Ch.01, "The Pre-Civilization Era"]). Its history is a history of unification and collapse, of standardization and fragmentation. The periods of the language coincide with the epochs of the civilization established in the historical canon (→ see [Volume II, Ch.06, "The Era of Unity"]): every political turn was reflected in the language, and every linguistic shift — in society.

11.1 Periods of Development

The history of the language is divided into five periods, coinciding with the epochs of the civilization. The boundaries of the periods are key events of history: the invention of writing, the founding and fall of the First Empire, the Unity Treaty.

Period Dates (civ. years) Epoch of civilization Characteristic
Proto-Xha'Ruun −9 800 – −6 200 Early Civilization Formation, 500 roots, proto-writing
Ancient Xha'Ruun −6 200 – −2 100 Era of Flourishing Imperial standard, dissemination
Classical −2 100 – −750 Era of Crisis Fragmentation, regional variants
Revived −750 – +850 Renaissance Restandardization, flourishing of writing
Modern +850 – present Era of Unity Unified language, 50 000+ words

Each period left its trace in the modern language: the roots of the Proto period, the grammar of the Empire, the dialectal diversity of the Classical period, and the norm of the Renaissance.

11.2 Proto-Xha'Ruun: The Birth of the Language (−9 800 – −6 200)

At the beginning of Early Civilization a corpus of approximately 500 semantic roots took shape (→ see [Volume IV, Ch.08, "Lexicon and Word Formation"]). The language was oral; the main achievement of the period was the invention of proto-writing.

  • −9 780 — the invention of the proto-syllabary independently in three regions (→ see [Volume II, Ch.02, "Early Civilization"]). The three writing traditions — on Kharak, Rhuval, and Vexar — subsequently merged into a single system.
  • The proto-language is root-isolating, without developed morphology. Meaning was conveyed by word order and particles.
  • Oral tradition: epic tales and genealogies transmitted by word of mouth (→ see [Volume IV, Ch.07, "Sharuu Writing System"]).

11.3 Ancient Xha'Ruun: Imperial Standardization (−6 200 – −2 100)

The founding of the First Empire in −6 150 (the Verksan dynasty) made the language an instrument of governance (→ see [Volume II, Ch.03, "The First Empire (Era of Flourishing)"]). For the first time, a single standard covered the entire northern continent.

  • The Imperial chancellery — unified norms of writing and official speech; edicts and codes were recorded in the standard language.
  • Dissemination — through colonies, trade routes, and roads (→ see [Volume II, Ch.09, "Economic History"]), the standard spread beyond Kharak.
  • Grammatical consolidation — in this period the foundations of the 14 cases (→ see [Volume IV, Ch.04, "14 Cases"]) and of the agglutinative structure (→ see [Volume IV, Ch.03, "Grammar: Overview"]) took shape.
  • Writing — from the three proto-traditions a syllabary took shape, the predecessor of the modern Sharuu (→ see [Volume IV, Ch.07, "Sharuu Writing System"]).

11.4 The Classical Period: Fragmentation (−2 100 – −750)

The fall of the First Empire in −2 150 and the ensuing Dark Ages (−2 100 – −1 800) destroyed the single standard (→ see [Volume II, Ch.04, "The Era of Crisis"]).

  • Regional variants — without central authority the norms diverged; from them the modern dialects subsequently grew (→ see [Volume IV, Ch.10, "Dialects"]).
  • Preservation of classical writing — temples and archives continued to keep records in the classical language; in this way it became "sacred" and bookish (→ see [Volume II, Ch.12, "Legal History"]).
  • Technical vocabulary — the invention of the steam engine in −1 200 gave rise to the first layer of technical lexicon.
  • The classical language — the period gave its name to the "classical" variant, on which the main literary and scientific works are written.

11.5 Revived Xha'Ruun: Restandardization (−750 – +850)

The signing of the "Unity Treaty" in −750 united eight regions and returned a single standard to the language (→ see [Volume II, Ch.05, "The Renaissance and the Second Unity"]).

  • Reform of writing — the ordering of the syllabary, the fixing of 124 syllabic signs and 48 logograms (→ see [Volume IV, Ch.07, "Sharuu Writing System"]).
  • Introduction of the unified calendar (year 0) — required a new lexicon of time reckoning (→ see [Volume II, Ch.17, "History of the Calendar"]).
  • The flourishing of the sciences — the vocabulary was supplemented with terms of mathematics and astronomy; the senary numeral system was introduced (→ see [Volume IV, Ch.09, "Senary Numeral System"]).
  • The Revived standard — based on the classical language, but with simplified orthography.

11.6 Modern Xha'Ruun: The Era of Unity (+850 – Present)

The founding of the Council of Unity in +850 established the unified language as the norm of the entire planet (→ see [Volume II, Ch.06, "The Era of Unity"]).

  • A unified language — the Kharak-Thar standard is taught everywhere; mass media and education sustain unity (→ see [Volume IV, Ch.10, "Dialects"]).
  • A lexical explosion — the modern vocabulary exceeds 50 000 words; the growth is provided by agglutinative word formation (→ see [Volume IV, Ch.08, "Lexicon and Word Formation"]).
  • Digital writing — Sharuu is encoded and used in digital systems (→ see [Volume IV, Ch.07, "Sharuu Writing System"]).
  • The dialects persist — as markers of regional identity, with full mutual intelligibility (→ see [Volume IV, Ch.10]).

11.7 Lexical Growth

The growth of the vocabulary reflects the development of the civilization: from the 500 roots of the proto-language to 50 000+ modern words.

Period Words Main source of growth
Proto-Xha'Ruun 500 Basic semantic roots
Ancient 5 000 Imperial administration, law
Classical 8 000 Technology, crafts, book culture
Revived 20 000 Science, mathematics, calendar
Modern 50 000+ Technologies, mass media, science

Chapter Summary

  1. Five periods of the language coincide with the epochs of the civilization.
  2. Proto-writing was invented in −9 780 independently in three regions.
  3. Imperial standardization (−6 200 – −2 100) laid the foundations of grammar and the norm.
  4. The crisis gave rise to regional variants — the ancestors of the modern dialects.
  5. The Unity Treaty and the Era of Unity restored the single standard; the modern vocabulary — 50 000+ words.
