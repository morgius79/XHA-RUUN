Volume III: Society — Chapter 9: Law of Silence

"Silence is not the absence of sound. Silence is the absence of interference. There are places where nature must be left to itself." — From the Law of Silence, CANON.md §4.3

Related sections: → see [Volume I, Ch.21, "Ecosystems"], → see [Volume I, Ch.19, "Natural Resources"], → see [Volume II, Ch.18, "Isolation of Vexar"] Length: 25 pages

Introduction

The Law of Silence is one of the six fundamental laws of the Union, establishing nature's right to freedom from technological interference. Its origins lie in the ecological thought of Vexar — the first civilization to create nature reserves (→ see [Volume II, Ch.18, "Isolation of Vexar"]) — and in the catastrophic lesson of the First Empire (→ see [Volume II, Ch.04, "The Era of Crisis"]).

9.1 The Law

Certain zones of the planet are declared technology-free reserves: industrial activity, extraction, construction, and other interference are prohibited there. The law protects not only individual species but entire ecosystems — from deep-sea to polar.

The law's principle: not "preservation for man" but recognition of nature's intrinsic value — ecosystems exist not to serve civilization but in their own right.

9.2 Protected Zones

Zone Area Type Prohibitions
Gharra Deep 512 thousand mo² Oceanic Industry, extraction
Vexar Rainforest 132 thousand mo² Forest Logging, construction
Khar-Tor Peaks 74 thousand mo² Mountain Building
Shan Core 24.8 thousand mo² Desert All activities
Ven-Deep (subglacial) 496 mo² Lake Drilling

Total area of protected zones — hundreds of thousands of square kilometers. They represent all major biomes: ocean, forest, mountains, desert, subglacial lakes.

9.3 Protection and Monitoring

Protected zones are guarded by a system without technological interference:

  • Observation — remote monitoring without the presence of machinery;
  • Scientific access — limited research programs;
  • Protection — prevention of illegal activity.

The principle of "Silence" extends to scientific research: observation is permitted, interference is not.

9.4 The Law of Silence in Culture

The Law of Silence is not only a legal norm but also a cultural value:

  • Ecological education — a mandatory part of the school curriculum;
  • Ritual significance — visiting protected zones without technology — a form of spiritual practice;
  • Identity — the law inherits Vexar's ecological ethics.

Chapter Summary

  1. The Law of Silence — one of the six fundamental laws of the Union.
  2. Technology-free reserves: ocean, forest, mountains, desert, subglacial lakes.
  3. The principle — recognition of nature's intrinsic value.
  4. Protection — remote monitoring, limited scientific access.
  5. The law is a cultural value and part of the civilization's identity.
