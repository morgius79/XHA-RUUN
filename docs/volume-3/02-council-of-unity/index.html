Volume III: Society — Chapter 2: Council of Unity

"49 voices — one truth." — Motto of the Council of Unity

Related sections: → see [Volume III, Ch.01, "System of Government"], → see [Volume II, Ch.06, "The Era of Unity"], → see [Volume II, Ch.11, "History of the Eight Domains"] Length: 25 pages

Introduction

The Council of Unity (Rass-Vokh-Sha) — the supreme body of the civilization, founded in +850 and continuously operating for over three millennia. Its structure is the institutional peak of Xha'Ruun political evolution: a balance of professional and territorial interests, fixed in the Council Charter.

2.1 Composition

Role Count Elected by Term
Council Chair 1 The Council 10 years
Guild Chamber 24 (2 from each of 12 guilds) The guilds 8 years
Region Chamber 25 (from eight domains) The domains 6 years
Keeper of Knowledge 1 Lifelong (appointed) Until departure

Total 49 members (24 + 25) plus the Keeper without vote. The 24/25 distribution is a deliberate bias toward the regions: the guilds represent the professional cross-section, the domains the territorial one, and neither side can dominate without the other's consent.

2.2 Decision-Making Process

  1. Submitting a question — any member of the Council;
  2. Discussion — minimum 3 days (for constitutional questions — 30 days);
  3. Voting — simple majority in each chamber;
  4. Keeper's veto — if a decision contradicts the canon, the Keeper suspends it and returns it for reconsideration;
  5. Publication — per the Law of Knowledge.

Key decisions (Charter amendments, interspecies questions, planetary programs) require the consent of both chambers and a qualified majority (2/3).

2.3 The Guild Chamber

The Guild Chamber — 24 members, 2 from each of the 12 guilds. Guilds delegate their representatives by internal procedures; delegates must represent not narrow guild interests but the professional cross-section of society as a whole.

Chamber's purview:

  • Economy, science, technology;
  • Quality standards and professional norms;
  • Education and healthcare;
  • Space programs.

2.4 The Region Chamber

The Region Chamber — 25 members from eight domains (distribution by population; larger domains have more representatives).

Chamber's purview:

  • Territorial structure;
  • Cultural autonomy of domains;
  • Demographic policy;
  • Center-periphery balance.

2.5 The Keeper of Knowledge

The Keeper of Knowledge — a lifelong constitutional office (→ see [Volume III, Ch.01, "System of Government"]). Without vote, but with the right of veto on canon-conformity questions.

2.6 Committees and Commissions

To prepare decisions, the Council forms standing and ad-hoc committees:

  • Planning Committee — long-term programs;
  • Ethics Committee — science and interspecies questions;
  • Canon Commission — jointly with the Keeper;
  • Budget Committee — allocation of planetary resources.

Committees gather expertise from guilds and domains and prepare draft decisions for plenary sessions.

Chapter Summary

  1. The Council of Unity — 49 members: 24 from guilds, 25 from regions, the Keeper without vote.
  2. Process: submission → discussion → voting → Keeper's veto → publication.
  3. The Guild Chamber — professional cross-section; the Region Chamber — territorial.
  4. Key decisions require both chambers' consent and 2/3 of votes.
  5. The Keeper of Knowledge — guarantor of decisions' canon conformity.
