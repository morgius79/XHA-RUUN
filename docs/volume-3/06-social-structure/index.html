Volume III: Society — Chapter 6: Social Structure

"There are no castes. There are no estates. There is only your contribution to the common cause — and the respect you have earned." — From the Code of the Council of Unity

Related sections: → see [Volume III, Ch.01, "System of Government"], → see [Volume III, Ch.04, "The 12 Guilds: Structure"] Length: 25 pages

Introduction

The social structure of Xha'Ruun civilization is the result of a conscious rejection of the estate model. Formally, society is casteless: social status is determined not by origin but by contribution, education, and professional recognition. The mechanism is the guild system, which ensures high social mobility.

6.1 Formal Structure

Xha'Ruun society is formally casteless. Social status is determined by:

Factor Weight Comment
Guild 40% Membership and rank
Merit 30% Contribution to society
Education 20% Level of knowledge
Origin 10% Family (minimal influence)

Guild membership and rank are the main status factor (40%). Origin, in contrast, is deliberately minimized (10%) — a lesson of history: the estate-based First Empire fell partly because of the elite-people gap (→ see [Volume II, Ch.04, "The Era of Crisis"]).

6.2 Social Mobility

High mobility: 60% of guild members come from families of a different status.

Path Difficulty Time
Education → Guild Low 10–15 years
Merit → Recognition Medium 5–20 years
Guild change Medium 1–3 years
Entrepreneurship Low Any

The key mobility channel — education: free and accessible, it allows anyone to enter a guild by ability rather than origin.

6.3 Family and Kinship

In post-scarcity society the family is not an economic unit — it is based on emotional bonds. The three Xha'Ruun sexes form diverse family structures (→ see [Volume I, Ch.08, "Culture and Society"]). The state supports families through the guilds but does not control their structure.

6.4 Equality of Species

The social structure includes all three sapient species (→ see [Volume II, Ch.14, "The Three Species"]). Xha'ri, Kel'vash, and Moryn are equal before the law; social status within each species is determined by the same factors. Interspecies relations are governed by the Law of Accord.

6.5 Institutional Protection

Equality is protected institutionally:

  • The canon — prohibition of discrimination by origin and species;
  • Guild courts — protection of members' rights;
  • The Keeper of Knowledge — canon conformity control;
  • Educational accessibility — removal of mobility barriers.

Chapter Summary

  1. Society is formally casteless; status — by contribution, not origin.
  2. Status factors: guild (40%), merit (30%), education (20%), origin (10%).
  3. High mobility: 60% of guild members from families of another status.
  4. Education — the main channel of social mobility.
  5. Equality of the three species is protected by the canon and the Law of Accord.
