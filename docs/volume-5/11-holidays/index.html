Volume V: Culture — Chapter 11: Holidays and Calendar Rites

"A people without holidays is a people without memory. A people celebrating every day is a people without a future. We celebrate six times a year — and that is enough to remember who we are and not to forget why we live." — From the canon of the Council of Unity, +1,800 Y.E.

Introduction

The Xha'Ruun holiday calendar is an institution in which history, ethics, and cosmology meet in ritual. There are few holidays: six main ones and five days of Renewal, set apart from the months (→ see [Volume II, Ch.17, "History of the Calendar"]). Each holiday is not a break from work but a reproduction of the cultural core: memory of the past, affirmation of values, renewal of bonds. The principle of a small number of holidays is a consequence of the culture's long-term orientation: if you celebrate often, the holiday is devalued. Six holidays a year is a rhythm that does not satiate but also does not let one forget. Calendar rites are not religious in the strict sense: modern Xha'Ruun society is predominantly secular (→ see [Volume V, Ch.06, "Religion and Mythology"]), and the holidays reproduce not a cult but memory and ethics.

11.1 The Calendar Foundation

The Xha'Ruun year lasts 412.7 local days (~10,040 standard hours) and is divided into 14 months of ~29.5 days (→ see [Volume II, Ch.17, "History of the Calendar"]). At the end of the year — 5 days of Renewal (Ven-Khal), belonging to no month.

Element Value Note
Months 14 ~29.5 days each
Days of Renewal 5 Outside the months; every 5th year — 6 days
Main holidays 6 Planetary
Regional holidays ~20 By domain and guild

The days of Renewal are a unique structural feature: they end the year but belong to no month. This is "time outside time" — a universal truce during which the guilds and domains suspend activity.

11.2 The Six Main Holidays

11.2.1 New Year (Vek'shan) — 1 Khar-Ven

Element Meaning
Date 1 Khar-Ven (first day of the year)
Symbolism Beginning of the year, renewal of obligations
Clothing White cloak over the basic set (→ see [Volume V, Ch.10])
Cuisine Light dishes, thar-bread, drinks (→ see [Volume V, Ch.09])
Rite Exchange of obligations (guilds, families, domains)

The rite of obligations: on the first day of the year, each triad, guild, and domain repeats its obligations to others — not an oath but a public reminder of what binds them. The obligation is recorded orally, and failing to fulfill it during the year is a social stain.

11.2.2 Unity Day (Rass-Vek) — 15 Rass-Ur

Element Meaning
Date 15 Rass-Ur (month of Unity)
Symbolism Signing of the Treaty of Unity (−750 Y.E.)
Clothing Six colors on one outfit (→ see [Volume V, Ch.10])
Cuisine "Dish of consent" — six kinds on one dish
Duration 3 days
Rite Reading of the Treaty in the squares

The Treaty of Unity (−750 Y.E.) united eight regions after the Dark Ages (→ see [Volume II, Ch.05, "The Era of Revival"]). The holiday reproduces historical memory: the text of the Treaty (canonical, 108 lines) is read aloud in every district by a Keeper or a Council member. The holiday lasts three days: reading, celebration, silence (the third day is a day of solitary reflection).

11.2.3 Remembrance Day (Mor-Ghal) — 11 Thar-Ghal

Element Meaning
Date 11 Thar-Ghal (month of Remembrance)
Symbolism Memory of the Red Rot (−2,120 – −2,105)
Clothing Dark, without guild marks
Cuisine Lean "gray dish" (without a sulfur component)
Duration 1 day
Rite Silence until noon, then reading of names

The Red Rot (−2,120 – −2,105, → see [Volume II, Ch.04, "The Era of Crisis"]) — an epidemic that claimed up to 60% of the First Empire's population. Remembrance Day is not a mourning lament but a quiet reminder of the fragility of civilization. Silence until noon is observed everywhere; after noon — the reading of names. The "gray dish" is the only day of the year when a dish is prepared without a sulfur component: physiologically "unfinished," it symbolizes the irreplaceability of the loss.

11.2.4 Knowledge Day (Ven-Thex) — 1 Ven-Khal

Element Meaning
Date 1 Ven-Khal (month of Truth)
Symbolism Founding of the First Library (−4,800)
Clothing Azure elements in all guilds
Cuisine "Scholar's snack": fruits, flatbreads, thar-tea
Duration 1 day
Rite Handing in observations, Open College, Dispute of Six (→ see [Volume V, Ch.07])

Knowledge Day — the only holiday entirely devoted to intellectual activity (→ see [Volume V, Ch.07, "Science in Culture"]). In the morning — handing in personal observations to the archives; at midday — the Procession of Instruments; in the evening — the Dispute of Six. On this day children have the right to ask any question of any master, and the master is obliged to answer or to admit ignorance.

11.2.5 Birth Day (Nur-Vek) — 14 The-Nur

Element Meaning
Date 14 The-Nur (month of Birth)
Symbolism Birth of a new generation, continuation of the species
Clothing Festive, with "birth" elements (egg ornaments)
Cuisine Sweet dishes, "egg" forms
Duration 1 day
Rite Visiting schools, public presentation of newborns

Birth Day — the holiday of childhood and continuation. Triads whose larvae hatched in the past year present them publicly in the district square. This is not a show but a ritualized affirmation: "the new generation has arrived." The visiting of schools by elders is a custom by which any adult may come to a school on this day and tell children about their guild.

11.2.6 Harmony Day (Rass-Thal) — 30 Veks-An

Element Meaning
Date 30 Veks-An (last month of the year)
Symbolism Preparation for Renewal, equilibrium before the transition
Clothing Neutral colors, all six colors equally
Cuisine Six dishes in six servings
Duration 1 day
Rite Check of debts (not monetary — obligations to others)

The check of debts — a rite in which each person publicly reminds himself and others of unfulfilled obligations. Not a punitive procedure but self-reflection. Obligations not fulfilled before Renewal carry over into the new year.

11.3 The Days of Renewal (Nur-Vek) — 5 days outside the months

Day Name Content
1st Silence Silence, no work
2nd Purification Cleaning, repairs, preparation
3rd Feasting Communal meals, music
4th Exchange Gift-giving, transmission of knowledge and objects
5th Return Preparation for the year, reading of plans

The days of Renewal — the longest holiday, but also the most ascetic. The first day is complete silence: no work, no movement beyond what is necessary. The second is the physical renewal of space. The third is the only day of the year when communal meals "for the whole square" are permitted (→ see [Volume V, Ch.09, "Cuisine and Gastronomy"]). The fourth is the day of exchange: gift-giving, the handover of tools and knowledge. The fifth is the return to the plan: triads and guilds draw up plans for the year.

The universal truce of Renewal extends even to legal proceedings: courts do not sit, disputes are postponed. This is "time outside time" — the only pause in the annual cycle.

11.4 Regional Holidays

Alongside the six planetary holidays, the domains and guilds observe their own.

Domain/Guild Holiday Date Content
Kharak-Thar Capital Day 18 Khar-Ven Founding of Khar-Vexar (−7,800)
Rhuval-Ven Festival of Trade 20 Sha-En Opening of the first trade route
Vexar-Shan Philosophical Dispute Every 2 years Competition of schools (→ see [Volume V, Ch.05, "Philosophy"])
Island Festival of the Sea 15 Thalas-Mor First voyage to the island
Guild of Scholars Congress of Discoveries Every 3 years Presentation of the most important results
Guild of Warriors Day of Equilibrium 12 Thar-Ghal Ritual duels
Guild of Keepers Archive Day 6 Ven-Khal Opening of the archives to all

Regional holidays are not obligatory for the whole planet, but within a domain or guild they are observed as strictly as the planetary ones.

11.5 The Ritual Structure of a Holiday

All six holidays follow a single structure.

Phase Time Content
Preparation 3 days before Cleaning, food preparation, handing in affairs
Morning Start of the day Rite (reading, silence, exchange)
Noon Climax Public action (procession, dispute, meal)
Evening Completion Music, art, family gatherings
Night Silence Return to ordinary life

The structure is invariable: morning — rite, noon — climax, evening — completion. Each holiday reproduces the same form, filling it with different content.

11.6 Holiday and Music

Music is an integral part of holidays (→ see [Volume V, Ch.04, "Music"]).

Holiday Musical Form
New Year Choral hymns ("dawn" ones)
Unity Day Symphony orchestra (performance of the "Symphony of the Treaty")
Remembrance Day Silence until noon, then low infrasonic tones
Knowledge Day Sonification of scientific series (→ see [Volume V, Ch.07])
Birth Day Children's choirs
Harmony Day Chamber music

The "Symphony of the Treaty" — a canonical musical text written in +850 Y.E. to commemorate the founding of the Council of Unity. Performed once a year, on Unity Day, by a full orchestra of 60 instruments. Duration — 108 minutes (the number of lines of the Treaty). The work is structured in senary 6/8 meters (→ see [Volume IV, Ch.09, "The Senary Numeral System"]).

11.7 Holiday and Art

Holiday rituals provide a commission for art.

Art Holiday Application
Painting Depiction of historical scenes (Unity Day)
Sculpture Monuments in the squares
Theater Performance of the "Fall of the Empire" (Remembrance Day)
Poetry Recitation of the "Cycle of Times" (→ see [Volume I, Ch.08, "Culture and Society"])
Resonance installation Acoustic design of Knowledge Day

The Remembrance Day theater — a unique tradition: every year on Remembrance Day, one and the same play is staged in the squares — the "Fall of the Empire." A canonical text, unchanged since +1,200 Y.E., reproducing the last days of the First Empire. This is not a "historical drama" in the modern sense but a ritualized reminder.

11.8 Holiday and Childhood

Children participate in holidays on equal terms with adults.

Holiday Children's Role
New Year Recitation of the "children's oath" (promise to learn)
Unity Day Reading one line of the Treaty (in turn)
Remembrance Day Participation in the silence
Knowledge Day The right to ask any question of any master
Birth Day Welcoming the newborns
Harmony Day Check of obligations (children's)

The "children's oath" of the New Year — a formula recited by children aged 5–15: "I promise to learn, to ask, and not to be silent when I see injustice." The oath is not legally binding but culturally significant: reciting it is a sign of entering the community of the sentient.

11.9 The Holiday of Three Species

The three species participate in holidays on equal terms, but in different ways.

Species Feature of Participation
Xha'Ri Main organizer, oral speech
Kel'vash Participation in "stone meals," vibrational communication
Moryn Participation in "light meals," bioluminescence

The "stone meal" of Unity Day — a ritual in which the Xha'Ri and the Kel'vash sit side by side but do not eat; instead they exchange knowledge about food through rock vibrations (→ see [Volume V, Ch.09, "Cuisine and Gastronomy"]). Similarly, the "light meal" with the Moryn is an exchange of bioluminescent phrases.

11.10 Prohibitions and Violations

Prohibition Content Consequence of Violation
Work during Renewal Any labor activity except what is necessary Social disapproval
Breaking the silence on Remembrance Day Speech before noon Expulsion from the square
Refusing to answer children on Knowledge Day A master refuses to answer a child Loss of guild status for a year
Gift-giving during Renewal without reciprocation Gift without exchange Disrespect for the principle of exchange

The punishments are not legal but social: the violator is not condemned by a court but loses status in the community. In a post-scarcity society (→ see [Volume III, Ch.03, "Economic System"]) social disapproval is punishment enough.

11.11 Holiday and Memory

A holiday is not only a ritual but also a mechanism of memory transmission. Each generation reproduces the same rites, and in this reproduction historical memory is preserved.

Holiday Transmitted Memory
New Year Renewal of obligations
Unity Day Treaty of Unity, unification after the crisis
Remembrance Day Red Rot, fragility of civilization
Knowledge Day First Library, value of knowledge
Birth Day Continuation of the species
Harmony Day Equilibrium before the transition

Memory is not written down — it is experienced through the body: silence, quiet, exchange, reading. This is physical memory, not only mental.

Chapter Summary

  1. The holiday calendar — six main holidays and five days of Renewal; the small number of holidays is a consequence of long-term orientation.
  2. Each holiday reproduces the cultural core: New Year — renewal of obligations, Unity Day — the Treaty (−750), Remembrance Day — the epidemic (−2,120), Knowledge Day — the First Library (−4,800).
  3. The ritual structure is uniform: morning — rite, noon — climax, evening — completion; music, art, and cuisine are integral elements.
  4. The days of Renewal — "time outside time," a universal truce; five days of silence, purification, feasting, exchange, and return.
  5. A holiday is a mechanism of memory transmission through bodily experience (silence, quiet, exchange, reading); the three species participate on equal terms through "stone" and "light" meals.
