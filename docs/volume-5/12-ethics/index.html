Volume V: Culture — Chapter 12: Ethics and Morality

"Ethics is not a list of rules. Ethics is a way of seeing the other. If you see yourself in him — you cannot do him harm. If you see in him a source of knowledge — you cannot destroy him. If you see in him a part of harmony — you cannot break it." — From "The Three Paths of Ethics," the school of the "Three Paths," −400 Y.E.

Introduction

Xha'Ruun ethics is not a religious doctrine — it grew out of historical experience, is fixed in the canon, and is reproduced by everyday practice. The three pillars of ethics — knowledge (ven), harmony (rass), truth (ven-ash) — are not abstract ideals but practical principles built into the grammar of the language, the structure of the guilds, and the rules of the table. The ethical system of the Xha'Ruun is unique in that it is asymmetric: ignorance is not stigmatized, while concealment of knowledge is severely stigmatized; losing a dispute does not lower one's standing, but refusing a path review after a victory does. This asymmetry is not accidental: it was shaped by the memory of the Dark Ages (−2,100 – −1,800), when the loss of knowledge cost civilization centuries (→ see [Volume II, Ch.04, "The Era of Crisis"]). The ethics of the modern civilization is the ethics of a surviving species that once nearly vanished and decided it would not allow it again.

12.1 The Three Pillars of Ethics

The canon of the Council of Unity fixes three highest values (→ see [Volume I, Ch.08, "Culture and Society"]).

Value Concept Meaning Everyday Embodiment
Knowledge Ven Truth won by experience belongs to all Debt of transmission (ven-mor)
Harmony Rass Balance of the personal and the social, tradition and progress Law of Consent
Truth Ven-Ash Truthfulness as the foundation of trust Mandatory evidentiality

The three values are arranged in strict order: knowledge is first. This is no accident: a civilization that survived the loss of its libraries placed knowledge above all other values, including the life of the individual. "To kill a body is to take one life. To kill knowledge is to take thousands of lives that might have used it" (→ see [Volume I, Ch.08, "Culture and Society"]).

12.2 The Debt of Knowledge (ven-mor)

Ven-mor — the central category of Xha'Ruun ethics: the obligation to pass what one has learned to the archives and to descendants.

Aspect Content
Formulation "Knowledge, once acquired, must be transmitted"
Subject Anyone who has acquired new knowledge or experience
Addressee Guild of Keepers, district archive, students
Term Until death or until leaving the guild
Violation Concealment of knowledge — a grave offense

Practical embodiment:

  • The District Diary — a collective journal of observations (weather, migrations, phenology) kept in all 182 districts (→ see [Volume V, Ch.07, "Science in Culture"]);
  • The path review in sport — the winner is obliged to explain the technique to the losers (→ see [Volume V, Ch.08, "Sports and Games"]);
  • Culinary apprenticeship — recipes are transmitted orally from master to apprentice (→ see [Volume V, Ch.09, "Cuisine and Gastronomy"]);
  • Knowledge Day — handing in personal observations to the archives (→ see [Volume V, Ch.11, "Holidays and Calendar Rites"]).

Violation of the debt of knowledge is not a legal crime but a moral one. A scientist who carried a discovery to the grave stands lower in the ethical hierarchy than a thief. The cultural memory of the Dark Ages makes the concealment of knowledge one of the gravest offenses.

12.3 The Law of Consent (rass-tor)

Rass-tor — the principle by which no decision affecting several parties is made without consulting all those affected (→ see [Volume II, Ch.14, "History of the Three Species"]).

Level Application
Inter-species The three species (Xha'Ri, Kel'vash, Moryn) consult on common matters
Guild Council decisions require coordination with the guilds
Family A triad makes decisions only with the consent of all three members
Personal Creating a mind without its consent is unethical

The prohibition of self-aware artificial intelligence — a direct consequence of the Law of Consent: a mind brought into existence without the possibility of consenting finds itself in a position for which Xha'Ruun ethics has no justification (→ see [Volume VI, Ch.05, "Non-AI: Another Path"]).

12.4 Mandatory Evidentiality

The Xha'Ruun language requires the speaker to mark the source of information (→ see [Volume IV, Ch.06, "Evidentiality"]).

Marker Source Ethical Assessment
-ven Direct evidence (saw oneself) Highest reliability
-sha Indirect (heard, sensed) Reliable
-tor Inference from traces Conjecture
-rass Hearsay Requires citing the source

A false marker — using -ven ("saw oneself") for what one has not seen — is counted among grave offenses. Direct lying with the -ven marker entails up to 20 years of corrective labor (→ see [Volume I, Ch.08, "Culture and Society"]). The cultural logic: lying with direct evidence destroys the basic trust on which society rests.

Plagiarism in the Xha'Ruun ethical system is not the theft of intellectual property (in a post-scarcity society the concept of "ownership of an idea" is absent) but a variety of falsehood: claiming another's observation requires a false -ven marker instead of -rass, which moves it from the unethical to the false.

12.5 Asymmetries

Xha'Ruun ethics is full of asymmetries: some actions are severely stigmatized, while their opposites are not.

Action Assessment Opposite Action Assessment
Concealment of knowledge Grave Admitting ignorance Safe
Lying with -ven Grave Admitting inference (-tor) Normal
Refusal of a path review Grave Losing a dispute Does not lower standing
Destruction of an archive Gravest Error in an experiment Acceptable
Suppressing birth without medical indications Grave Voluntary refusal to reproduce A right

The key asymmetry: ignorance is not stigmatized; concealment is stigmatized. This is the opposite of cultural models in which admitting ignorance lowers one's standing. The formula "Mor-ven" ("I do not know") is socially safe; it does not lower authority but demonstrates honesty.

12.6 Taboos

Taboos are actions that are not legally prohibited but arouse revulsion at the level of culture.

Taboo Justification Historical Origin
Deliberate destruction of knowledge Knowledge belongs to descendants Loss of the libraries of the Dark Ages
Conscious lying with -ven Destruction of basic trust Imperial bureaucracy
Suppression of birth without medical indications The continuation of the species is a moral duty Demographic crisis of the Dark Ages
Killing a fellow Fundamental taboo Universal ethics
Harming a child/larva Protection of the future Low population growth
Breaking guild oaths Destruction of institutional fabric Collapse of the First Empire

Destruction of knowledge — the only taboo perceived as worse than murder: "To kill a body is to take one life. To kill knowledge is to take thousands."

12.7 Ethics and Law

Xha'Ruun ethics is not law, but it deeply influences law.

Sphere Legal Ethical
Concealment of knowledge Not a crime Grave offense, loss of guild status
Lying with -ven Up to 20 years of corrective labor Grave offense
Plagiarism Not a crime Loss of status in the guild
Murder Life imprisonment / execution Gravest
Violation of the Law of Consent Depends on the sphere Unethical

Corrective labor — not imprisonment but labor service with the preservation of basic rights. The principle: punishment should restore, not destroy.

12.8 Ethics and Childhood

Ethical education begins in early childhood.

Age Skill Method
3–5 Distinguishing the -ven / -rass markers The game "Question-Answer" (→ see [Volume V, Ch.08])
5–8 The formula "I do not know" Encouraging the admission of ignorance
8–12 The debt of transmission Handing in observations to the District Diary
12–15 The Law of Consent The game "Consent" (→ see [Volume V, Ch.08])

The "children's oath" of the New Year — a formula recited by children aged 5–15: "I promise to learn, to ask, and not to be silent when I see injustice" (→ see [Volume V, Ch.11, "Holidays and Calendar Rites"]). The oath is not legally binding but culturally significant.

12.9 The Ethics of Three Species

The three species do not share a common ethics in every detail, but the Law of Consent fixes a common core.

Principle Xha'Ri Kel'vash Moryn
Debt of knowledge (ven-mor) Mandatory Adapted (long experiment) Adapted (light memory)
Law of Consent Basic Basic Basic
Evidentiality Mandatory Through vibrations Through light phrases
Ban on self-aware AI Yes Yes Yes

The "long experiment" of the Kel'vash — an adaptation of the debt of knowledge to their metabolism and time perception: Kel'vash experiments are designed to outlast the life of an Xha'Ri, and so the transmission of knowledge requires a generational relay (→ see [Volume V, Ch.07, "Science in Culture"]).

The "light memory" of the Moryn — a form of the debt of knowledge realized through bioluminescent records fixed on reefs and in deep archives.

12.10 Ethics and Economics

A post-scarcity society (→ see [Volume III, Ch.03, "Economic System"]) makes ethical norms stricter: when hunger and need are absent, moral choice becomes the only choice.

Sphere Ethical Norm
Labor Not for survival but for contribution
Distribution "By need" at the base level, "by contribution" above it
Knowledge Open, not sold
Land and resources Belong to civilization, not to the individual
Archives Accessible to all

Energy-backed currency (1 VT = 1 kWh, → see [Volume I, Ch.08]) — an ethical decision: pegging to an inexhaustible resource (thermonuclear fusion) makes speculation impossible.

12.11 Ethics and Technology

Technological development is bounded by ethical frameworks (→ see [Volume II, Ch.10, "History of Science and Technology"]).

Restriction Justification
Ban on self-aware AI Creating a mind without its consent
Caution in biology Memory of the Red Rot (−2,120 – −2,105)
Law of Silence The intrinsic value of nature (→ see [Volume III, Ch.09])
Ban on cloning the sentient Uniqueness of the person

The Law of Silence — an ethical prohibition of technological intervention in protected zones: one may observe, but not intervene. The principle: nature has intrinsic value, not merely value for civilization.

12.12 Ethics and Inter-Species Relations

The recognition of the sentience of the three species gave rise to unique ethical problems.

Problem Solution
Physiological incompatibility The principle of proportionality (→ see [Volume V, Ch.08, "Sports and Games"])
Different time perception The "long experiment" and patience
Different ethical systems The Law of Consent as the minimal common
Dietary incompatibility "A meal without food" (→ see [Volume V, Ch.09, "Cuisine and Gastronomy"])

The principle of proportionality in sport is an example of an ethical solution to a physiological problem: results are normalized on species scales so that competition does not become a demonstration of anatomy.

12.13 Ethical Dilemmas and Disputes

Modern Xha'Ruun ethics is not a closed system — it includes unresolved disputes.

Dilemma Positions
Cloning the sentient "For" (prolongation of life) vs "Against" (uniqueness of the person)
Interstellar expansion "For" (spreading life) vs "Against" (risk of losing unity)
UV-invisible patterns in art "For" (aesthetic freedom) vs "Against" (visual exclusivity)
Accelerated learning "For" (efficiency) vs "Against" (the debt of personal experience)

Disputes are not resolved by voting but maintained as part of living culture: an ethics without disputes is dead.

12.14 Ethics and Memory

Xha'Ruun ethics is not a set of principles but a living memory of what happens when they are violated.

Principle Historical Memory
Debt of knowledge Loss of the libraries of the Dark Ages (−2,100 – −1,800)
Law of Consent Conflicts with the Kel'vash and Moryn (−2,400 – −800)
Evidentiality Imperial bureaucracy and its lies
Ban on self-aware AI The philosophical dispute of +2,000 – +2,400 Y.E.
Law of Silence Ecological collapse of the First Empire (−2,150)

Each principle is not an abstraction but an answer to a concrete catastrophe. Xha'Ruun ethics is the ethics of a surviving species.

Chapter Summary

  1. The three pillars of ethics — knowledge (ven), harmony (rass), truth (ven-ash); knowledge is first, for the loss of knowledge cost civilization centuries.
  2. The debt of knowledge (ven-mor) — the obligation to transmit what one has learned; concealment of knowledge is a grave offense, worse than theft; "Mor-ven" ("I do not know") is socially safe.
  3. The Law of Consent (rass-tor) — the principle of consulting all those affected; the ban on self-aware AI is a consequence (a mind without its consent is unethical).
  4. Mandatory evidentiality — a false -ven marker entails up to 20 years of corrective labor; plagiarism is a variety of falsehood, not theft.
  5. Ethics is not law but living memory of catastrophes: each principle is an answer to a concrete historical event (the Dark Ages, the epidemic, ecological collapse).
