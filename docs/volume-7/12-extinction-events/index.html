Volume VII: Nature — Chapter 12: Extinction Events

"Five times everything died. Five times everything returned. The sixth extinction — the last — we stopped ourselves. The memory of the five holds us." — Law of the Keepers of Biodiversity, +2,100 Y.E.

Related sections: → see [Volume I, Ch.12, "Geological Evolution"], → see [Volume I, Ch.20, "The Origin of Life"], → see [Volume I, Ch.11, "Ecology"], → see [Volume VII, Ch.11, "Evolution Patterns"], → see [Volume VII, Ch.13, "Biosphere Interactions"], → see [Volume II, Ch.04, "The Era of Crisis"] Length: 18 pages

Introduction

A mass extinction is an event in which, over a geologically short time (less than 2 million cycles), more than 50% of the species of the biosphere disappear. In the 4.2 billion cycles of the history of life on Théxar five such events have been recorded, each of which radically restructured the ecosystems and redefined the direction of evolution (→ see [Volume I, Ch.11, "Ecology"]).

The sixth extinction — anthropogenic, caused by the activity of the Xha'Ruun in the Crisis Era (−2,100 – −750) — was stopped by the efforts of the entire civilization (→ see [Volume II, Ch.04]). Since +2,100 Y.E. not a single species extinction has been recorded on the planet — a record already maintained for 1,928 cycles.

This chapter systematizes the five great extinctions of Théxar, their causes, consequences, and lessons for the management of the biosphere.

12.1 Criteria of a Mass Extinction

Criterion Threshold Note
Species lost >50% Of the total number that existed
Duration of the crisis <2 million cycles Period of elevated mortality
Global scale All continents Excludes local catastrophes
Trace in the geological record Layer boundary Sharp change of the paleontological complex
Recovery of diversity >10 million cycles Time to return to the pre-event level

12.2 The First Extinction: The Oxygen Catastrophe

Date: 4.0–3.8 billion cycles ago Duration: ~200 million cycles (gradual) Cause: Accumulation of free oxygen in the atmosphere Lost: ~40% of anaerobic species

12.2.1 Prehistory

Before the Oxygen Catastrophe the atmosphere of Théxar contained trace amounts of O₂ (<0.02%). Life was based on H₂S chemosynthesis and anoxygenic photosynthesis. About 4.1 billion cycles ago the first oxygenic phototrophs appeared — organisms capable of splitting water with the release of oxygen (→ see [Volume I, Ch.20, "The Origin of Life"]).

12.2.2 The Mechanism of the Catastrophe

Oxygen is a powerful oxidizer. For anaerobic life forms lacking protective mechanisms, its appearance meant a chemical attack on cell membranes. The concentration of O₂ grew slowly (stretched over 200 million cycles), but inexorably:

Time (billion Y.A.) O₂ in the atmosphere Consequence
4.1 0.02% First oxygenic phototrophs
4.0 0.5% Beginning of the extinction of methanogens
3.9 3.8% Oxidation of Fe²⁺ in the oceans, red oozes
3.8 12% Formation of the ozone layer, end of anaerobic dominance

12.2.3 Consequences

  • Mass extinction of anaerobic forms (~40%);
  • Survival in refugia (hydrothermal vents, ocean depths, anaerobic sediments);
  • Liberation of ecological niches for aerobic forms;
  • Formation of the ozone layer, which protected life from UV radiation;
  • Transition to aerobic respiration — a metabolism 18× more efficient.

KEY FACT

The Oxygen Catastrophe on Théxar occurred 1.6 billion cycles earlier than on Earth (4.0 against 2.4 billion years ago) because of the earlier appearance of efficient photosynthesis. But it was more gradual (~200 million cycles), which allowed part of the anaerobes to adapt rather than die out entirely.

12.3 The Second Extinction: The Volcanic Catastrophe

Date: 1.8 billion cycles ago Duration: ~1.2 million cycles Cause: Super-eruption, global winter Lost: ~55% of species

12.3.1 Geological Context

In the period 1.85–1.75 billion cycles ago Théxar experienced a peak of plume volcanism — the formation of massive traps on the Vaal-Kharak continent (the predecessor of Kharak). The total volume of erupted basaltic magma is estimated at 4.2 million mo³ — 3× more than the largest known trap event.

12.3.2 The Mechanism of the Catastrophe

The eruption released into the atmosphere:

  • SO₂ (sulfur dioxide): 6.1 × 10¹⁴ ru
  • H₂S (hydrogen sulfide): 2.8 × 10¹⁴ ru
  • CO₂: 1.4 × 10¹⁵ ru
  • Volcanic dust: 3.2 × 10¹⁵ ru

The sulfate aerosol formed from SO₂ blocked 68% of sunlight for up to 800 cycles. The surface temperature fell by 18°X. Photosynthesis shrank by 84%.

12.3.3 Consequences

  • Collapse of photosynthetic production → extinction of 62% of primary producers;
  • Cascading extinction of consumers (up to the fifth trophic level);
  • Oxidation of the oceans (pH fell to 7.2);
  • Mass death of reef systems;
  • Survivors: deep-water chemosynthetic communities, anaerobic refugia.

Recovery: 42 million cycles to return biodiversity to the pre-event level.

12.4 The Third Extinction: Total Glaciation

Date: 900 million cycles ago Duration: ~0.8 million cycles (glacial phase) Cause: Global glaciation ("Snowball Théxar") Lost: ~65% of species

12.4.1 The Trigger

A combination of three factors:

  1. Decline of volcanic activity → fall of atmospheric CO₂ to 180 ppm;
  2. Continents near the equator (high albedo);
  3. Positive feedback: ice → albedo → cold → more ice.

12.4.2 The Glacial Maximum

At the glaciation maximum (890 million Y.A.) the entire surface of Théxar was covered with ice up to 1,200 sha thick. Average temperature: −41°X. The oceans did not freeze completely thanks to the geothermal flux through the crust.

Parameter Before the glaciation Maximum After
Average surface T +13.6°X −41°X +10.2°X
Ice cover 18% 100% 24%
Photosynthesis (% of normal) 100 0.4 82
Planet biomass 1,340 Gt C 31 Gt C 940 Gt C

12.4.3 Exit from the Glaciation

Continuing volcanism accumulated CO₂ under the ice shell. Over 18 million cycles the concentration reached 6,800 ppm — critical for the breakthrough of the greenhouse effect. A rapid (~80,000 cycles) exit from the glacial phase began.

12.4.4 Consequences

  • Death of 65% of species, chiefly terrestrial;
  • Survivors: deep-water communities (chemosynthetic), microorganisms under the ice;
  • Radiation of multicellular forms after the thaw;
  • The first large predators appeared in the post-glacial fauna.

12.5 The Fourth Extinction: Impact

Date: 250 million cycles ago Duration: <0.5 million cycles (acute phase) Cause: Fall of an asteroid ~3 mo in diameter Lost: ~70% of species

12.5.1 The Event

An asteroid 3.05 mo in diameter fell into the Gharra Ocean (→ see [Volume I, Ch.12, "Geological Evolution"]). Impact energy — 8.4 × 10²³ J (the equivalent of 200 million Mt of TNT).

Consequence Scale
Crater 65 mo diameter (today the Thash Trench)
Tsunami Up to 2,440 sha high
Release into the atmosphere 1.2 × 10¹⁶ ru of rock, dust, water vapor
Fires 38% of the planet's forests burned
"Impact winter" 15 cycles
Temperature (fall) −22°X in the first 2 cycles

12.5.2 The Mechanism of Extinction

Three-stage:

  1. Mechanical impact — tsunami, shock wave, seismic tremors (0–10 days);
  2. Impact winter — dusting of the atmosphere, blocking of light (0–15 cycles);
  3. Greenhouse phase — release of CO₂ from carbonates, rise of T by 8°X (15–2,000 cycles).

12.5.3 Consequences

  • The largest extinction of fauna in the planet's history (70%);
  • Total disappearance of 8 orders of the Thermochromadae;
  • Liberation of ecological niches;
  • Radiation of the ancestors of the Xha'Ruun (240–180 million Y.A.) — a direct consequence of the liberated niches;
  • Formation of the modern appearance of the biosphere.

KEY FACT

The impact extinction is the only one of the five not connected with the internal dynamics of the planet. It is a random event whose consequences set the path of evolution 250 million cycles ahead — including the appearance of reason.

12.6 The Fifth Extinction: Anthropogenic (Incomplete)

Date: −2,100 – −750 Y.E. (the Crisis Era) Duration: 1,350 cycles (peak: −1,800 – −1,200) Cause: The activity of the Xha'Ruun civilization Lost: ~12% of species (stopped)

12.6.1 Causes

Unlike the four previous ones, this extinction was caused by sentient activity (→ see [Volume II, Ch.04, "The Era of Crisis"]).

Factor Mechanism Scale
Deforestation Agriculture, fuel 34% of forest cover destroyed
Overfishing Unrestricted fishing Marine fauna biomass −62%
Pollution Industrial runoff 18% of rivers dead
Urbanization Growth of cities 2.4% of land built over
Hunting Uncontrolled hunting 8 species of large herbivores exterminated

12.6.2 The Turning Point

In −1,200 Y.E. the Guild of Natural History presented the Council a report: "At current rates, in 600 cycles the biosphere will reach the threshold of irreversible collapse." This launched the greatest reform in the history of the civilization:

  • −1,150: The Law on Protected Territories (12% of land);
  • −980: Ban on uncontrolled logging;
  • −840: Introduction of fishing quotas;
  • −750: The Treaty of Unity — the union of eight regions around the principle of nature conservation (→ see [Volume II, Ch.05]).

12.6.3 The Result

The extinction was stopped. Since +2,100 Y.E. not a single species disappearance has been recorded. Eight species have been reintroduced (including the Ghal-thass, restored in +2,850 Y.E.). Forest cover has been restored to 40% (+12% over 500 cycles).

KEY FACT

The fifth extinction is the only stopped one. It did not enter the geological record as a layer boundary, but it did enter the cultural memory of the Xha'Ruun as the Lesson of the Crisis: the biosphere tolerates abuse, but not infinitely.

12.7 Patterns of the Extinctions

12.7.1 Comparative Table

Extinction Cause Lost Recovery Surviving groups
I (Oxygen) Biogenic 40% 120 million Y.A. Aerobes, refugia
II (Volcanic) Geological 55% 42 million Y.A. Chemosynthetics
III (Glacial) Climatic 65% 38 million Y.A. Deep-water
IV (Impact) Cosmic 70% 24 million Y.A. Small generalists
V (Anthropogenic) Biogenic 12% (stopped) — All except large specialists

12.7.2 Common Patterns

  1. Selection against specialists: in all five events the highly specialized forms died out first; generalists survived.
  2. Refugia: the deep-water chemosynthetic communities survived all five events with minimal losses.
  3. Body size: large forms (>100 kh) went extinct 3.4× more often than small ones (<1 kh).
  4. Recovery time: grows with the severity of the event; on average 10–40 million cycles.
  5. Radiation after extinction: each event opened the path for a new adaptive radiation — the Thermochromadae after IV, primate-analogues after III.

12.7.3 The "Rule of Five"

Five extinctions in 4.2 billion cycles give an average frequency: one mass extinction per 840 million cycles. This is close to the frequency of the "Wilson supercycles" — global tectonic rearrangements (→ see [Volume I, Ch.12, "Geological Evolution"]). Hypothesis: mass extinctions are connected with the long-period geological cycles of the planet.

12.8 Lessons for the Management of the Biosphere

The experience of five extinctions and the success of stopping the sixth shaped the modern ecological ethics of the Xha'Ruun:

Principle Justification
The "six steps" principle Assessing the consequences 6 generations ahead
The 12% rule No less than 12% of land — reserves
The sustainable catch law Taking no more than 68% of the annual increment
The ban on destroying species Even "useless" ones — they may prove key
Reintroduction The duty to restore species extinct through the civilization's fault

Chapter Summary

  1. In the 4.2 billion cycles of the life of Théxar there were five mass extinctions: the oxygen one (40%, 4.0 billion Y.A.), the volcanic one (55%, 1.8 billion Y.A.), the glacial one (65%, 900 million Y.A.), the impact one (70%, 250 million Y.A.), the anthropogenic one (12%, −2,100 – −750, stopped).
  2. The largest extinction was the impact one (70% of species); the only one not connected with the internal dynamics of the planet; it opened the path for the radiation of the ancestors of the Xha'Ruun.
  3. Common patterns: selection against specialists, survival of refugia (the deep-water chemosynthetics survived all five), large forms go extinct 3.4× more often than small ones.
  4. The fifth extinction is the only stopped one; it did not enter the geological record, but it did enter cultural memory as the "Lesson of the Crisis."
  5. Since +2,100 Y.E. not a single species extinction has been recorded on Théxar — a record lasting 1,928 cycles.
