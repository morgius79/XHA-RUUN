**Mor-ven:** Sport is shaped by six-limbed anatomy — three pairs of limbs, three eyes (~210°), four ears, pheromone communication, infrasonic hearing — and by a historical choice: to test **mastery, not superiority**. ## 8.1 Early Forms: From Hunting to Ritual

| Era | Form | Function |
|-----|------|----------|
| Pre-Civilization | Track running, stone throwing, wrestling | Training for prey and defense |
| Early Civilization | Ritual races at temples | Calendar symbolism |
| First Empire | Military games, kharru racing | Selection, demonstration of strength |
| Dark Ages | Regional folk games | Maintaining communal bonds |
| Era of Unity | Institute of Games, guild tournaments | Testing mastery, unity |

**Kharru racing** is the oldest sport to survive. The kharru — a large herbivore, domesticated around **−10 200**, the first domesticated species — makes racing the only discipline in which a contestant competes in a pair with an animal.

## 8.2 The Institute of Games

The **Great Games** (Thar-Vex-Sha, "strength-will-word") — a planetary competition once every six years in Khar-Vexar, established in **+1 600** as a replacement for the imperial military reviews.

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| Frequency | Once every 6 years |
| Venue | Arena "Six Circles," Khar-Vexar |
| Participants | ~6 000 (guilds, domains, three species) |
| Disciplines | 36 (6 × 6) |
| Duration | 12 days (2 × 6) |

**The prize** — a **line in the General Registry of Achievements** (Guild of Keepers), recorded forever, supplementable by descendants. Victory is recorded, not awarded — a consequence of the value of knowledge.

| Teams | 6 players each |

**The scoring reform (+3 020)** — senary scoring persisted in sport longer than in science and trade, replaced by decimal only after three decades of debate, for spectator ergonomics. Sports scoring remained the only domestic domain where the senary system gave up ground.

## 8.4 Team Games

### 8.4.1 Khar-Tor (Six-Handed Ball)

The most popular team sport: a hexagonal field with a side of 40 sha (~33 m); two teams of 6; ball of tar-vine fibers, one dlan in diameter.

| Element | Rule |
|---------|------|
| Goal | Carry the ball into the opponent's "nest" |
| Limbs | All six except the lower limbs when standing |
| Prohibition | Contact only with the forearms of the middle hands |
| Time | 6 periods of 6 migs (≈ 6 × 3.6 s) |
| Resolution | Draws not recorded: the sixth period is "open" |

The **"Open Period"** — on a draw, no extra time: a sixth, unplanned period starts with a new score. Completion must be achieved through play, not the clock.

### 8.4.2 Thalas-Tor (Aquatic Hexathlon)

A relay of six stages alternating swimming, diving, and passing objects during breathing pauses. Three-minute breath holds (a diving adaptation) make possible disciplines inaccessible to other humanoids.

### 8.4.3 The Game "Consent"

**Rass-Tor** — two teams of 6 negotiate the distribution of a limited resource over six sectors; the goal is a configuration the referee recognizes as "consenting" (rass). It reproduces the structure of the Council of Unity and is used in education as a practice of negotiation ethics.

## 8.5 Individual Disciplines

- **Trinocular throwing series** — precision beyond one-eyed observers:
  - **Six Rings** — a disc at six concentric targets, points in powers of 6
  - **Werksan Flight** — throwing an asymmetric glider
  - **Shadow Shot** — shooting at a UV target invisible to part of the audience, verified instrumentally
- **Infrasonic sports** (8 Hz – 42 kHz):
  - **Mor-Thash** ("roll call of the depths") — blindfolded players locate objects by infrasonic echo
  - **Resonance Six** — holding six sound frequencies in equilibrium with the vocal cords
- **The Jump of Three** — three successive pushes (lower right, lower left, both), corresponding to the three eras of the civilization's memory
- **The Solo Hexathlon** — six events: running 6 leagues, the Jump of Three, wing throw, swimming 6 leagues, climbing 60 sha, "quiet sitting"; formerly the military standard of the First Empire

**"Quiet sitting"** — the winner is not the one who lasted longest, but the one with the most even parameters of breathing and thermal radiation. From the meditative practice of the "Resonators": **sport can test inner equilibrium as well as outer strength**.

## 8.6 Mind Games

Intellectual games hold a status comparable to athletics — a consequence of the value of knowledge.

| Game | Players | Content |
|------|---------|---------|
| **Mor-Tor** ("path of strength") | 2 | Strategy on a hexagonal board; 6 kinds of pieces |
| **Ven-Thek** ("truth of writing") | 2–6 | Statements with evidentiality markers |
| **Gharra-Mor** ("depth of time") | 2 | 3-dimensional board; moves through "layers" |
| **Six Questions** | 2–6 | Guessing an object with minimum "observer's questions" |

**Ven-Thek** — the only known strategic game built on a grammatical category: a move must be marked `-ven`, `-sha`, `-tor`, or `-rass`; an erroneous marker is treated as a lie with penalty points — evidential ethics transferred into the game space (→ rhu [Thexar-IV, Sha-El 06, "Sha-Ven Rass"]).

## 8.7 The Three Species in Sport

| Species | Disciplines | Specifics |
|---------|-------------|-----------|
| **Xha'ri** | Hexathlon, khar-tor, mind games | Main participant in the Games |
| **Kel'vash** | Climbing-"silence," long balance, load-carrying | Endurance with minimal thermal trace |
| **Moryn** | Light-writing-"depth," current-races | Bioluminescent communication instead of a sound start |

**The principle of proportionality** — results normalized on the physiological scales of each species; otherwise the competition would demonstrate anatomy.

**Light-writing-"depth"** — Moryn compose bioluminescent "phrases" of maximum complexity at a depth of 1 200–3 000 sha; judged from bathyscaphes by panels that know the "language of light" (→ rhu [Thexar-I, Ruun 39, "Moryn"]).

## 8.10 Sports Ethics: "Fair Play Without Winners"

The formula worked out after the Games of +1 600: **"testing mastery, not superiority."**

| Norm | Content |
|------|---------|
| Recording, not awarding | Victory entered in the Registry of Achievements |
| Draws are acceptable | Recorded if completion is not achieved through play |
| Doping ban | Disruption of the metabolic baseline — "destruction of the body-instrument" |
| Insult ban | Demonstrating superiority (the "winner's dance") — a violation |
| Mandatory transmission | The winner conducts a "path review" for the losers |

The **"path review"** — the winner explains technique, decisions, and mistakes; refusal is treated as concealment of knowledge (ven-mor) and entails exclusion from the Registry (→ rhu [Thexar-V, Sha-El 12, "Ven-Rass-El"]). Sport reproduces the key cultural asymmetry: **concealment of knowledge is worse than defeat**. Doping is perceived as interference destroying the "body-instrument."
