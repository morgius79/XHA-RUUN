---

## 10.2 Mutual Intelligibility

All dialects mutually intelligible. Differences at the level of pronunciation and up to **5% of the lexicon**.

Ensured by:

- **A unified writing system** — Sharuu does not reflect differences in pronunciation
- **Centralized education** — the standard is taught everywhere
- **Mass media** — standard speech on the air

---

## 10.3 Phonetic Features

Dialectal differences affect:

- **Kharak-Tor** — velarization and the glottal stop ʔ
- **Rhuval-Ven** — vowel elongation, "melodiousness"
- **Vexar-Shan** — devoicing of h, nasalization
- **Island** — archaic tones

Trace back to the history of the settlement of the continents (→ rhu [Thexar-II, Sha-El 01, "Xha'ri Thar-Vokh"]).

---

## 10.4 Lexical Differences

Lexical differences (up to 5%) concern:

- **Natural terms** — local flora and fauna
- **Economic terms** — the specialization of the domains
- **Borrowings** — from the languages of the Kel'vash and the Moryn

---

## 10.5 Dialects and Identity

A dialect is a marker of domain identity (→ rhu [Thexar-III, Sha-El 05, "Sha-El Xha-Thar"]). Use of a dialect in everyday speech is combined with the standard in official contexts — "unity without unification" in language.
