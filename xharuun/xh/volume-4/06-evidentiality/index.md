# Thexar-IV: The-Sha-Ruun — Sha-El 06: Sha-Ven Rass — Evidentiality

> *"In the Xha'Ruun language, you cannot simply say 'it is raining.' You must say: 'I see that it is raining,' or 'I was told that it is raining,' or 'it appears to be raining.' Truth is not a fact. Truth is a source."*
> — Academic of Semantics, Khal-Ven

---

**Ruun-ak rass:** → rhu [Thexar-IV, Sha-El 05, "Khal-ven Thar"], → rhu [Thexar-IV, Sha-El 03, "Khal-sha Rass"]
**Volume:** 20 sha-ruun
**Mor-ven:** The most striking grammatical phenomenon of the language. Every utterance obliges the speaker to indicate the source of information: direct observation, indirect perception, inference, or retelling. The language does not allow the origin of knowledge to be concealed — a reflection of a culture in which truth is inseparable from its source.

---

## 6.1 Four Types of Evidentiality

| Type | Suffix | Meaning | Example |
|------|--------|---------|---------|
| Direct | -ven | I see/hear it myself | **khal-en-ven** — "does (I see)" |
| Indirect | -sha | I feel/hear | **khal-en-sha** — "does (I hear)" |
| Inferential | -tor | I draw a conclusion | **khal-en-tor** — "must be doing" |
| Reportative | -rass | I was told | **khal-en-rass** — "they say (he) does" |

Direct (-ven) vs indirect (-sha) = sensory modality: visual vs auditory/tactile. Inferential (-tor) = inference from evidence; reportative (-rass) = retelling another's speech.

---

## 6.2 Obligatoriness

Mandatory in every utterance. Omitting evidentiality is a grammatical error.

Marks not only verbs, but also nominal predicates, subordinate clauses, and even individual particles. In connected texts, the reportative is regularly used for citation, making the boundary between direct and reported speech explicit.

---

## 6.3 Evidentiality and Grammar

Interacts with other categories:

- **With tense** — the inferential in the past tense marks a retrospective inference
- **With negation** — negation may fall within the scope of evidentiality ("I see that it is not...")
- **With interrogativity** — in questions, evidentiality indicates the expected source of the answer

---

## 6.4 Cultural Significance

Not only grammar, but also a cultural norm:

- **Science** — requires an explicit indication of the source of data
- **Law** — distinguishes eyewitness testimony from retelling
- **Religion** — sacred texts marked by direct evidentiality ("I see") or reportative ("transmitted")

Language reflects and shapes culture: the obligatoriness of the source of knowledge is a principle built into speech itself.
