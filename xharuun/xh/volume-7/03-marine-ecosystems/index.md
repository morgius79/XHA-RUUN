# Thexar-VII: The-Xa — Sha-El 03: Thar-Mor Xa-Sha — Marine Ecosystems

> *"Land is an island. Everything else is the ocean. We call ourselves children of the earth, but our blood is salty, and the first cell from which we descend was born in darkness beneath three thousand measures of water."*
> — Guild of Navigators, "Book of the Depths," +3,640 Y.E.

**Volume:** 20 sha-ruun
**Mor-ven:** The oceans of Théxar (67% of surface) span the northern **Thalas** and southern **Gharra**, holding ~980,000 described species and most living matter. The marine biosphere preserves ancient H₂S chemosynthesis, builds reefs from a silicon-calcium composite, and is the homeland of the third sentient species, the **Moryn**. This digest traces marine communities from the euphotic surface to the hadal trenches.

## 3.2 Vertical Zonation

The orange spectrum of Khar'Vex (peak ~570 nm) is absorbed faster than blue-green, so the euphotic zone is **shallower** than expected.

| Zone | Depth (sha) | Illumination | Share of ocean volume | Production (% of marine) | Key forms |
|------|-------------|--------------|-----------------------|--------------------------|-----------|
| Euphotic | 0–146 | Sufficient for photosynthesis | 3.4% | 86% | Phytoplankton, Thalophyta, reefs |
| Dysphotic | 146–732 | Twilight | 13.1% | 6% | Phycobilin algae, vertical migrants |
| Aphotic | 732–4,878 | Total darkness | 61.8% | 3% | Bioluminescent fauna, Moryn (upper tier) |
| Abyssal | 4,878–8,537 | Total darkness | 20.4% | 4% | Chemosynthetic communities, benthos |
| Hadal | 8,537+ | Total darkness | 1.3% | 1% | Trench extremophiles |

> **KEY FACT** — The euphotic zone extends only to 146 sha (≈120 m), a third shallower than terrestrial. Compensated by chromophyll quantum efficiency (0.92) and deep-water **Thal phycobilin** (absorption peak 620 nm, working to 240 sha).

### 3.2.1 The Dysphotic Zone and "Twilight Farms"
The 146–732 sha layer hosts **diel vertical migration** — the largest regular mass movement of living matter: up to 4.1 Gt of biomass rises during dark hours of the 27.3 h day and descends 300–600 sha at dawn (ascent ~4.5 h, surface stay up to 11 h, descent ~3.8 h), making an efficient organic-carbon pump.

## 3.3 Producers of the Ocean

### 3.3.1 Phytoplankton
Microscopic single-celled producers provide 28% of the planet's total primary production.

| Group | Size | Pigments | Share of marine production | Range |
|-------|------|----------|----------------------------|-------|
| **Chromoplankta** (chromoplankton) | 4–90 μm | Chromophyll a+b | 51% | Ubiquitous, surface layer |
| **Silodiatoma** (silica-flagellates) | 12–210 μm | Chromophyll a, theta-carotene | 24% | Upwellings, cold waters |
| **Thalobionta** (phycobilins) | 2–18 μm | Thal phycobilin (620 nm) | 14% | Dysphotic zone |
| **Pikochroma** (picoplankton) | 0.4–2 μm | Chromophyll a | 8% | Oligotrophic gyre centers |
| **Sulfoplankta** | 1–6 μm | — (chemosynthesis) | 3% | H₂S upwelling zones |

**Silodiatoma** shells are twice as thick as terrestrial diatoms (dissolved Si 0.14 vs 0.07 mmol/L); their settling forms siliceous oozes covering 31% of the abyssal floor.

### 3.3.2 Macroalgae — Division Thalophyta
Division **Thalophyta** numbers ~32,000 described species, peaking in cold Gharra shelf waters and the Ven-Khal Strait.

| Species | Thallus length | Depth | Region | Feature |
|---------|----------------|-------|--------|---------|
| **Gharra-Kelp** | 24–61 sha | 6–90 sha | Gharra shelves | Commercial; yield 18.2–30.3 ru/ha |
| **Thal-Ribbon** (Thalas ribbon) | up to 104 sha | 12–140 sha | Thalas, Serrin Sea | The planet's longest organism |
| **Deep-Fan** (fan of the depths) | 1.2–3.7 sha | 140–240 sha | Gharra slopes | Pure Thal phycobilin, works at 0.8% of surface illumination |
| **Sulfo-Weed** (sulfur-weed) | 0.4–1.8 sha | 40–200 sha | Seeps, volcanic banks | Mixed photo-chemotrophic metabolism |

**Thal-Ribbon** is the largest organism by linear size: thalli reach 104 sha (≈85 m); clone fields in the Serrin Sea occupy up to 340 mo²; growth up to 0.6 sha per local day.

## 3.4 Pelagic Communities

### 3.4.1 Trophic Structure of the Pelagial

| Level | Group | Biomass (Gt C) | Transfer efficiency |
|-------|-------|----------------|---------------------|
| I — producers | Phytoplankton, Thalophyta | 14.0 | — |
| II — filter feeders | Zooplankton, krill analogues | 3.1 | 14% |

Energy transfer efficiency (~12%) is slightly higher than Earth's, from higher photosynthetic efficiency (4.2%) and lower thermoregulation losses among poikilothermic marine Thermochromadae.

| Species | Size | Mass | Trophic level | Range |

**Thal-mor** is the largest animal of Théxar; migrates up to 1,626 mo per cycle following phytoplankton blooms, carrying ~0.9 Gt of biogenic elements per year from depths to surface. **Ven-Krill** biomass in the polar Koss Sea is ~1.4 Gt, base of all high-latitude communities.

## 3.5 Benthos and Reef Systems

### 3.5.1 Silicon-Calcium Reefs
Reef-builders build skeletons from a **silicon-calcium composite** (SiO₂·CaCO₃ ≈ 2:3), far less soluble in weakly acidic water.

| Parameter | Théxar reefs | Note |
|-----------|--------------|------|
| Skeleton material | Si-Ca composite | Stable at pH 7.6 |
| Maximum growth depth | 190 sha | Against ~50 m on Earth |
| Symbionts | Algae **and** chemoautotrophs | Double energy source |
| Growth rate | 0.4–1.8 pa/cycle | — |
| Number of reef-building species | ~2,400 | Of which 640 deep-water |

Double symbiosis (photosynthetic + H₂S-oxidizing chemoautotrophic bacteria) lets reefs hold a positive energy balance below the light limit and survive turbidity.

### 3.5.2 The Largest Reef Systems

| System | Ocean | Length | Depth | Species | Status |
|--------|-------|--------|-------|---------|--------|
| **Great Reef of Thalas** | Thalas | ~650 mo | 4–120 sha | ~28,000 | Reserve since +2,640 Y.E. |
| **Serrin Barrier** | Serrin Sea | ~310 mo | 6–90 sha | ~19,000 | Fishing limited |
| **Ven-Khal Deep Reef** | Ven-Khal Strait | ~140 mo | 90–190 sha | ~4,200 | Total activity ban |
| **Irkhen Ring** | Irkhen Sea | ~220 mo | 2–70 sha | ~11,500 | Restored after +2,900 Y.E. |

**The Ven-Khal Deep Reef** exists almost entirely on chemoautotrophic symbionts at 90–190 sha (illumination <1.4% of surface), supporting the hypothesis that the first reefs of Théxar were chemosynthetic.

### 3.5.3 Soft Grounds and Infauna
Abyssal plains (2.61 million mo² in Thalas) hold up to 1,200 species per mo² at only 0.02 ru/mo² biomass.

| Ground type | Share of the floor | Dominant group | Biomass |

## 3.6 Chemosynthetic Ecosystems

### 3.6.1 Hydrothermal Fields
**12 fields** of hydrothermal vents have been discovered on the floor of the oceans.

| Field | Ocean | Depth (sha) | Source T (°X) | Area (mo²) | Feature |
|-------|-------|-------------|---------------|-------------|---------|
| **Tor-1** | Thalas | 3,902 | 232.6 | 0.33 | The planet's largest field |
| **Thash-Deep** | Gharra | 10,000 | 254.3 | 0.09 | The deepest; 96% endemism |
| **Gharra-Vent** | Gharra | 5,854 | 213.5 | 0.21 | Rich in sulfur and REE |
| **Khar-Rift** | Thalas | 2,940 | 198.2 | 0.17 | Shallow, the most thoroughly studied |
| **Ven-Smoke** | Ven-Khal | 1,780 | 176.4 | 0.06 | Accessible to manned vehicles |
| **Koss-Cold** | Gharra | 4,210 | 121.9 | 0.11 | "Warm" field, low-temperature |

| Tier | Distance from the vent | Organisms | Metabolic base |
|------|------------------------|-----------|----------------|
| Vent | 0–3 sha | Thermophilic archae-analogues | Direct H₂S oxidation at 210–255°X |
| Tubular | 3–24 sha | Si-tubeworms (up to 2.4 sha) | Endosymbiosis with sulfobacteria |
| Molluscan | 24–120 sha | Bivalves, blind crabs | Symbiosis + filtration |
| Peripheral | 120–600 sha | Predators, scavengers | Consumption of field production |
| Background | >600 sha | Ordinary abyssal fauna | Detritus |

> **KEY FACT** — Field productivity reaches 12 ru/mo² — 600 times the surrounding abyss — yet fields occupy only 1.1 mo² total (<one-millionth of the floor): **refugia of ancient metabolic pathways**.

### 3.6.2 Cold Seeps and Sulfide Banks
More than 340 **cold seeps** (methane/H₂S seepages) are described; total area ~940 mo², nearly a thousand times the hydrothermal fields.

| Seep type | Depth (sha) | Main substrate | Number known |
|-----------|-------------|----------------|--------------|
| Methane (gas-hydrate) | 600–3,400 | CH₄ | 186 |
| Sulfide | 200–1,800 | H₂S | 121 |
| Brine pools | 900–2,600 | Hypersaline brine + H₂S | 33 |

Brine pools (salinity up to 18%) hold the most extremophilic forms, with lineages that diverged >900 million cycles ago per the Guild of Natural History.

### 3.6.3 Carrion Communities
A **Thal-mor** carcass on the abyssal floor creates an ecosystem lasting 40–90 cycles across four stages: scavengers (0.5–2), gnawers (2–8), sulfophilic (8–70), and reef (70–90). The sulfophilic stage releases H₂S from bone lipids, forming chemosynthetic "stepping stones" of dispersal between divided hydrothermal fields.

## 3.7 Hadal Ecosystems
The hadal zone (below 8,537 sha) is 1.3% of ocean volume — isolated "pockets" with the highest endemism.

| Trench | Ocean | Max depth (sha) | Pressure (tyazh) | T (°X) | Endemism |
|--------|-------|-----------------|------------------|--------|----------|
| **Thash** | Gharra | 12,010 | ~952 | 46.6 | 91% |
| **Khar-Tor** | Thalas | 10,270 | ~814 | 46.1 | 84% |
| **Irkhen-Deep** | Thalas | 9,140 | ~724 | 46.3 | 76% |
| **Koss-Trough** | Gharra | 8,900 | ~705 | 45.8 | 71% |

The Thash Trench is the planet's deepest point; bottom water is 1.6°X warmer than comparable trenches (elevated geothermal flux), supporting 610 described species, 555 of them endemic.

**Adaptations of the hadal fauna:** silicon-organic membranes (as in Moryn and Kel'vash); piezolytes rising linearly with depth; complete loss of gas cavities including auditory; metabolism 6–9× lower than shelf relatives; lifespans up to 240 cycles at small body size.

## 3.8 The Moryn as an Ecological Factor
The Gharra Ocean is an ecosystem **modified** by the third sentient species.

| Aspect | Scale of impact |
|--------|-----------------|
| Cultivated chemosynthetic fields | ~2.4 mo² of artificially maintained communities |
| Domesticated species | 14 (symbiotic bacteria, 6 mollusc species, 3 worm species) |
| Regulated populations | Gharra-Lance and 4 other apex predators |
| Bioluminescent "lighting" of settlements | Altered diel rhythm of fauna within a radius of up to 6 mo |
| Reserve zones established by the Moryn | 0.19 million mo² (within the Gharra Deep zone) |

The Moryn are the only sentient species whose agriculture is built entirely on chemosynthesis; their "fields" are artificially widened seeps fed by H₂S. This practice, ≥40,000 cycles old, makes them the planet's oldest farmers — though they have never seen sun or plants.

## 3.10 Threats and Conservation
Marine ecosystems survived heavy pressure during the Crisis Era (−2,100 – −750) and the Rebirth; modern policy rests on the Law of Silence and the Law of Consent.

| Threat | Peak of impact | Modern status |
|--------|----------------|---------------|
| Overfishing | −1,400 – +900 | Sustainable catch of 24 million t/cycle |
| Acidification (industrial CO₂) | +200 – +1,400 | Reversed; pH restored to 7.8–8.2 |
| Bottom trawling | +600 – +1,900 | Total ban since +1,940 Y.E. |
| Deep-sea nodule mining | +2,400 – present | Regulated; banned in 3 zones |
| Noise pollution | +1,100 – +2,300 | Law of Silence; critical for the Moryn |
| Invasive species (Rhu-Crab) | +2,600 – present | Barriers, local control |

**Protected waters:** 8% of ocean area has reserve status; the largest zone, **Gharra Deep** (0.51 million mo²), covers the southern trenches and the Moryn's main settlement area. Since +2,100 Y.E. no marine species extinction has been recorded.
