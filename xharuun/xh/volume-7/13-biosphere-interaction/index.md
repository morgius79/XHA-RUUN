## 13.1 The Carbon Cycle

Time scales span seconds (respiration) to hundreds of millions of cycles (carbonate burial).

### 13.1.1 Carbon Reservoirs

| Reservoir | Stock (Gt C) | Turnover time | Main process |
|-----------|--------------|---------------|--------------|
| Atmosphere (CO₂) | 2,840 | 4.2 cycles | Photosynthesis ↔ respiration |
| Ocean (dissolved) | 38,400 | 380 cycles | Carbonate pump |
| Biomass (terrestrial) | 1,186 | 14.2 cycles | Growth, death |
| Biomass (marine) | 14 | 2.6 cycles | Same |
| Soils and peat | 1,640 | 28 cycles | Decomposition |
| Carbonate rocks | 48,000,000 | 120 million cycles | Subduction, volcanism |
| Organogenic rocks | 12,000,000 | 180 million cycles | Same |

### 13.1.2 Carbon Fluxes (Gt C/cycle)

| Flux | Value | Path |
|------|-------|------|
| Photosynthesis | 120 | Atmosphere → terrestrial biosphere |
| Respiration, fires | 114 | Terrestrial biosphere → atmosphere |
| Death, erosion | 61 | Biosphere → soils |
| Leaching | 24 | Soils → ocean |
| Burial in sediments | 0.12 | Ocean → lithosphere |
| Volcanism, metamorphism | 0.082 | Lithosphere → atmosphere |

Terrestrial production: 84 Gt C/cycle. Marine production: 36 Gt C/cycle. Lithosphere total: 60,000,000 Gt C.

### 13.1.3 The Biological Carbon Pump

1. **Carbonate pump** — reefs and plankton build silicon-calcium composite skeletons (→ rhu [Thexar-VII, Sha-El 03, "Marine Ecosystems"]), binding CO₂;
2. **Organic pump** — dead biomass and fecal pellets descend into the depths;
3. **Vertical migration** — zooplankton feeds at the surface at night, metabolizes in the depths by day.

Efficiency: ~8.4 Gt C/cycle carried from the surface layer into the depths — 18% higher than the terrestrial figure, thanks to heavier, faster-sinking silicon skeletons.

### 13.1.4 Climate Regulation

CO₂ at 0.8% (2,840 Gt C) gives a greenhouse effect of +31°C — higher than pre-industrial Earth (0.028%), but necessary: the orange star Khar'Vex delivers 15% less radiation than the Sun (→ rhu [Thexar-I, Ruun 03, "The Planet Théxar"]). Negative feedback: CO₂ ↑ → temperature ↑ → silicate weathering ↑ → CO₂ bound into carbonates → concentration ↓. Response time: 100,000–500,000 cycles.

### 13.2.2 Biological Fixation

Carried out by **diazotrophs** (nitrogen-fixing bacteria and cyano-like forms).

| Mechanism | Fixation (Gt N/cycle) | Main agents |
|-----------|----------------------|-------------|

| **Total** | **162** | — |

Anthropogenic fixation exceeds natural soil fixation (42 vs 34 Gt N/cycle) from intensive agriculture, but causes no ecological crisis — unlike the Crisis Era — thanks to strict runoff control.

### 13.2.3 Denitrification

Anaerobic respiration of denitrifying bacteria returns bound nitrogen to the atmosphere as N₂ or N₂O.

| Environment | Denitrification (Gt N/cycle) |
|-------------|-----------------------------|
| Soils (anaerobic zones) | 68 |

| **Total** | **158** |

Balance: 162 − 158 = **+4 Gt N/cycle** accumulation of bound nitrogen.

## 13.3 The Sulfur Cycle

Unique to Théxar: H₂S is not metabolic waste but a full-fledged energy substrate (→ rhu [Thexar-VII, Sha-El 03, "Marine Ecosystems"]).

### 13.3.1 Sulfur Reservoirs

|-----------|--------------|------|---------------|
| Atmosphere | 0.084 | H₂S (0.0008%) | 18 days |
| Biomass | 12 | Sulfur-containing amino acids | 6 cycles |
| Ocean (dissolved) | 1,280 | SO₄²⁻, H₂S | 12,000 cycles |
| Soils | 840 | Sulfides, sulfates, organic S | 240 cycles |
| Evaporites, pyrites | 24,000,000 | FeS₂, CaSO₄ | 180 million cycles |

### 13.3.2 Sulfur Fluxes (million ru S/cycle)

| Flux | Value | Path |
|------|-------|------|
| Biogenic emission | 46 | Biosphere → atmosphere |
| Wet/dry deposition | 48 | Atmosphere → biosphere + soils |
| River runoff of sulfates | 121 | Land → ocean |

| Pyrite burial in sediments | 118 | Ocean → lithosphere |
| Volcanism, degassing | 72 | Lithosphere → atmosphere |

Chemosynthesis: 52 million ru S/cycle on land, 340 million ru S/cycle in the ocean.

### 13.3.3 Biogenic Emission of H₂S

H₂S at 0.0008% is the **biomarker of Théxar**, visible from space.

| Source | Emission (million ru S/cycle) |
|---------|-------------------------------|
| Oceanic phytoplankton (DMS → H₂S) | 18 |
| Decomposition of organics in swamps | 12 |
| Thexophyta epiphytes (H₂S oxidation + re-emission) | 8 |
| Hydrothermal fields (direct release) | 4 |
| Volcanism | 4 |
| **Total** | **46** |

DMS (dimethyl sulfide) from marine phytoplankton oxidizes to SO₂ and H₂SO₄, forming aerosol condensation nuclei; oceanic cloudiness rises 4–7%, raising albedo and cooling the planet — **biological climate regulation**.

### 13.3.4 Chemosynthesis as a Dominant

- Ocean: 340 million ru S/cycle → 8.4 Gt C/cycle (23% of marine production);
- Land (soils, caves): 52 million ru S/cycle → 1.2 Gt C/cycle (1.4% of terrestrial).

Total: **8%** of planetary primary production — 40× the terrestrial figure.

## 13.4 The Silicon Cycle

Biologically active on Théxar, practically absent as such on Earth (→ rhu [Thexar-VII, Sha-El 09, "The Plant Kingdom"]).

### 13.4.1 Silicon Reservoirs

| Reservoir | Stock (Gt Si) | Form | Turnover time |
|-----------|---------------|------|---------------|
| Ocean (dissolved) | 28 | H₄SiO₄ | 18,000 cycles |
| Biomass (diatoms, plants) | 0.84 | Amorphous SiO₂ | 4 cycles |
| Soils | 840,000 | Silicates, silica | 340,000 cycles |
| Earth's crust | 240,000,000,000 | Silicates, quartz | — |

### 13.4.2 Biological Mobilization

- **Silodiatoma** (silica-flagellates) — 24% of marine phytoplankton;
- **Silophyta** — 45,000 species of terrestrial silicon plants;
- **Mycorrhizal fungi** — dissolve and transport SiO₂ to the roots;
- **Reef polyps** — silicon-calcium skeletons;
- **Sponge-analogues** — silicon spicules.

The biota extracts **12 Gt Si/cycle** from the ocean; the biological silicon pump accelerates the cycle **14-fold** over the abiotic rate.

### 13.4.3 Climate Feedback

CaSiO₃ + CO₂ → CaCO₃ + SiO₂. Biological acceleration of weathering (roots, mycorrhiza) intensifies CO₂ binding: warming → faster plant growth → more roots → more weathering → CO₂ ↓ → cooling. Response time 100,000 cycles.

Average plankton biomass composition: **C₁₀₆N₁₆P₁S₀.₅Si₁₅**. Differences from Earth: more sulfur (0.5 vs 0.1), more silicon (15 vs 0).

## 13.6 The Biosphere and the Atmosphere

### 13.6.1 Composition of the Atmosphere

| Gas | Concentration | Source | Sink |
|-----|---------------|--------|------|
| N₂ | 71.8% | Volcanism (primary) | Fixation ↔ denitrification (balance) |
| O₂ | 22.6% | **Photosynthesis** | Respiration, rock oxidation |
| Ar | 2.9% | Decay of ⁴⁰K | — |
| CO₂ | 0.8% | Respiration, volcanism | **Photosynthesis** |
| H₂O | 1.2% (average) | Evaporation | Precipitation |
| H₂S | 0.0008% | **Biogenic emission** | Oxidation, deposition |

> **KEY FACT**
>
> Without life the atmosphere of Théxar would be N₂ 95%, CO₂ 4%, Ar 1%, O₂ <0.001%. The current composition is entirely a product of the biosphere — a **thermodynamically non-equilibrium system** maintained by life.

### 13.6.2 Regulation of Oxygen

O₂ at 22.6% exceeds Earth's 21% due to higher photosynthetic efficiency (4.2% vs 3%) at the same respiration rate. Rise is capped by fire risk — at 25% spontaneous combustion becomes frequent. Feedback: O₂ ↑ → more fires → biomass burns → production ↓ → O₂ ↓. Stabilization at 22.6%.

### 13.6.3 Greenhouse Gases

| Gas | Contribution to the greenhouse effect (+31°C) |
|-----|-----------------------------------------------|
| H₂O | 54% |
| CO₂ | 38% |
| CH₄ | 6% |
| H₂S | 2% |

CH₄ (0.0003%) is biogenic — anaerobic decomposition in swamps and herbivore guts. Atmospheric lifetime: 8.4 cycles (oxidized to CO₂).

## 13.8 Feedbacks and Homeostasis

### 13.8.1 The Living Planet Hypothesis

| Disturbance | Feedback | Response time |
|-------------|----------|---------------|
| Rise of CO₂ | Intensification of photosynthesis → absorption of CO₂ | 10–100 cycles |
| Fall of O₂ | Fewer fires → growth of biomass → more O₂ | 100–1,000 cycles |
| Warming | More DMS → more clouds → cooling | 1–10 cycles |
| Cooling | Less weathering → growth of CO₂ → warming | 10,000–100,000 cycles |

### 13.8.2 The Limits of Homeostasis

The five mass extinctions (→ rhu [Thexar-VII, Sha-El 12, "Extinction Events"]) show that a sufficiently strong disturbance **switches** the system into a new state: the Oxygen Catastrophe (anaerobic → aerobic biosphere) and total glaciation ("snowball," reversible only through volcanism).
