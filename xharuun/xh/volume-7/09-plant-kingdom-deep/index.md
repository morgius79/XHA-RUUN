## 09.1 The Photosynthetic Apparatus

Photosynthetic efficiency of 4.2% comes from a coordinated antenna complex of five pigments.

| Pigment | Color | Absorption peak | Localization in the leaf | Role |
|---------|-------|-----------------|--------------------------|------|
| Chromophyll a | Copper-red | 570 nm | Reaction center | Primary, photosystem I |
| Chromophyll b | Golden | 540 nm | Light-harvesting complexes | Auxiliary, photosystem II |
| Theta-carotene | Orange | 480 nm | Periphery | Photoprotection |
| Ther xanthophyll | Yellow | 450 nm | Inner antenna | Heat dissipation, Ther cycle |
| Seriflavin | Purple | 620 nm | Lower side of the leaf | Capture of reflected light |

**The double-sided leaf.** Upper side saturated with chromophyll a/b (direct light of Khar'Vex), lower side with **seriflavin**, capturing light reflected from soil and neighboring leaves. In jungle understory up to 23% of a leaf's photosynthesis comes from upward scattered light.

**The Ther cycle.** Ther xanthophyll reversibly converts into theroflavin, dissipating 78% of excess energy as heat in 2–4 minutes. Plants endure brief illuminances up to 1,100 W/m² without damage, though photosynthesis saturates at 420 W/m².

## 09.2 The Vascular System and Silicon

Amorphous silica (SiO₂) is deposited in the cell walls of conducting elements throughout the flora.

| Division | SiO₂ content in dry mass | Tissue hardness (Mohs) | Water transport |
|----------|--------------------------|------------------------|-----------------|
| Chromophyta | 2–9% | 2.5–4.0 | Vessels + tracheids |
| Silophyta | 8–35% | 3.5–5.5 | Tracheids only |
| Thexophyta (epiphytes) | 3–7% | 2.0–3.0 | Moisture capture by bark |
| Thalophyta (algae) | 4–18% | — | Osmotic |
| Bryoforma | 5–22% | — | Capillary |

Silicon performs four functions: structural (rigidity), protective (against herbivores), radiative (UV shielding), and hydraulic (reduced cavitation under water stress). The silicon ring around vascular bundles prevents vessel collapse at negative water pressure, allowing Chromophyta to reach 100+ sha.

**Concentrating metabolism.** Over 60% of vascular species pump CO₂ into bundle-sheath cells, where chromophyll works at 6–8 times atmospheric substrate concentration.

| Parameter | C₃-like | Concentrating |
|-----------|---------|---------------|
| Share of species | ~40% | ~60% |
| Photorespiration (losses) | 22–30% | 8–12% |
| Transpiration (rel.) | 100 | 75 |
| Photosynthetic efficiency | 3.0–3.6% | 4.2–4.9% |
| Ecological optimum | Humid forests, coasts | Dry savannas, steppes, highlands |

## 09.4 Life Strategies

Plants are synchronized with the calendar of 412.7 local days, 14 months of ~29.5 days, and 5 days of Renewal. A typical Chromophyta year has **six phases**:

| Phase | Dates | Event |
|-------|-------|-------|
| I. Awakening | months 1–2 (Khar-Ven, Serrin-Tash) | Bud opening, root growth |
| II. Vegetation | months 3–4 (Thalas-Mor, Ven-Khal) | Maximum shoot growth |
| III. Flowering | months 5–6 (Rass-Ur, Sha-En) | Reproductive structures |
| IV. Fruiting | months 7–8 (Khar-Tor, The-Nur) | Ripening of fruits and seeds |
| V. Shedding | months 9–10 (Gharra-Vek, Mor-Irk) | Leaf fall, storage of substances |
| VI. Dormancy | months 11–14 (Thar-Ghal — Veks-An) | Deep dormancy, 5 days of Renewal |

Chromophyta wood grows **six rings per year**, one per phase:

| Layer | Phase | Density | Color | Information for dendrochronology |
|-------|-------|---------|-------|----------------------------------|
| 1 | Awakening | Low | Light | Length of the dry season |
| 2 | Vegetation (early) | High | Dark | Abundance of precipitation |
| 3 | Vegetation (late) | Medium | Medium | Temperature |
| 4 | Flowering | High | Dark | Wind regime |
| 5 | Fruiting | Low | Light | Reserve of nutrients |
| 6 | Dormancy | Minimal | Almost white | — |

From the rings of the **Mor-Ven** (Tree of Time) the Keepers of the Archives reconstruct climate at a resolution of one vegetation phase; continuous series reach 38,000 cycles.

**Ephemeral strategies.** Annuals of the Shan Desert (genus **Ash-Grass**) complete their life cycle in 6 weeks after rain; a germination inhibitor breaks down only after a second wetting at an interval of no less than 11 days.

## 09.5 Unique Forms

**Gethers — moving plants.** Subclass **Ghett-Chroma** ("walking chromophytes"), 14 species of ephemeral grasses of the dry plains of Rhuval.

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| Speed | Up to 0.9 mo per local day |
| Mechanism | Directed drying-swelling of the root "foot" |
| Migration range | Up to 240 sha in a lifetime |
| Reason for movement | Following local rains |
| Population | ~2.4 billion individuals (seasonal peak) |

A full "step" takes 6–9 minutes; visible movement requires 10–14 days of observation. The storage "foot" of 18–24 root lobes anchors the plant in drought and walks it in the wet season.

**Guy-wire lianas.** Genus **Rhu-Koss** (up to 61 sha) climbs Tor-Khar trunks along its own taut fibers anchored in the host's bark, opening a crown at 30–60 sha. One plant intercepts up to 4.1% of the light falling on a canopy patch with 800 times less biomass than the supporting tree.

**H₂S-filtering epiphytes.** Division **Thexophyta** absorbs hydrogen sulfide directly from air. **Xha-Ven** ("the rational fern") forms a cushion filter up to 2.4 sha across; per cycle one plant binds 3–6 ru of sulfur and reduces ground-layer H₂S within a radius of up to 40 sha by 18–26%, with an auxiliary chemotrophic circuit duplicating photosynthesis.
