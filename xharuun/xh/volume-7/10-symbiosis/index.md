## 10.2 Endosymbiosis: The Cell as a Union

### 10.2.1 The Silicon Symbiont

~1.4 billion cycles ago a proto-eukaryote absorbed a **silicon bacterium** (Silobacteriae) capable of polymerizing SiO₂ into transmembrane pores, yielding: heat/pressure-resistant channels; selective Si-ion transport; radiation resistance. Descendants persist as **membrane symbionts** in every eukaryote-like cell on Théxar — functional analogues of mitochondria.

> **KEY FACT** — The membrane silicon symbiont retains no independent genome: only **37 genes** remain, encoding silicon pore assembly proteins. One of the most complete cases of genomic absorption in known biology.

### 10.2.2 Sulfur Endosymbionts

| Host | Environment | Share of energy from the symbiont | Role of the symbiont |
|------|-------------|-----------------------------------|----------------------|
| Si-tubeworms | Hydrothermal vents | 96% | C fixation from H₂S |
| Bivalve analogues | Cold seeps | 88% | Same |
| Moryn (domesticated) | Cultivated fields | 30–40% | Supplementary nutrition |
| Blind crabs | Caves | 55% | H₂S utilization |
| Abyssal stinger (Thal-shak) | Trenches | 12% | Vitamin synthesis |

Sulfoendosymbionts transmit vertically (via eggs/larvae) — a rare exception to the environmental-acquisition rule.

### 10.3.3 Bleaching and Recovery

At +2.2–2.8°X above norm, polyps expel **chemosymbionts first** (photosymbionts retained — the reverse of terrestrial bleaching). Recovery: 8–14 months, twice as fast as Earth, since surviving chemosymbionts sustain the polyp during regeneration.

## 10.4 Terrestrial Symbiosis

### 10.4.1 Bryoforma Lichens

~11,000 species described; unique **triple composition** — fungus + phototroph + silicon bacterium.

| Component | Role |
|-----------|------|
| Mycobiont (fungus) | Framework, water transport, protection |
| Photobiont | Photosynthesis (chromophyll or phycobilin) |
| Silicon bacterium | SiO₂ reinforcement, radiation screen |

Lichens pioneer lava fields and cliffs, initiating soil formation.

### 10.4.2 Mycorrhiza and Silicon Fungi

**90% of terrestrial plants** form mycorrhiza with Mycophyta fungi; **silicon hyphae** reinforced with SiO₂ penetrate plant silicon walls.

| Mycorrhiza type | Partner plants | Exchange |
|-----------------|----------------|----------|
| Arbuscular | Khar-Chroma (grasses) | Phosphorus, silicon ↔ sugars |
| Ectotrophic | Ven-Chroma (forest) | Nitrogen, water ↔ sugars |
| Silicon (unique) | Silophyta | SiO₂ mobilization ↔ sugars |

**Silicon mycorrhiza** — the chief accelerator of the terrestrial silicon cycle; no terrestrial-Earth analogue.

### 10.4.3 Epiphytic Cushions

Thexophyta forms "cushion cities" on Tor-Khar trunks — up to **140 species** per tree. Closed micro-cycle: (1) Xha-Ven filters H₂S; (2) mosses store water/nutrients; (3) Xhel-kreb (ant-analogues) fertilize substrate; (4) epiphyte roots convert to humus reused by the crown. Epiphyte biomass per tree: up to **4.2 ru**.

## 10.5 Predator-Prey Coevolution

### 10.5.1 The Arms Race in the Savannas — Ghal-thass vs. Xa-ruunidae (kharru)

| Predator adaptation | Prey response | Selection cycle |
|---------------------|---------------|-----------------|
| Trinocular vision (accuracy 0.2°) | Circular herd view (peripheral eyes) | 1.2 million cycles |
| Burst speed 13.2 mo/h | Endurance running 9.8 mo/h over 20 mo | 3.4 million cycles |
| IR sensors on the forelimbs | Thermo-chromatic masking (cooling spots) | 2.1 million cycles |
| Electroreception (rudimentary) | Group defense (ring of horns) | 5.6 million cycles |

Kharru cooling spots (3–5°X below body) break up the IR silhouette beyond 40 sha. Trait spread in ~**900,000 cycles** — planet's fastest coevolutionary adaptation.

### 10.5.2 Pollination

| Pollination system | Share of species | Main agents |
|--------------------|-----------------|-------------|
| Zoophily | 58% | Six-winged, leaf-nosed, ants |
| Anemophily | 31% | Wind |
| Hydrophily | 6% | Water (coastal) |
| Getherophily (active) | 5% | Gethers |

**Getherophily** — unique active pollination: Silophyta gethers "walk" between partner plants along attractant gradients. Coevolutionary pair ~**28 million cycles** old.

### 10.5.3 The Light Language in the Depths

In the aphotic zone, bioluminescent "tongues" of fish-analogues and predators exchange species-species pair signals.
