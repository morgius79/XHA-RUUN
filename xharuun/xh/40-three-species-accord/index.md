## 40.1 Comparative Overview

Théxar is the only known planet to have given rise to not one but three independently arisen sentient species. This chapter closes the naturalistic survey of Volume I: how three radically different life forms share a single planet — not by displacing one another, but by complementing each other, occupying three distinct niches of one world.

## 40.2 The Council of Unity and the Principle of Three

**CANON §4.3 — Law of Accord:** No decision of the Council of Unity may be made without consulting representatives of all three sentient species of Théxar — Xha'Ruun, Kel'vash, and Moryn. This provision, known as the **Principle of Three**, was established upon the Kel'vash admission (+1 200 yr.) and confirmed upon the Moryn admission (+1 500 yr.).

The Principle of Three is not merely a diplomatic formality but a structural response to a problem Xha'Ruun civilization recognized early: the three species perceive the world, time, and ethics so differently that decisions made by only one of them systematically undervalue the interests of the other two.

### 40.2.1 The Reconciliation Mechanism

Because the three species communicate, think, and act at radically different tempos (seconds to minutes for Xha'Ruun, hours to days for Kel'vash, a dialogue slowed roughly threefold for Moryn), the Council developed a multi-stage protocol:

1. **Initiation** — the matter is raised in Xha'Ruun spoken language (the Council's working language)
2. **Translation** — "tempo translators" for Kel'vash, transluminators for Moryn convert the matter into vibrational and bioluminescent formats
3. **Deliberation** — each species considers the matter at its own pace (from hours to several days)
4. **Reconciliation** — the responses are synchronized and presented simultaneously
5. **Ratification** — the decision takes effect only if none of the three species objects

In both cases the delay stems from the same factor: each species' communication is so unlike spoken language that recognizing it as *language*, rather than a natural phenomenon, took centuries of observation.

## 40.4 Interspecies Communication Barriers

| Barrier | Xha'Ruun ↔ Kel'vash | Xha'Ruun ↔ Moryn | Kel'vash ↔ Moryn |
|---------|---------------------|-------------------|-------------------|
| Tempo | ~40× difference | ~3× difference | ~13× difference |
| Channel | Sound ↔ vibration | Sound ↔ light | Vibration ↔ light |
| Privacy of thought | Compatible | Incompatible (Moryn thought is public) | Both "transparent" to differing degrees |
| Temporal ethics | Kel'vash — "geological" thinking | Moryn — collective present | Requires double translation |

The most difficult remains direct dialogue between Kel'vash and Moryn — two species whose communication has never naturally overlapped (mountains and the deep ocean do not touch). All contact between them passes through Xha'Ruun mediation and a double technical conversion of the signal.

## 40.5 Ecological Unity of Théxar

The three species occupy three mutually exclusive ecological niches, together covering nearly the full range of conditions suitable for complex life:

```
        THE THREE NICHES OF SENTIENT LIFE ON THÉXAR

  Altitude/Depth           Species          Temperature
  ──────────────────────────────────────────────────
   +2,400…+9,500 sha     Kel'vash          180–220°X
        (mountains/deserts)
   ──────────────────────────────────────────────────
      0…+2,400 sha       Xha'Ruun          ~30–65°X
        (all land)
   ──────────────────────────────────────────────────
   −1,200…−3,000 sha       Moryn             2–8°X
        (Gharra depths)
```

None of the three species competes directly with the others for territory or resources — which, in the view of Xha'Ruun historians, is one reason first contact (unlike hypothetical scenarios in Xha'Ruun fiction, → rhu [Ruun 10, "Vokh-Ven Sha-El"]) led not to conflict but to cooperation.

## 40.6 Conclusion of Volume I

Here the naturalistic and civilizational survey of Théxar comes full circle: from the star Khar'Vex and the planet's formation (Ruun 1–3, 12) — through biochemistry and the origin of life (Ruun 4, 20) — to the emergence of not one but three independent minds, sharing a planet without empty niches between them (Ruun 5, 38–40).

Volume I has given the reader a complete picture of Théxar as a physical and biological whole. Volume II ("History") continues from the standpoint of events and civilization; Volumes III–X delve into society, language, culture, technology, and nature for each of the three species in turn.
