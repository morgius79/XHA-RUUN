## Introduction

The political system of Xha'Ruun civilization is the result of a millennium of institutional evolution. From the theocratic chiefdoms of early protocities through empire and fragmentation — to a unique system of dual representation in which professional and territorial interests are balanced. At the system's core is the principle of "unity without uniformity": common norms with maximum autonomy of the parts.

This chapter describes the structure of modern governance: its principles, the structure of the Council of Unity, executive mechanisms, and the place of the Keeper of Knowledge.

---

## 1.1 Principles of Governance

| Principle | Description | Implementation |
|-----------|-------------|----------------|
| Meritocracy | Power by ability | Guild system |
| Collegiality | Decisions made collectively | Council of Unity (49 members) |
| Transparency | Access to information | Law of Knowledge |
| Sustainability | Long-term planning | Growth Commandment |

**Meritocracy** — power based on proven ability, not origin. The guild system (→ see [Volume II, Ch.08, "History of the Guilds"]) is its institutional foundation: the path to power lies through professional recognition.

**Collegiality** — no body decides unilaterally. The Council of Unity, domains, and guilds make all decisions collectively; key ones require the consent of both chambers.

**Transparency** — the Law of Knowledge guarantees access to information (→ see [Volume II, Ch.12, "Legal History"]). Council decisions are published, and their rationale is available to all.

**Sustainability** — long-term planning governed by the Growth Commandment and ecological limits.

---

## 1.2 Structure of Power

![Structure of Xha'Ruun power: Council of Unity (49) → Executive Committee (12) → 8 Domains → 12 Guilds](../../assets/art/ART-000030-xharuun-power-structure.png)

**The Council of Unity (Rass-Vokh-Sha)** — the supreme body of **49 members**: 24 delegates from 12 guilds (2 each) and 25 delegates from eight domains. The **Keeper of Knowledge** participates without vote — his function is constitutional: control of decisions' conformity to the canon.

The Council Chair is elected from among the members for 10 years and chairs the sessions without a casting vote.

---

## 1.3 Executive Power

The Executive Committee (12 members) is the Council's working body, ensuring the execution of its decisions. The committee is formed proportionally to the Council's composition: 6 members from guilds, 6 from domains.

Committee functions:
- Preparation of decisions and analytical materials;
- Coordination of domains' and guilds' activities;
- Management of planetary programs (infrastructure, space);
- Interaction with the Keeper of Knowledge.

The committee has no legislative power — it is accountable to the Council and can be re-elected.

---

## 1.4 Territorial Structure

Local implementation is ensured by **eight domains** (→ see [Volume II, Ch.11, "History of the Eight Domains"]): domain → province → district.

Domains have broad autonomy: local laws, culture, education. The Council — domain — province vertical is complemented by the horizontal structure of guilds, whose headquarters are distributed across domains.

---

## 1.5 Guilds as Governance Sectors

The twelve guilds are the second circuit of power, representing the professional cross-section of society. Each guild manages its sector: economy, science, healthcare, infrastructure, culture.

Guilds not only delegate members to the Council but also implement state functions locally: standards, licensing, training, adjudication (→ see [Volume II, Ch.08, "History of the Guilds"]).

---

## 1.6 The Role of the Keeper of Knowledge

**The Keeper of Knowledge** — a unique institution: a lifelong office without political power, but with a constitutional function.

The Keeper:
- Ensures Council decisions conform to the canon;
- Heads the Keeper Guild (archives, libraries);
- Has the right to suspend a decision contradicting the canon, referring it for reconsideration;
- Is nominally not elected — appointed by the previous Keeper with Council confirmation.

This office embodies the principle of the **inviolability of history**: knowledge of the past is protected from political expediency.

---

## Chapter Summary

1. Governance rests on four principles: meritocracy, collegiality, transparency, sustainability.
2. The Council of Unity — 49 members (24 guilds + 25 regions), the Keeper without vote.
3. The Executive Committee (12 members) — the Council's working body.
4. Territorial vertical: domain → province → district; guilds — the horizontal circuit.
5. The Keeper of Knowledge — a constitutional office, guarantor of the inviolability of history.
