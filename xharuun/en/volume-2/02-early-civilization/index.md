## Introduction

The early civilization era is the period in which Théxar society made the transition that determined its entire subsequent trajectory: from non-literate kin groups to cities, crafts, and the first state formations. In 3,600 years the Xha'ri covered a path that on other planets took several times longer.

The era opens with the invention of proto-writing (−9,780) and ends with the founding of the First Empire (−6,150) — the threshold from which the next stage begins. In this time all the basic institutions of civilization were created: writing, the city, metallurgy, specialized crafts, regular trade, temples, and the first codified norms.

Chronological accuracy for this era is higher than for the pre-civilization era (uncertainty ±20–50 years), since written monuments allow events to be dated by references rather than only by archaeology.

## 2.1 Invention of Writing

**−9,780 y.c.** — a key date in the history of civilization. Proto-writing emerged **independently in three regions**: the Thar Valley (Kharak), the Rhuval coast, and southern Vexar. The oldest and most developed center is the Thar Valley.

The very fact of three independent inventions points to two important circumstances. First, by −9,800 Xha'ri societies had reached sufficient density and complexity for a *need* to fix information to arise. Second, the separation of the centers means that writing was not an "accidental discovery" of a single genius, but a natural product of social evolution.

The development of writing passed through four stages:

| Stage | Period | Type | Example |
|-------|--------|------|---------|
| Pictograms | −9,800 – −9,500 | Drawings of objects | ^^^ (grain) |
| Ideograms | −9,500 – −9,200 | Abstract signs | ⨁ (god/spirit) |
| Syllabographs | −9,200 – −8,500 | Syllabic signs | 𐤑𐰀 (xha) |
| Logograms | −8,500 | Word-signs | 𐤑𐰀𐰺𐰆𐰣 (Xha'Ruun) |

Each stage was not a mechanical replacement of the previous one, but an accumulation: pictograms did not disappear but entered the ideographic script, syllabographs coexisted with logograms. By the end of the era a mixed syllabo-logographic system had formed, which later evolved into the classical Sha'Ruun script (→ see [Volume I, Ch.06, "Language and Writing"]).

## 2.2 First Cities

Sedentism, surplus product, and written accounting created the preconditions for the era's main social invention — the **city**.

| City | Founded | Population (peak) | Significance |
|------|---------|-------------------|-------------|
| Thar-Vex | ~−11,500 | — | Oldest settlement, protocity |
| **Khar-Vexar** | **−7,800** | 18.6 million (current) | Oldest continuously inhabited city |
| Thar-Mor | −7,200 | 2.3 million | Religious center |
| Ven-Khal | −6,800 | 12.4 million (current) | Trade center of Rhuval |

**Khar-Vexar** holds a special place in history. Founded in −7,800 on the site of the oldest settlement Thar-Vex, it became the first true city in the full sense: with temples, administrative quarters, written bureaucracy, and fortifications. Since −7,800 the city has been continuously inhabited — the oldest continuously inhabited city of Théxar (EV-0003, LOC-0001).

The cities of the era arose at the intersection of three factors: a fertile valley (agricultural production), trade routes (exchange), and a cult center (ideology). Thar-Mor became a religious center — here the early priestly schools formed, later united in the Teaching of Vibration (→ see [Volume I, Ch.10, "Mythology and Religion"]).

## 2.3 Crafts and Metallurgy

**−9,200** — the first copper (Kharak mines). The beginning of metallurgy became the technological threshold of the era: copper tools and ornaments supplemented (not replaced) the stone industry.

| Material | Appearance | Application |
|----------|-----------|-------------|
| Obsidian, flint | since the pre-civilization era | Work tools |
| Pottery | ~−9,500 | Storage, firing |
| Copper | −9,200 | Tools, ornaments, ritual objects |
| Bronze | end of era | Beginning of alloys |

With metallurgy came the emergence of **specialized craftsmen** — the first professional group not directly engaged in food production. Their existence required a surplus and a redistribution system, which accelerated social stratification.

---

## 2.4 Social Stratification and Power

By the middle of the era the kin group yields to the **territorial** (neighboring) community. Along with it appear the first stable forms of inequality:

- **Priesthood** — control over ritual life and, through writing, over knowledge;
- **Military leaders** — protection of settlements, relying on retinues;
- **Elder-administrators** — management of surplus product and irrigation works.

Power in the early protocities took the form of theocratic chiefdom: the high priest-leader simultaneously directed the cult, justice, and distribution. This model — the union of spiritual and secular power in one person — became the precondition for the institution of emperor in the First Empire (→ see [Volume II, Ch.03, "The First Empire"]).

---

## 2.5 Trade and Exchange Networks

By the end of the era regular trade networks had formed, linking all three continents:

- **Obsidian** — from the volcanic massifs of Vexar to Kharak and Rhuval;
- **Copper** — from the Kharak mines;
- **Pottery and ornaments** — prestige goods marking status.

The regularity of exchange is confirmed by the standardization of weight units: weights of identical mass (±2%) have been found in different settlements, implying an agreed measurement system long before the unified calendar. Trade followed two routes — riverine (along the Thar and other rivers) and coastal (along the shores by boat).

---

## 2.6 Cults and Temples

The spiritual life of the era is reflected in temple archaeology:

- **Resonator temple** in Thar-Mor (prototype of Khar-Vokh-Thel) — early acoustic structures for collective singing;
- **Shrines of the Vexar caves** — continuation of pre-civilization traditions;
- **Ritual pottery** with symbols of celestial bodies — confirmation of an early moon cult.

Religious ideas gradually systematize: from local cults a common pantheon forms, in which the figure of the demiurge Khar-Vex holds the supreme place. This prepared the ground for the Teaching of Vibration — the doctrine that would later become the dominant religion of the civilization.

---

## 2.7 End of the Era: Preconditions of the First Empire

By −6,200 the civilization possessed all the components of statehood: cities, writing, metallurgy, a professional priesthood, trade networks, and codified customs. Only one thing was missing — a political union capable of managing these components as a single whole.

The answer was the **Code of Khar** (≈−7,200) — the oldest known written legal code (40 articles carved on a stone stele in Khar-Vexar). It regulated property, marriage (triads), punishments, and debts. The Code is the first step from custom to law: the norm ceased to be oral tradition and became a fixed text.

The founding of the First Empire in −6,150 (EV-0004) was the logical conclusion of the era: the institutions created in early civilization required a political form capable of uniting them.

## Chapter Summary

1. Proto-writing was invented independently in three regions (−9,780); the oldest center was the Thar Valley.
2. Khar-Vexar (−7,800) — the oldest continuously inhabited city of Théxar.
3. The first copper (−9,200) began metallurgy and the emergence of specialized craftsmen.
4. The territorial community replaced the kin group; power took the form of theocratic chiefdom.
5. Regular trade networks formed between the three continents.
6. The Code of Khar (−7,200) — the first step from custom to codified law.
7. The era ends with the founding of the First Empire (−6,150).
