## Introduction

The calendar is the first tool with which Xha'Ruun civilization ordered time. Its history reflects the transition from observing natural cycles (moon phases, solstices) to a unified planetary system of reckoning introduced in 0 y.e. This chapter traces the evolution of the calendar and the structure of the modern unified year.

---

## 17.1 Development of the Calendar

| Period | Calendar | Basis |
|--------|----------|-------|
| −10,000 – −8,000 | Lunar (Serrin) | Moon phases |
| −8,000 – −5,000 | Solar-lunar | Serrin + solstices |
| −5,000 – −2,150 | Imperial | 14 months |
| −2,150 – −750 | Regional | Divergent systems |
| −750 – +850 | Pre-Unity | 14 months + 5 days |
| 0 – present | Unified calendar | Standard |

The earliest calendars relied on the **phases of Serrin** — the largest moon of Théxar, visible from the surface (→ see [Volume I, Ch.25, "The Night Sky"]). The transition to the solar-lunar calendar (−8,000) is linked to the development of agriculture: planting cycles required attachment to seasons, not just moon phases.

---

## 17.2 The Unified Calendar

Adopted in **0 y.e.** (EV-0009) as the result of centuries of standardization begun by the Unity Treaty:

- The year lasts **412.7 local days** (~10,040 standard hours);
- **14 months** of ~29.5 days, synchronized with the phase of Serrin;
- **5 "intercalary" Days of Renewal** (Ven-Khal) at the end of the year; in every 5th year — 6 days;
- Accuracy: 1 day of error per ~3,200 years.

Month names (Volume II, reference canon): Khar-Ven, Serrin-Tash, Thalas-Mor, Ven-Khal, Rass-Ur, Sha-En, Khar-Tor, The-Nur, Gharra-Vek, Mor-Irkh, Thar-Ghal, Ruun-Shan, Serrin-Ur, Veks-An.

> 📝 **Editor's note:** the month names adopted in Volume II differ from the list in [Volume I, Ch.34 "Measurement Systems"]. The discrepancy is recorded and subject to reconciliation in the canon.

## 17.3 Days of Renewal

The five Days of Renewal (Ven-Khal) are a unique element of the calendar: they belong to no month and complete the year. This is not a "leap adjustment" but a full social institution — a period of celebration, renewal of obligations, and collective rituals. Every fifth year a sixth Day of Renewal is added to compensate for accumulated error.
