## Introduction

The history of Xha'Ruun science is the history of turning empirical knowledge into systematic science, and then into the foundation of a post-scarcity civilization. The path from the first calendar observations to quantum communication took ~15,000 years, and each era added a new dimension to the scientific method. This chapter traces four stages: the pre-scientific, the era of discovery, the industrial-thermonuclear revolution, and modern science.

---

## 10.1 The Pre-Scientific Era

**−9,800 – −750.** Accumulation of empirical knowledge without a theoretical base: observations were recorded but not systematized into general principles.

| Achievement | Time | Significance |
|-------------|------|--------------|
| Calendar | −8,000 | 14 months × ~29.5 days |
| Astronomy | −7,000 | Prediction of eclipses |
| Medicine | −6,000 | Herbal treatment |
| Mathematics | −5,500 | Six-base system |
| Geometry | −4,000 | Land surveying |

The outstanding achievements of this period — **six-base mathematics** (Sha-Ruun, −5,500) and **atomic theory** (Ven-Tal, −5,200), laid down in the Era of Great Discoveries of the First Empire. The first circumnavigation (Gharra-Vex, −4,200) proved the sphericity of Théxar — the first major theoretical conclusion confirmed by practice (→ see [Volume I, Ch.28, "History of Exploration"]).

---

## 10.2 The Era of Discovery (−750 – +1,000)

Systematic application of the scientific method: from observation to experiment and theory. Science takes shape as an institution, separated from religion.

Great scientists:

| Name | Years | Discovery |
|------|-------|-----------|
| Vel-Shar | −500 – −450 | Magnetism, compass |
| Khal-Ven | +80 – +120 | Circumference of Théxar |
| Tor-Mor | +220 – +260 | Height of the atmosphere (85 km) |
| Ven-Khal | +380 – +420 | Bathymetry of Thalas |

In this era the first scientific societies were created (predecessors of the Scholars Guild), and the Academy of Khar-Vexar became a center of systematic knowledge.

---

## 10.3 The Industrial-Thermonuclear Revolution (−1,200 – +2,000)

| Year | Discovery | Scientist/Engineer |
|------|-----------|--------------------|
| −1,200 | Steam engine | Thal-Ven |
| +1,200 | Fusion (first demonstration) | Khal-Shar |
| +1,420 | First industrial fusion reactor | — |
| +1,500 | Bio-optical processor | Ven-Mor |
| +1,800 | Quantum communication | Ru-Khal |

Two revolutions in this era defined modernity. The **industrial** (steam engine, −1,200) made machine energy accessible; the **thermonuclear** (+1,420) made it practically inexhaustible. Together they turned the economy from an economy of scarcity into an economy of abundance (→ see [Volume II, Ch.09, "Economic History"]).

## 10.4 Modern Science

By 4,028 E.U.:

- 240 research institutes;
- 8.4 million scientists (of all three species);
- R&D spending: 7.2% of GDP;
- 12 hydrothermal vent fields under permanent observation.

Modern science is organized on the guild principle: the Scholars Guild coordinates the research program, the Keepers Guild — archives and canon compliance. Science remains open: results are published in a unified access system, and the most important discoveries undergo collegial review.

---

## 10.5 Science and Ethics

The history of Xha'Ruun science is marked by the conscious restriction of dangerous directions:

- **Restriction of biological research** — the legacy of the "Red Rot" pandemic (−2,120 – −2,105);
- **Ethics of sapience** — research on the minds of all three species is subject to strict regulation (→ see [Volume II, Ch.14, "The Three Species"]);
- **Limited AI** — artificial intelligence is created without self-awareness, within the Law of Accord.

These restrictions are not a prohibition of knowledge but an institutional framework developed in response to historical experience.

## Chapter Summary

1. Science traveled from empirical knowledge to post-scarcity technology in ~15,000 years.
2. Pre-scientific era: calendar, astronomy, six-base mathematics, the −4,200 circumnavigation.
3. Era of discovery (−750 – +1,000): scientific method, first societies and academies.
4. Industrial-thermonuclear revolution: steam engine (−1,200), fusion (+1,420), quantum communication (+1,800).
5. Modern science: 240 institutes, 8.4 million scientists, 7.2% of GDP on R&D.
6. Ethics of science: restrictions on biological research and self-aware AI.
