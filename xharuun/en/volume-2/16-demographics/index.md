## Introduction

The demographic history of the Xha'Ruun is the story of how a small founder population became a civilization of billions, survived a catastrophic decline, and developed conscious control over growth. Each era left its demographic profile, and the dynamics of numbers directly reflect the state of society — from the Empire's flourishing to crisis and stabilization.

---

## 16.1 Population Growth

Below is the population of the **Xha'ri** (the main land species) by era:

| Period | Population | Growth rate | Event |
|--------|-----------|-------------|-------|
| −15,000 | 8,000 | — | First Xha'ri |
| −9,800 | 200,000 | 0.04% | Writing |
| −6,200 | 2.5 million | 0.06% | First Empire |
| −2,150 | 18 million | 0.08% | Empire peak (Kharak) |
| −750 | 4 million | −0.12% | After the crisis (Kharak) |
| +850 | 400 million | 0.15% | Council of Unity |
| +4,028 | 2.8 billion | 0.30% | The present |

> The table shows the **Xha'ri** population. Including Kel'vash and Moryn, the total planetary population ≈ 4.8 billion (see §16.2).

Key patterns:

- **First Empire peak** (−2,150): ~18 million (Kharak's population) — the limit at which the agricultural resources of the Central Plain were exhausted;
- **Crisis** (−750): decline to ~4 million — a demographic collapse following the ecological one;
- **Recovery and the Era of Unity**: growth accelerates with each technological transition (industrialization −1,200, fusion +1,420, post-scarcity +2,800).

Demographic policy considers all three species: the Growth Commandment applies to total population, and the Council monitors the balance (→ see [Volume II, Ch.14, "The Three Species"]).

## 16.3 The Growth Commandment

The modern demographic regime is regulated by the **Growth Commandment**: population growth must not exceed **0.3% per year**. The mechanism of compliance is cultural norm and economic planning, not coercion:

- Family policy encourages stability rather than maximizing births;
- Education and medicine make family planning accessible;
- The post-scarcity economy removes economic incentives for large families.

The Growth Commandment is a direct response to the ecological crisis of the First Empire (→ see [Volume II, Ch.04, "The Era of Crisis"]): the civilization consciously limits numbers so as not to repeat the mistake of exhausting the resource base.
