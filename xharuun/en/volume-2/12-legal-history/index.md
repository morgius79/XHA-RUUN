## Introduction

The legal history of the Xha'Ruun is the history of turning custom into codified law, and then into a constitutional system regulating relations between three sapient species. Each era left a legal layer built into the subsequent ones: the principles of the Imperial Code outlived the Empire, the treaty law of the Renaissance became the basis of the Council Charter, and modern law has absorbed all that preceded it.

---

## 12.1 Stages of Legal Development

| Era | System | Basis |
|-----|--------|-------|
| Pre-state | Customary law | Precedents, traditions |
| First Empire | Imperial Code | Codification |
| Dark ages | Regional law | Divergent systems |
| Renaissance | Treaty law | Unity Treaty |
| Era of Unity | Constitutional law | Council Charter |

Each transition was a response to the crisis of the previous system: codification — to the uncertainty of custom, treaty law — to fragmentation, constitutional law — to the complexity of an intercontinental society.

---

## 12.2 Customary Law

In the pre-state era law was custom: norms were transmitted orally, applied by precedent, and interpreted by the elders of the kin. Key features:

- **Collective responsibility** — the community answered for the actions of its members;
- **Restitution, not vengeance** — harm was compensated, blood feud was restrained;
- **Sacral status** — violations of norms were seen as an insult to the spirits.

The Code of Khar (≈−7,200, 40 articles) became the first written body fixing these customs (→ see [Volume II, Ch.02, "Early Civilization"]).

---

## 12.3 The Imperial Code

Created by Verksaren I (−6,150). **48 articles**, of which **42** survive.

Key principles:

- **Equal for equal** — restitution, not vengeance;
- **Community responsibility** — collective suretyship;
- **Inviolability of envoys** and merchants;
- **The right of a slave to buy freedom**;
- **Freedom of slaves' children**;
- **Protection of the farmer** from the arbitrariness of tax collectors.

The Imperial Code is the first empire-wide legal act, spreading a unified norm across all Kharak. Its principles outlived the Empire: after its fall (−2,150) the surviving copies became the core of the legal tradition of subsequent eras (→ see [Volume II, Ch.03, "The First Empire"]).

---

## 12.4 Legal Decline in the Dark Ages

After the Empire's fall, unified law broke apart into regional systems. The loss of the Imperial Code for 600 years (preserved only in the Irkhen Library) led to a return to customary law and arbitrary local norms. This period entrenched in the culture the value of the **written law** as a guarantee against arbitrariness.

---

## 12.5 Treaty Law of the Renaissance

The Unity Treaty (−750) created a fundamentally new legal construction — a **treaty between equals**: eight regions voluntarily limited their autonomy in exchange for common guarantees. Key legal innovations:

- **Free trade** as a treaty norm;
- **A unified system of measures and weights**;
- **Renunciation of wars** between participants;
- **The Interregional Council** — the first supranational legal body.

---

## 12.6 Modern Law: The Council Charter

Based on the **Charter of the Council of Unity** (+850, with amendments):

| Principle | Significance |
|-----------|--------------|
| Equality of all species | Xha'ri, Kel'vash, Moryn — equal before the law |
| Freedom of information | Access to any fact of the canon |
| Inviolability of history | Prohibition of rewriting |
| Rational stewardship | The Law of Silence |

Modern law is supplemented by the **Law of Accord**: no decision affecting all species may be made without consultation of Xha'ri, Kel'vash, and Moryn (→ see [Volume II, Ch.14, "The Three Species"]). Justice is administered on the basis of the codified canon, whose interpretation is controlled by the Keeper of Knowledge.

## Chapter Summary

1. Law passed through five stages: customary → Imperial Code → regional → treaty → constitutional.
2. The Imperial Code (48 articles, 42 preserved) — the first empire-wide body of law.
3. The loss and restoration of the Code in the dark ages entrenched the value of the written law.
4. The Unity Treaty (−750) created the construction of a treaty between equals.
5. The Council Charter (+850): equality of species, freedom of information, inviolability of history.
6. The Law of Accord — mandatory consultation of the three species on common decisions.
