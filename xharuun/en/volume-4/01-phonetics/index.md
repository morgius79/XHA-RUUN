## Introduction

Phonetics is the fundamental layer of the Xha'Ruun language. The sound inventory (24 consonants, 8 vowels) reflects the physiology of its speakers: three pairs of vocal cords make it possible to produce tones and overtones inaccessible to speakers of other languages. This chapter describes the phoneme inventory and the features of their articulation.

The consonant inventory is symmetric by voicelessness/voicing and includes characteristic sounds: the dental fricative θ (as in the word "xha"), the glottal stop ʔ (a syllable separator), and the velar nasal ŋ.

### 1.1.2 Vowels (8)

| | Front | Central | Back |
|--|-------|---------|------|
| High | i iː | | u uː |
| Mid | e | ə | o |
| Low | | a | |

The eight vowels form three series and three heights; length is phonemic (i/iː, u/uː). The neutral vowel ə plays an epenthetic role — a filler for forbidden clusters.

## 1.2 Articulatory Features

Xha'Ruun articulation has several features related to physiology:

- **Tones** — three pairs of vocal cords allow the production of up to five distinguishing tones;
- **Overtones** — the vocal apparatus is capable of producing two tones simultaneously (used in music and religion);
- **Uvular x** — a "guttural" fricative, characteristic of the language;
- **Glottal stop ʔ** — a morpheme separator that prevents vowel contraction.

---

## 1.3 Syllable Structure

The Xha'Ruun syllable is predominantly open (CV). Allowed structures:

- CV — basic;
- CVC — with a final sonorant (m, n, ŋ, r, l);
- CCV — with an initial cluster (p, b, k, g + sonorant).

Forbidden clusters are resolved by epenthesis of ə. Stress is tonic, falling on a long vowel or the first syllable.

## 1.4 Sound and Writing

The phonetic inventory is directly linked to the writing system (→ see [Volume IV, Ch.07, "Sharuu Writing System"]): the syllabary reflects the syllable structure, and tones are conveyed by diacritics.
