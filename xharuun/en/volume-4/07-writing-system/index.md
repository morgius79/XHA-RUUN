## Introduction

The Sharuu writing system is one of the oldest continuously used writing systems (almost 10 000 years). Its mixed character — syllabary + logograms — reflects the history of the development of writing (→ see [Volume II, Ch.02, "Early Civilization"]). This chapter describes the type, the structure of graphemes, and the rules of notation.

---

## 7.1 Script Type

Mixed: syllabary (124 signs) + logograms (48 signs).

| Component | Count | Function |
|-----------|-------|----------|
| Syllabic signs (V, CV, CCV) | 124 | Phonetic notation |
| Logograms | 48 | Semantic roots |
| Numerals (senary) | 6 | 0–5 |
| Punctuation | 8 | Separation |

The syllabary covers the syllabic structure of the language; logograms encode semantic roots (often polysemantic). The numerals are senary — a legacy of senary mathematics (→ see [Volume I, Ch.34, "Measurement Systems"]).

---

## 7.2 Direction

Right to left, top to bottom. Paragraphs are arranged in columns.

The direction of writing reflects physiology and history: the oldest texts were carved on stone stelae from top to bottom and right to left — the direction of the hand in carving.

---

## 7.3 Graphemes

Modular grid 16×16. 64 base graphemes (G-0001–G-0064), combining into syllabic signs.

The system is built hierarchically:

- **64 base graphemes** — the simplest elements (strokes, arcs, dots);
- **Syllabic signs** — combinations of 2–4 base graphemes;
- **Logograms** — stylized compositions of syllabic signs.

The modular grid ensures machine recognition and calligraphic flexibility.

---

## 7.4 Phonetic Writing and Tones

Tones (→ see [Volume IV, Ch.01, "Phonetics"]) are conveyed by diacritics above the syllabic signs. The system of tonal diacritics — 5 distinctive tones.

---

## 7.5 Writing and Society

- **Literacy 100%** — writing is mandatory in education;
- **Sacred texts** — the canon is written in classical Sharuu;
- **Double notation** — logograms + phonetic transcription for unambiguity.
