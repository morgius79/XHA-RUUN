## Key Ideas

1. **Precision** — 14 cases and mandatory evidentiality allow expressing an exact picture of the world: who speaks, where they know from, how it is connected (→ see [Volume IV, Ch.04, "14 Cases"], → see [Volume IV, Ch.06, "Evidentiality"]).
2. **Agglutination** — the lexicon grows not by borrowing but by combining roots; ~500 roots generate 50 000+ words (→ see [Volume IV, Ch.08, "Lexicon and Word Formation"]).
3. **Unity in diversity** — a single standard and five mutually intelligible dialects; the language unites without unifying (→ see [Volume IV, Ch.10, "Dialects"]).
4. **Writing as memory** — Sharuu (124 syllabic signs + 48 logograms) preserved the language through centuries of crises (→ see [Volume IV, Ch.07, "Sharuu Writing System"]).
5. **Six as a constant** — the senary system was reflected in mathematics, the calendar, and measurements (→ see [Volume IV, Ch.09, "Senary Numeral System"]).

## Language and History

The modern language is the result of the processing of historical experience (→ see [Volume IV, Ch.11, "History of the Language"]). Imperial standardization laid the foundations of the grammar; the crisis gave rise to dialectal diversity; the Unity Treaty returned the norm. The language survived all political catastrophes precisely because it was written down: writing became a "repository" that survived where institutions collapsed (→ see [Volume II, Ch.04, "The Era of Crisis"]).
