## Introduction

Phonology — the rules of organizing sounds into words and phrases. If phonetics describes the inventory, then phonology describes the regularities of its use: syllable structure, stress, vowel harmony, assimilation.

---

## 2.1 Phonotactics

Syllable structure: (C)(C)V(C)(C)

| Position | Possible Sounds |
|----------|----------------|
| C₁ (initial) | Any consonant |
| C₂ (initial) | r, l, w, j |
| V (nucleus) | Any vowel |
| C₃ (final) | Any consonant |
| C₄ (final) | s, ʃ, t, d, n |

The constraint on C₂ (only sonorants r, l, w, j) and on C₄ (only s, ʃ, t, d, n) are the key rules of phonotactics. Forbidden combinations are resolved by epenthesis of the neutral ə.

---

## 2.2 Stress

Dynamic, on the first syllable of the root. Non-phonemic (does not change the meaning of a word).

Stress serves as a word boundary and does not depend on morphological complexity: when suffixes are added it remains on the root, which facilitates recognition of the lexical core.

---

## 2.3 Vowel Harmony

Assimilation by backness: suffixes adapt to the vowel of the root.

Harmony is the most important morphophonological process: case and derivational suffixes have variants (front/back vowels) selected according to the vowel of the root. Example: the locative suffix -ven/-van is selected by the backness of the root vowel.

---

## 2.4 Assimilation

Assimilation of consonants:

- **By voicing** — voicing/devoicing at junctions;
- **By place** — assimilation of n → ŋ before velars;
- **Progressive** — in suffixes after voiceless consonants.

These processes motivate the allomorphy of morphemes (→ see [Volume IV, Ch.04, "14 Cases"]).

---

## 2.5 Tone and Prosody

The tonal system (up to five tones, → see [Volume IV, Ch.01, "Phonetics"]) plays a prosodic role: it distinguishes grammatical forms (for example, evidentiality, → see [Volume IV, Ch.06, "Evidentiality"]) and marks phrase boundaries.
