## Introduction

The survey chapter of Volume I established the fundamental parameters of the flora of Théxar: chromophyll with an absorption peak at 570 nm, ~380,000 described species, dominance of the division Chromophyta (→ see [Volume I, Ch.17, "The Flora of Théxar"]). This chapter deepens that description: it examines the anatomy and physiology of the planet's plants, unique life strategies, sixfold growth cycles, and specialized photosynthetic adaptations.

Three circumstances make the flora of Théxar fundamentally different from a terrestrial-type flora. The first is **chromophyll photosynthesis** under the orange spectrum of the star Khar'Vex, which set the characteristic "living silver" color of the vegetation. The second is **silicon biomineralization**, pervading the whole flora: from the cell walls of mosses to the bark of centennial trees. The third is **sixfold periodicity**: most plants of Théxar pass through six phases of vegetation per year and add six annual rings, inheriting the senarity of the biosphere (→ see [Volume I, Ch.20, "The Origin of Life"]).

## 9.1 The Photosynthetic Apparatus

### 9.1.1 The Antenna Complex

The pigment system of the plants of Théxar is built around chromophyll, but is not limited to it. The photosynthetic efficiency of 4.2% is achieved by the work of a coordinated antenna complex of five pigments (→ see [Volume I, Ch.17, "The Flora of Théxar"]).

| Pigment | Color | Absorption peak | Localization in the leaf | Role |
|---------|-------|-----------------|--------------------------|------|
| Chromophyll a | Copper-red | 570 nm | Reaction center | Primary, photosystem I |
| Chromophyll b | Golden | 540 nm | Light-harvesting complexes | Auxiliary, photosystem II |
| Theta-carotene | Orange | 480 nm | Periphery | Photoprotection |
| Ther xanthophyll | Yellow | 450 nm | Inner antenna | Heat dissipation, Ther cycle |
| Seriflavin | Purple | 620 nm | Lower side of the leaf | Capture of reflected light |

### 9.1.2 The Double-Sided Leaf

The typical Chromophyta leaf has an asymmetric pigment structure: the upper side is saturated with chromophyll a/b (works on the direct light of Khar'Vex), the lower with **seriflavin**, a purple pigment that captures light reflected from the soil and neighboring leaves. In the understory of the jungles up to 23% of a leaf's photosynthesis is provided by upward light that has passed through the canopy and been scattered in the lower tiers.

### 9.1.3 The Ther Cycle

The heat-resistant variant of the xanthophyll cycle — the **Ther cycle** — protects the reaction centers from overheating at peak illumination. With an excess of energy, Ther xanthophyll reversibly converts into theroflavin, which dissipates 78% of the excess energy as heat in 2–4 minutes. Thanks to this cycle, the plants of Théxar endure brief illuminances up to 1,100 W/m² without damage — even though photosynthesis saturates already at 420 W/m².

## 9.2 The Vascular System and Silicon

### 9.2.1 Silicon Reinforcement

The uniqueness of the flora of Théxar is the ubiquitous deposition of amorphous silica (SiO₂) in the cell walls of the conducting elements.

| Division | SiO₂ content in dry mass | Tissue hardness (Mohs) | Water transport |
|----------|--------------------------|------------------------|-----------------|
| Chromophyta | 2–9% | 2.5–4.0 | Vessels + tracheids |
| Silophyta | 8–35% | 3.5–5.5 | Tracheids only |
| Thexophyta (epiphytes) | 3–7% | 2.0–3.0 | Moisture capture by bark |
| Thalophyta (algae) | 4–18% | — | Osmotic |
| Bryoforma | 5–22% | — | Capillary |

Silicon performs four functions: structural (rigidity), protective (against herbivores), radiative (UV shielding), and hydraulic (reduction of cavitation in vessels under water stress). The silicon ring around the vascular bundles prevents the collapse of a vessel at negative water pressure — an adaptation that allowed Chromophyta to reach heights of 100+ sha.

### 9.2.2 Concentrating Metabolism

More than 60% of the vascular plant species of Théxar use concentrating metabolism: CO₂ is actively pumped into the bundle-sheath cells, where chromophyll works at a substrate concentration 6–8 times higher than atmospheric.

| Parameter | C₃-like | Concentrating |
|-----------|---------|---------------|
| Share of species | ~40% | ~60% |
| Photorespiration (losses) | 22–30% | 8–12% |
| Transpiration (rel.) | 100 | 75 |
| Photosynthetic efficiency | 3.0–3.6% | 4.2–4.9% |
| Ecological optimum | Humid forests, coasts | Dry savannas, steppes, highlands |

## 9.4 Life Strategies

### 9.4.1 The Six-Phase Annual Cycle

Most terrestrial plants of Théxar are synchronized with the planet's calendar: 412.7 local days, 14 months of ~29.5 days, and 5 days of Renewal (→ see [Volume I, Ch.34, "Systems of Measurement"]). A typical Chromophyta year consists of **six phases**:

| Phase | Dates | Event |
|-------|-------|-------|
| I. Awakening | months 1–2 (Khar-Ven, Serrin-Tash) | Bud opening, root growth |
| II. Vegetation | months 3–4 (Thalas-Mor, Ven-Khal) | Maximum shoot growth |
| III. Flowering | months 5–6 (Rass-Ur, Sha-En) | Reproductive structures |
| IV. Fruiting | months 7–8 (Khar-Tor, The-Nur) | Ripening of fruits and seeds |
| V. Shedding | months 9–10 (Gharra-Vek, Mor-Irk) | Leaf fall, storage of substances |
| VI. Dormancy | months 11–14 (Thar-Ghal — Veks-An) | Deep dormancy, 5 days of Renewal |

### 9.4.2 Six-Layer Annual Rings

Chromophyta wood grows **six rings per year** — one for each phase of vegetation (→ see [Volume I, Ch.11, "Ecology"]).

| Layer | Phase | Density | Color | Information for dendrochronology |
|-------|-------|---------|-------|----------------------------------|
| 1 | Awakening | Low | Light | Length of the dry season |
| 2 | Vegetation (early) | High | Dark | Abundance of precipitation |
| 3 | Vegetation (late) | Medium | Medium | Temperature |
| 4 | Flowering | High | Dark | Wind regime |
| 5 | Fruiting | Low | Light | Reserve of nutrients |
| 6 | Dormancy | Minimal | Almost white | — |

From the six-layer rings of the **Mor-Ven** (Tree of Time), the Keepers of the Archives reconstruct the climate of Théxar with a resolution of one vegetation phase. Continuous series reach 38,000 cycles — the longest climatic reconstruction on the planet.

### 9.4.3 Ephemeral Strategies of the Deserts

The annuals of the Shan Desert (genus **Ash-Grass**) complete their full life cycle within 6 weeks after rain. Their seeds contain a germination inhibitor that breaks down only after a second wetting at an interval of no less than 11 days — protection against "false starts" during rare isolated showers.

## 9.5 Unique Forms

### 9.5.1 Gethers — Moving Plants

The greatest sensation of the botany of Théxar remains the subclass **Ghett-Chroma** ("walking chromophytes") — 14 species of ephemeral grasses of the dry plains of Rhuval, capable of limited active movement.

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| Speed | Up to 0.9 mo per local day |
| Mechanism | Directed drying-swelling of the root "foot" |
| Migration range | Up to 240 sha in a lifetime |
| Reason for movement | Following local rains |
| Population | ~2.4 billion individuals (seasonal peak) |

The movement is slow: a full "step" takes 6–9 minutes, and a movement visible to the eye requires 10–14 days of observation. The mechanics are based on the asymmetric swelling of the peripheral roots: the side facing the moistened soil elongates, dragging the plant by fractions of a sha per swelling cycle. The storage "foot" of 18–24 root lobes holds the plant in place during drought and rolls it — in essence, walks it — during the wet season.

### 9.5.2 Guy-Wire Lianas

The great lianas of the jungles (genus **Rhu-Koss**, up to 61 sha) climb the trunks of Tor-Khar without winding around them: their shoots grow vertically along "guy-wires" — their own taut fibers anchored in the host's bark. At a height of 30–60 sha the liana opens a crown that intercepts light that has not reached the lower tiers. Efficiency: a single Rhu-Koss plant intercepts up to 4.1% of the light falling on a patch of canopy, with a biomass 800 times smaller than that of the supporting tree.

### 9.5.3 H₂S-Filtering Epiphytes

The division **Thexophyta** consists of epiphytes specialized in absorbing hydrogen sulfide directly from the air (→ see [Volume VII, Ch.13, "Biosphere Interactions"]).

**Xha-Ven** ("the rational fern") is an epiphyte whose aerial roots form a cushion filter up to 2.4 sha across. In one cycle a single plant binds 3–6 ru of sulfur, reducing the H₂S concentration in the ground layer of the jungle within a radius of up to 40 sha by 18–26%. In the lower tiers of the jungle, where illumination is low, part of the metabolic needs is covered by the energy of H₂S oxidation — an auxiliary chemotrophic circuit duplicating photosynthesis.

### 9.6.2 Driving Forces

Three main factors in the evolution of the flora of Théxar:

1. **Spectral pressure** — the orange maximum of Khar'Vex (570 nm) selected pigments covering precisely that region; the quantum efficiency of 0.92 is close to the theoretical limit.
2. **Water deficit** in the dry zones under a generally warm climate — stimulated the concentrating metabolism (60% of species) and the CAM-like strategies of the Silophyta.
3. **Silicon availability** — the excess of dissolved SiO₂ in soils and ocean (a consequence of the elevated metallicity of the crust, → see [Volume I, Ch.12, "Geological Evolution"]) made silicon biomineralization evolutionarily cheap.

## Chapter Summary

1. The photosynthetic apparatus of the plants of Théxar is built on five pigments around chromophyll (570 nm); the double-sided leaf captures both incident and reflected light, and the Ther cycle protects against overheating.
2. Silicon biomineralization pervades the whole flora (SiO₂ from 2% to 35% of dry mass), providing rigidity, protection, UV shielding, and vessel resistance to cavitation.
3. The flora numbers 380,000 described species; 6 subclasses of Chromophyta (120,000 species) dominate the land, and Silophyta is a unique silicon division of the dry zones.
4. The annual cycle of most plants is six-phase, wood grows six rings per year, giving climatic reconstructions with a resolution of one vegetation phase (series up to 38,000 cycles).
5. Among the unique forms are "walking" gethers (up to 0.9 mo/day), guy-wire lianas, and H₂S-filtering epiphytes binding up to 3–6 ru of sulfur per cycle.
6. The evolution of the flora is driven by the spectral pressure of Khar'Vex, water deficit, and silicon availability; plant extinctions ceased since +2,100 Y.E.
