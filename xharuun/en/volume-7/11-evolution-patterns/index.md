## Introduction

Evolution on Théxar proceeds within the same fundamental principles as on any other planet with carbon life: heredity, variation, selection. However, the specific patterns — speed, direction, constraints — are set by the specifics of the local biochemistry and environment. The six-letter genetic code, the opposite chirality, elevated radiation resistance, and a sulfur-rich biosphere created an evolutionary trajectory with no direct analogues.

This chapter systematizes the evolutionary patterns of Théxar: rates, adaptive zones, key innovations, and constraints.

## 11.1 Rates of Evolution

### 11.1.1 The Rate of Speciation

Measurements by molecular clocks and the paleontological record show that the net rate of speciation on Théxar is **30–40% lower** than on Earth, at a comparable extinction rate.

| Parameter | Théxar | Earth (reference) |
|-----------|--------|-------------------|
| Speciation rate (species/million cycles per 1,000 extant) | 3.2 | 4.5–5.1 |
| Background extinction rate | 2.8 | 2.9–3.4 |
| S/E ratio | 1.14 | 1.3–1.5 |
| Time to fixation of a mutation in a population (thousand cycles) | 84 | 62 |
| Average number of genes per species | ~34,000 | ~22,000 |

The reason for the slower speciation is the **elevated error-resistance of the six-letter code**. The redundancy of coding (9× against 3× in the terrestrial code) reduces the probability that a mutation will lead to a significant change in a protein. Beneficial mutations accumulate more slowly; harmful ones are more often neutralized.

### 11.1.2 Phases of Acceleration

Against this background stand out periods of accelerated evolution, connected with:

1. **Liberation of niches** after mass extinctions (→ see [Volume VII, Ch.12]);
2. **The appearance of key innovations** (thermochromatism, hexapodalism);
3. **Horizontal gene transfer** between unrelated lineages.

| Period | Duration | Cause | Speciation rate |
|--------|----------|-------|-----------------|
| Radiation of the Thermochromadae | 20 million cycles (350–330 million Y.A.) | New adaptive zone | 8.4× background |
| Post-impact radiation | 12 million cycles (250–238 million Y.A.) | Liberation of niches | 11.2× background |
| Dispersal of the Moryn | 3 million cycles (120–117 million Y.A.) | New environment (depth) | 6.8× background |
| Era of the Rebirth (anthropogenic) | 600 cycles (−750 – −150) | Domestication selection | 2.4× background |

## 11.2 Adaptive Zones

Three factors determined the structure of the adaptive zones of Théxar: the orange spectrum of the star, the global sulfur cycle, and tectonic activity.

### 11.2.1 The Spectral Adaptive Zone

The emission peak of Khar'Vex (570 nm) created a zone in which selection favored pigments absorbing precisely that range. Chromophyll occupied this niche ~3.0 billion cycles ago and holds it to this day. Alternative pigments (phycobilins) have been pushed into peripheral environments: deep waters (Thalophyta) and caves.

### 11.2.2 The Sulfur Adaptive Zone

H₂S is not a toxin but a resource. On Théxar there exists a full-fledged adaptive zone of chemosynthesis, parallel to the photosynthetic one. It covers:

- Hydrothermal fields and seeps;
- Cave ecosystems (→ see [Volume VII, Ch.05]);
- Subglacial lakes (→ see [Volume VII, Ch.04]);
- The guts of symbiotic organisms;
- Soils with high sulfur content.

In this zone selection favored not vision but chemoreception and electroreception; not movement but sedentary habits; not metabolic speed but its efficiency at low temperatures.

### 11.2.3 The Tectonic Adaptive Zone

The active plate tectonics of Théxar creates a dynamic landscape: mountains rise, continents drift, oceans change shape. This gives rise to a zone of "variable environment," where selection favors generalized strategies (omnivory, mobility) over specialized ones.

## 11.3 Key Evolutionary Innovations

### 11.3.1 Hexapodalism (Six Limbs)

Its appearance ~410 million cycles ago in the ancestors of the Thermochromadae. Advantage: division of functions between limb pairs without loss of stability.

| Function | Lower pair | Middle pair | Upper pair |
|----------|------------|-------------|------------|
| Locomotion | Primary | Auxiliary | — |
| Manipulation | — | Primary | Auxiliary |
| Thermoregulation | — | — | Primary (IR sensors, thermochrom) |
| Courtship displays | — | Auxiliary | Primary |

Hexapodalism became established and spread to the 24,000 species of the class, but did not penetrate other large taxa. Reason: the "adaptation trap" — once an organism specializes two additional limbs, it becomes dependent on them and cannot return to tetrapodalism without loss of fitness.

### 11.3.2 Thermochromatism

The ability of the skin to change color and surface temperature depending on the environment and the internal state. It appeared ~150 million cycles ago. Functions:

- Camouflage (slow changes, hours);
- Thermoregulation (medium, minutes);
- Communication (fast, seconds).

Thermochromatism is an example of **exaptation**: having originally developed as thermoregulation (reducing overheating under the orange sun), the ability was co-opted for a signaling function, and then for social communication in the Xha'Ruun (→ see [Volume VII, Ch.08, "The Biology of the Xha'Ruun"]).

### 11.3.3 Silicon-Organic Membranes

Appearance — the endosymbiotic absorption of a silicon bacterium ~1.4 billion cycles ago (→ see [Volume VII, Ch.10, "Symbiosis and Coevolution"]). Advantage: resistance to:

- High temperatures (up to 85°X without denaturation);
- High pressure (up to 1,000 tyazh);
- Radiation (4× higher than the standard lipid membrane).

It spread to all eukaryote-like forms. The cost: the rate of ion transport through the membrane is reduced by ~18% — a compromise between resilience and efficiency.

### 11.3.4 The Double Heart

Two hearts (thoracic and abdominal) — an innovation that appeared in the ancestors of the Thermochromadae ~340 million cycles ago. Advantage: independent regulation of pressure in the respiratory and systemic circulation. It made possible:

- Prolonged apnea (up to 40 minutes in deep-water forms);
- Separate thermoregulation of the upper and lower body;
- Survival with damage to one heart (the second compensates by 40–60%).

## 11.4 Evolutionary Constraints

### 11.4.1 Chiral Isolation

D-amino acids and L-sugars make the biosphere of Théxar biochemically incompatible with the terrestrial one (→ see [Volume I, Ch.04, "Biochemistry"]). This is not a constraint on evolution as such, but it means:

- The absence of a common basis for any possible interplanetary exchange of biomass;
- The impossibility of direct use of terrestrial agricultural crops without a complete biochemical rearrangement;
- The total impossibility of infection by terrestrial pathogens, and vice versa.

### 11.4.2 The Radiation Threshold

Although the radiation resistance of the biosphere of Théxar is high, it has a limit. At doses above 14 Gy (3× the lethal dose for terrestrial organisms, but 2× below the peak of the UV flux of early Théxar) even the six-letter code does not provide protection. This limited the colonization of the highlands until the appearance of silicon-organic membranes.

### 11.4.3 Sulfur Dependence

The high integration of the sulfur cycle into metabolism means that the organisms of Théxar cannot simply be "transplanted" into an environment without H₂S. For survival in artificial environments (space stations, colonies) either a constant supply of sulfur or an expensive genetic modification is required.

### 11.4.4 The Genetic "Ceiling of Complexity"

The six-letter code provides high stability, but the price is a **longer genome** and **more complex regulation**. Genome replication time is 1.6× longer than the terrestrial; embryonic development lasts longer; the number of possible regulatory links grows exponentially. This creates a "ceiling" on the speed of adaptation to rapid environmental changes.

## 11.5 Directions of Evolution

### 11.5.1 Progress vs. Reduction

On Théxar no linear "progress" from simple to complex is observed. The most successful groups are those that occupied stable niches and reduced the "superfluous":

| Group | Direction of evolution | Result |
|-------|------------------------|--------|
| Tubeworms of the hydrothermal vents | Reduction of the digestive system, strengthening of symbiosis | Total dependence on symbionts, survival in extremes |
| Moryn | Reduction of vision, strengthening of bioluminescence | A civilization without sunlight |
| Kel'vash | Reduction of metabolic rate, strengthening of longevity | "Geological" thinking, life of 800–1,200 cycles |
| Thexophyta | Reduction of roots, strengthening of epiphytism | Total independence from soil |

### 11.5.2 Convergence

Convergent evolution on Théxar is widespread:

- **Silicon membranes** appeared independently in the Moryn, the Kel'vash, and deep abyssal forms — three times;
- **Electroreception** — in the Moryn, the Thal-shak, and the Xha'Ruun (rudimentary) — independently;
- **Thermochromatism** — in the terrestrial Thermochromadae and deep-water fish-analogues (for communication in darkness) — independently.

This suggests that the number of "solutions" to adaptive tasks is limited, and biospheres with similar physico-chemical parameters will generate similar forms.

### 11.5.3 Adaptive Radiation of the Thermochromadae

The class Thermochromadae is the brightest example of adaptive radiation on Théxar: 24,000 species from 0.2 sha to 10 sha, from pure predators to pure herbivores, from terrestrial to fully aquatic. The reason for the success is the combination of three innovations (hexapodalism, the double heart, thermochromatism), which gave an "evolutionary key" to many niches at once (→ see [Volume VII, Ch.02, "Thermochromadae"]).

## 11.6 Horizontal Gene Transfer

On Théxar horizontal gene transfer (HGT) is more widespread than on Earth, because of the six-letter code and the elevated density of virus-like elements.

| HGT mechanism | Frequency (events/million cycles) | Main participants |
|---------------|-----------------------------------|-------------------|
| Virus-mediated | 1.2 × 10⁻⁴ | All groups |
| Symbiotic (endosymbiosis) | 2.8 × 10⁻⁵ | Eukaryotes + bacteria |
| Direct DNA uptake | 6.4 × 10⁻⁶ | Prokaryotes |

HGT accelerates adaptation to new conditions, allowing "jumps" past intermediate stages. However, it also creates a risk: about 14% of species extinctions in the last 100 million cycles are attributed to the "malignization" of HGT — when a population receives not an adaptive but a lethal genetic element.

## 11.7 Social Evolution

In the three sentient species of Théxar sociality arose independently:

| Species | Time of appearance | Preconditions |
|---------|--------------------|---------------|
| Xha'Ruun | ~15,000 years ago | Clan structure, division of sexes, cooperative hunting |
| Kel'vash | ~40,000 years ago | Resonance consensus, geological thinking |
| Moryn | ~120,000 years ago | Collectivist "transparent" ethics, bioluminescent communication |

A common feature: sociality in all three species is based on the **division of cognitive functions** between individuals, not on simple aggregation. The Xha'Ruun divide knowledge and skills by castes; the Kel'vash by time (long-term vs. short-term); the Moryn by "light roles" in the group dance-speech.

## 11.8 The Future of Evolution

Predicting evolution is a task with high uncertainty, but some trends are traceable:

1. **Further integration of symbionts**: membrane symbionts may completely lose their residual genome, becoming organelles of a new type.
2. **Cultural selection**: in sentient species natural selection is increasingly replaced by cultural selection — the choice of a partner by social, not biological, criteria.
3. **Technological adaptation**: instead of biological evolution, the Xha'Ruun, the Kel'vash, and the Moryn use technological modifications (gene therapy, bioengineering).
4. **Possible fusion**: the "single organism of Théxar" hypothesis predicts that the three sentient species may ultimately form a single symbiotic system — but this is a planning horizon of tens of thousands of years.

## Chapter Summary

1. The rate of speciation on Théxar is 30–40% lower than on Earth because of the error-resistance of the six-letter code; periods of acceleration are connected with the liberation of niches and key innovations.
2. Three key adaptive zones: spectral (the orange light of Khar'Vex), sulfur (H₂S chemosynthesis), tectonic (a dynamic landscape).
3. Four key innovations: hexapodalism (~410 million Y.A.), thermochromatism (~150 million Y.A.), silicon-organic membranes (~1.4 billion Y.A.), the double heart (~340 million Y.A.).
4. Evolutionary constraints: chiral isolation, the radiation threshold, sulfur dependence, the "ceiling of complexity" of the genome.
5. The directions of evolution are nonlinear: reduction and simplification are as successful as complication; convergence is widespread (silicon membranes — 3 independent appearances).
6. Horizontal gene transfer is more active than on Earth; sociality arose independently in the three sentient species.
