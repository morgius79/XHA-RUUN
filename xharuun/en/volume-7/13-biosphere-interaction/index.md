## Introduction

The biosphere of Théxar is not simply "life on a planet," but a **planetary system** linking the atmosphere, hydrosphere, lithosphere, and cryosphere into a single metabolic cycle. Organisms do not adapt to the environment — they create it. The atmosphere of Théxar (22.6% O₂, 0.8% CO₂, 0.0008% H₂S) is a product of life, not its precondition. The climate of the planet is regulated not only by the star and albedo, but also by forest transpiration, the ocean's emission of dimethyl sulfide, and the deposition of carbonates by reefs.

Three biogeochemical cycles — **the carbon, nitrogen, and sulfur cycles** — form the framework of interactions. Their interweaving on Théxar is more complex than on Earth: the six-letter code elevated the role of sulfur, and silicon biomineralization added a fourth cycle — the silicon one, almost absent on Earth in a biologically active form.

This chapter examines the biosphere as a **single organism** — a hypothesis that on Théxar is called not a hypothesis but an observation.

### 13.1.3 The Biological Carbon Pump

The oceanic carbon pump of Théxar is based on three mechanisms:

1. **The carbonate pump** — reefs and plankton build skeletons of silicon-calcium composite (→ see [Volume VII, Ch.03, "Marine Ecosystems"]), binding CO₂; the skeletons settle to the floor;
2. **The organic pump** — dead biomass and fecal pellets descend into the depths, carrying carbon;
3. **Vertical migration** — zooplankton feeds at the surface at night, metabolizes in the depths by day, releasing CO₂ into the deep waters.

Efficiency: the ocean of Théxar carries ~8.4 Gt C/cycle from the surface layer into the depths — 18% higher than the terrestrial figure, thanks to the silicon skeletons (heavier, they sink faster).

### 13.1.4 Climate Regulation

Atmospheric CO₂ (0.8%, 2,840 Gt C) provides a greenhouse effect of +31°C. This is a **higher** concentration than the pre-industrial terrestrial one (0.028%), but necessary: the orange star Khar'Vex gives Théxar 15% less radiation than Earth receives from the Sun (→ see [Volume I, Ch.03, "The Planet Théxar"]).

Negative feedback: if CO₂ rises → temperature rises → silicate weathering intensifies → CO₂ is bound into carbonates → the concentration falls. Response time: 100,000–500,000 cycles.

## 13.2 The Nitrogen Cycle

Nitrogen (N₂) makes up 71.8% of the atmosphere of Théxar, but is biologically accessible only in bound form (ammonia, nitrates).

### 13.2.1 Nitrogen Reservoirs

| Reservoir | Stock (Gt N) | Form | Turnover time |
|-----------|--------------|------|---------------|
| Atmosphere | 3,840,000 | N₂ (gas) | — |
| Biomass | 18 | Proteins, DNA | 8 cycles |
| Soils | 240 | NH₃, NO₃⁻, organics | 42 cycles |
| Ocean (dissolved) | 620 | NO₃⁻, NH₄⁺ | 2,400 cycles |
| Sedimentary rocks | 42,000 | Organic compounds | 240 million cycles |

### 13.2.2 Biological Fixation

Nitrogen fixation — the conversion of N₂ into a bioavailable form — is carried out by **diazotrophs** (nitrogen-fixing bacteria and cyano-like forms).

| Mechanism | Fixation (Gt N/cycle) | Main agents |
|-----------|----------------------|-------------|
| Symbiosis with legumes | 52 | Mor-Bean and analogues |
| Free-living diazotrophs (soil) | 34 | Soil bacteria |
| Cyano-like (ocean) | 28 | Marine phytoplankton |
| Lightning | 6 | Abiogenic fixation |
| Anthropogenic (fertilizers) | 42 | Industrial synthesis |
| **Total** | **162** | — |

Anthropogenic fixation exceeds the natural one in soils (42 vs 34 Gt N/cycle) — a consequence of intensive agriculture. This does **not** cause an ecological crisis, unlike in the Crisis Era, thanks to strict control of runoff.

### 13.2.3 Denitrification

Closes the cycle: bound nitrogen returns to the atmosphere as N₂ or N₂O. The main process is **anaerobic respiration** of denitrifying bacteria in soils and sediments.

| Environment | Denitrification (Gt N/cycle) |
|-------------|-----------------------------|
| Soils (anaerobic zones) | 68 |
| Oceanic sediments | 54 |
| Freshwater sediments | 12 |
| Oxygen-minimum zones (ocean) | 24 |
| **Total** | **158** |

Balance: 162 (fixation) − 158 (denitrification) = **+4 Gt N/cycle** — a small accumulation of bound nitrogen in the biosphere.

### 13.3.3 Biogenic Emission of H₂S

The 0.0008% H₂S in the atmosphere is the **biomarker of Théxar**, visible from space. Sources:

| Source | Emission (million ru S/cycle) |
|---------|-------------------------------|
| Oceanic phytoplankton (DMS → H₂S) | 18 |
| Decomposition of organics in swamps | 12 |
| Thexophyta epiphytes (H₂S oxidation + re-emission) | 8 |
| Hydrothermal fields (direct release) | 4 |
| Volcanism | 4 |
| **Total** | **46** |

DMS (dimethyl sulfide) — a volatile compound emitted by marine phytoplankton — is oxidized in the atmosphere to SO₂ and H₂SO₄, forming aerosol condensation nuclei. This intensifies cloudiness over the ocean by 4–7%, raising the albedo and cooling the planet. **Biological climate regulation.**

### 13.3.4 Chemosynthesis as a Dominant

Chemosynthetic carbon fixation through H₂S oxidation:

- Ocean: 340 million ru S/cycle → 8.4 Gt C/cycle (23% of marine production);
- Land (soils, caves): 52 million ru S/cycle → 1.2 Gt C/cycle (1.4% of terrestrial).

In total, chemosynthesis provides **8%** of the planet's primary production — 40 times the terrestrial figure.

## 13.4 The Silicon Cycle

The silicon cycle on Théxar is **biologically active**, which is practically absent on Earth (→ see [Volume VII, Ch.09, "The Plant Kingdom"]).

### 13.4.1 Silicon Reservoirs

| Reservoir | Stock (Gt Si) | Form | Turnover time |
|-----------|---------------|------|---------------|
| Ocean (dissolved) | 28 | H₄SiO₄ | 18,000 cycles |
| Biomass (diatoms, plants) | 0.84 | Amorphous SiO₂ | 4 cycles |
| Soils | 840,000 | Silicates, silica | 340,000 cycles |
| Earth's crust | 240,000,000,000 | Silicates, quartz | — |

### 13.4.2 Biological Mobilization

Silicon biomineralization covers:

- **Silodiatoma** (silica-flagellates) — 24% of marine phytoplankton;
- **Silophyta** — 45,000 species of terrestrial silicon plants;
- **Mycorrhizal fungi** — dissolve and transport SiO₂ to the roots;
- **Reef polyps** — silicon-calcium skeletons;
- **Sponge-analogues** — silicon spicules.

Turnover: the biota extracts **12 Gt Si/cycle** from the ocean, deposits it in skeletons that, after death, settle to the floor and dissolve again. The biological silicon pump accelerates the silicon cycle **14-fold** relative to the abiotic one.

### 13.4.3 Climate Feedback

Silicate weathering (reaction with CO₂) is a long-term climate regulator:

CaSiO₃ + CO₂ → CaCO₃ + SiO₂

The biological acceleration of weathering (plant roots, mycorrhiza) intensifies CO₂ binding. With warming, plants grow faster → more roots → more weathering → CO₂ falls → cooling. Feedback with a response time of 100,000 cycles.

## 13.5 Interactions of the Cycles

### 13.5.1 The C-N-S-Si System

The four cycles are not independent — they are interwoven through biological processes:

| Link | Process | Scale |
|------|---------|-------|
| C ↔ N | Amino acids, DNA | Stoichiometry C:N = 6.6:1 in biomass |
| C ↔ S | Sulfur-containing amino acids | C:S = 200:1 |
| N ↔ S | Sulfate reduction + ammonification | Linked pathways in anaerobic zones |
| Si ↔ C | Photosynthesis of diatoms | Si limits production in 18% of the waters |

### 13.5.2 The Redfield Ratio of Théxar

The average composition of plankton biomass: **C₁₀₆N₁₆P₁S₀.₅Si₁₅**

Differences from Earth: more sulfur (0.5 against 0.1), more silicon (15 against 0).

### 13.5.3 Limiting Elements

| Environment | Limiting element | Consequence |
|-------------|-----------------|-------------|
| Open ocean (gyre centers) | Phosphorus (P) | Low production |
| Coastal ocean | Nitrogen (N) | Eutrophication when in excess |
| Silicate upwellings | Iron (Fe) | "Rusty" zones |
| Temperate forests | Nitrogen (N) | Slow growth |
| Deserts | Water + phosphorus | Minimal production |

## 13.6 The Biosphere and the Atmosphere

### 13.6.1 Composition of the Atmosphere

| Gas | Concentration | Source | Sink |
|-----|---------------|--------|------|
| N₂ | 71.8% | Volcanism (primary) | Fixation ↔ denitrification (balance) |
| O₂ | 22.6% | **Photosynthesis** | Respiration, rock oxidation |
| Ar | 2.9% | Decay of ⁴⁰K | — |
| CO₂ | 0.8% | Respiration, volcanism | **Photosynthesis** |
| H₂O | 1.2% (average) | Evaporation | Precipitation |
| H₂S | 0.0008% | **Biogenic emission** | Oxidation, deposition |

> **KEY FACT**
>
> Without life the atmosphere of Théxar would have the composition: N₂ 95%, CO₂ 4%, Ar 1%, O₂ <0.001%. The current composition (22.6% O₂, 0.8% CO₂, 0.0008% H₂S) is entirely a product of the biosphere. This is a **thermodynamically non-equilibrium system** maintained by life.

### 13.6.2 Regulation of Oxygen

The O₂ concentration (22.6%) is higher than on Earth (21%). Reason: higher photosynthetic efficiency (4.2% against 3%), but the same rate of respiration. However, the rise of O₂ is limited by the risk of **fires** — at 25% spontaneous combustion becomes frequent.

Feedback: if O₂ rises → fires become more frequent → biomass burns → production falls → O₂ falls. Stabilization at 22.6%.

### 13.6.3 Greenhouse Gases

| Gas | Contribution to the greenhouse effect (+31°C) |
|-----|-----------------------------------------------|
| H₂O | 54% |
| CO₂ | 38% |
| CH₄ | 6% |
| H₂S | 2% |

CH₄ (0.0003%) is biogenic, from anaerobic decomposition in swamps and the guts of herbivores. Lifetime in the atmosphere: 8.4 cycles (oxidized to CO₂).

## 13.8 Feedbacks and Homeostasis

### 13.8.1 The Living Planet Hypothesis

The aggregate of biospheric feedbacks creates **planetary homeostasis** — the ability of a system to return to equilibrium after disturbances.

| Disturbance | Feedback | Response time |
|-------------|----------|---------------|
| Rise of CO₂ | Intensification of photosynthesis → absorption of CO₂ | 10–100 cycles |
| Fall of O₂ | Fewer fires → growth of biomass → more O₂ | 100–1,000 cycles |
| Warming | More DMS → more clouds → cooling | 1–10 cycles |
| Cooling | Less weathering → growth of CO₂ → warming | 10,000–100,000 cycles |

### 13.8.2 The Limits of Homeostasis

Homeostasis is not absolute. The five mass extinctions (→ see [Volume VII, Ch.12]) show: under a sufficiently strong disturbance the system **switches** into a new state. Examples:

- The Oxygen Catastrophe: transition from an anaerobic to an aerobic biosphere;
- Total glaciation: transition into a "snowball" (reversible only thanks to volcanism).

## Chapter Summary

1. The biosphere of Théxar is a planetary system linking the four biogeochemical cycles (C, N, S, Si) with the atmosphere, hydrosphere, and lithosphere in a single metabolic circuit.
2. The carbon cycle regulates the climate (CO₂ = 0.8%, greenhouse effect +31°C); the oceanic carbon pump carries 8.4 Gt C/cycle into the depths, 18% more efficiently than the terrestrial.
3. The sulfur cycle is unique: H₂S (0.0008% of the atmosphere) is the planet's biomarker; chemosynthesis provides 8% of primary production (40× the terrestrial figure).
4. The silicon cycle is biologically active: the biota extracts 12 Gt Si/cycle, accelerating the cycle 14-fold; silicon biomineralization covers 45,000 plant species and all marine plankton.
5. The atmosphere (22.6% O₂, 0.8% CO₂) is entirely a product of the biosphere; without life the planet would have 95% N₂, 4% CO₂, and traces of O₂.
6. Planetary homeostasis is maintained by feedbacks (response times 1–100,000 cycles), but has limits — the five mass extinctions show switches into new states.
