## Introduction

Xha'Ruun infrastructure is the least visible and the most important of the technologies: it distributes energy, water, information, and food so that their absence becomes an event. In the post-industrial era the main lines of civilization are not visible to the eye: energy flows through thermonuclear circuits, information through the Xha-Ven mycelium (→ see [Volume VI, Ch.03]), movement through resonance corridors (→ see [Volume VI, Ch.02]). This chapter describes the six networks — energy, water, information, transport, waste, and settlements — and how they form a single organism of the planet.

## 10.1 The Energy Network

### 10.1.1 Generation

| Source | Share | Power (GW) | Note |
|--------|-------|------------|------|
| Thermonuclear stations (Th + He-3) | 68% | 1,560 | Primary source |
| Solar (including orbital) | 17% | 390 | Equatorial belt |
| Geothermal | 9% | 210 | Raal plateau, Rhuval nests |
| Hydro and tidal | 4% | 92 | Rivers of Kharak, straits |
| Biological (photosynthesis) | 2% | 46 | Urban facades, farms |
| **Total** | **100%** | **~2,300** | Kardashev ≈ 0.78 |

The civilization consumes ~2,300 GW — a level of ~0.78 on the Kardashev scale (→ see [Volume I, Ch.09, "Technologies"], → see [Volume VI, Ch.01, "Technologies of Xha'Ruun"]). The base load is carried by 214 thermonuclear stations placed along the thorium provinces of Kharak (52% of reserves — → see [Volume I, Ch.19, "Natural Resources"]) and at the poles, where helium-3 is extracted.

### 10.1.2 Transmission

| System | Range | Losses | Status |
|--------|-------|--------|--------|
| Resonance circuits | Up to 12,000 km | ~3% | Primary |
| Cryogenic lines | Up to 1,000 km | ~1% | Auxiliary |
| Local bio-circuits | Up to 100 km | ~0% | Urban |

Energy is transmitted not by wires but by **resonance circuits** — standing waves in the gravitational-electromagnetic lattice of the planet. A station "pumps" the circuit, a consumer "draws" energy like a subscriber to a water main. The losses of the circuit (~3%) are a fundamental limit connected with dissipation in rock and ocean.

### 10.1.3 Storage

|-------|----------|--------------|-------------|

| Living photosynthetics | Seasonal | Months | Agricultural sector |

## 10.2 The Water Network

The hydrosphere of Théxar (67% of the surface, → see [Volume I, Ch.03, §4.4]) is distributed unevenly: the arid interior regions of Vexar and the south of Rhuval suffer from a shortage of fresh water. The water infrastructure solves three tasks: distribution, purification, and conservation.

| System | Length | Volume | Note |
|--------|--------|--------|------|
| Living conduits | 640,000 km | 9 billion m³/year | Mycelial channels |
| Desalination colonies | — | 1.2 billion m³/year | Coasts, hybrid with bio-filters |
| Underground reservoirs | — | 240 billion m³ | Nest-warehouses |
| Atmospheric condensers | — | 0.6 billion m³/year | Shan Desert, highlands |

**Living conduits** are channels whose walls are grown from Thar-Vex and populated with filtering organisms (class 5, → see [Volume VI, Ch.07, "Materials Science"]). Water in them undergoes biological purification en route: from source to consumer it moves and is purified simultaneously.

**Water policy.** The right to water is vested in every living being regardless of species; the water network is managed not by a market but by distribution guilds within the post-scarcity economy (→ see [Volume III, Ch.03, "Economic System"]).

## 10.3 The Information Network

The Xha-Ven network (→ see [Volume VI, Ch.03]) is a planetary mycelium 500–2,000 m deep, covering 100% of the land and 60% of the ocean. Infrastructurally it consists of three tiers:

| Tier | Composition | Function |
|------|-------------|----------|
| Underground | ~12 billion km of hyphae, Rass-Mor nodes | Trunk |
| Surface | Relays, urban colonies | Distribution |
| Orbital | Crystal reflectors (12,000 satellites) | Communication with space |

The **bandwidth** of the network is formally limited by the speed of light in the mycelium, but practically is unlimited: encoding uses states of quantum entanglement, and subscribers receive data "instantly" (→ see [Volume VI, Ch.03, §3.1]). The infrastructural task is not speed but **addressing**: to whom the stream belongs, who has the right to read. Cryptographic control of entangled states forms the core of network security (→ see [Volume VI, Ch.03, §3.2]).

**Memory infrastructure.** Knowledge is stored in Ven-Khal crystals; the repository of the Guild of Keepers (→ see [Volume III, Ch.04, "The 12 Guilds"]) holds 4×10²⁹ TB — the complete body of knowledge of the three species, distributed across 12,400 vaults, including a duplicate archive on the satellite Thel.

## 10.4 The Transport Network

### 10.4.1 Surface

| System | Speed | Length | Note |
|--------|-------|--------|------|
| Resonance corridors (GR) | Instant "step" up to 8,000 km | 212 nodes | Only within the magnetosphere |
| Maglev lines | 650 km/h | 1,900,000 km | Freight, local service |
| Bio-carriers | 120 km/h | Routes | Rural areas |
| Waterways | 60 km/h | 450,000 km | Rivers, canals |

GR corridors (→ see [Volume VI, Ch.02, §2.1]) are not built like roads: they are resonance nodes "tuned" to the planet's frequency. Movement between nodes requires synchronization and takes fractions of a second, but the nodes must lie within the magnetosphere and the network of resonance lattices. Outside the corridors, classic maglev lines operate, requiring no resonance infrastructure.

### 10.4.2 Space

| System | Status |
|---------|--------|
| Orbital elevator (1 operating, equator) | Height 38,200 km |
| Second elevator (construction since +3,000 Y.E.) | Unfinished |
| Orbital forest (24 stations) | Permanently inhabited |
| Xha-Vokh (living ships) | Interplanetary routes |

Space infrastructure is described in Ch.06 (→ see [Volume VI, Ch.06, "Space: Symbiotic Expansion"]); from the standpoint of infrastructure it is important that orbital systems and the surface network form a single circuit: energy from orbital solar stations is transmitted to the surface by resonance circuits, information through reflectors, freight through the elevator.

## 10.5 The Waste Network and the Closed Cycle

The return standard (≥96% of material mass — → see [Volume VI, Ch.07, §7.8]) requires recycling infrastructure on a planetary scale:

| Stream | Volume | Recycling |
|--------|--------|-----------|
| Industrial waste | 420 million t/year | 96.2% |
| Biological waste | 890 million t/year | 100% (composting) |
| Spent building materials | 210 million t/year | 96.4% |
| Atmospheric emissions | 0.6 million t/year | Scrubber colonies |
| Space debris | 0.04 million t/year | Orbital "shepherds" |

**Orbital "shepherds"** are specialized Xha-Vokh of young age that collect spent satellites and return them to the atmosphere or to recycling. Thanks to their work the orbital environment remains clean, which is critical for the elevator and the stations.

The closure of the cycle is not ecological fashion but a historical imperative: the civilization survived the ecological collapse of the First Empire (−2,150 Y.E.) and has no intention of repeating it (→ see [Volume II, Ch.04, "The Era of Crisis"]).

Xha'Ruun cities are designed around six infrastructural "roots": the energy circuit, the water conduit, the Xha-Ven node, the GR node, the feeding farm, and the resonator. The layout of a city reproduces the structure of the clan nest on the scale of a metropolis (→ see [Volume VI, Ch.09, §9.3]).

## 10.7 Life Support of Settlements

A modern settlement is autonomous in supply for 90–120 days (covering the crisis period of any network) and maintains its own habitat:

| System | Coverage | Reserve |
|--------|----------|---------|
| Food | 100% through local farms | 90 days |
| Water | 100% | 120 days |
| Energy | 100% (resonance circuit + local generation) | 90 days |
| Information | 100% (Xha-Ven) | Local Ven-Khal crystals |
| Medicine | Hospital + 6 field colonies | — |

Autonomy is not an end in itself: settlements are designed for mutual support. The Xha'Ruun network principle is **redundancy through diversity**: not one giant system but six independent ones, deliberately weakly connected, so that a failure of one does not paralyze the rest. This is a direct legacy of the lesson of the First Empire, which collapsed together with its centralized infrastructure (→ see [Volume II, Ch.04]).

## 10.8 Management of Infrastructure

### 10.8.1 Institutions

| Guild | Area of Responsibility |
|-------|------------------------|
| Engineers | Energy, transport, construction |
| Keepers | Information, archives, memory network |
| Agriculturists | Water, food, bio-network |
| Medics | Sanitary systems, environmental hygiene |
| Navigators | GR corridors, space logistics |
| Miners | Raw-material flows |

The guilds (→ see [Volume III, Ch.04, "The 12 Guilds: Structure"]) do not compete for resources — they are coordinated through the Council of Unity (→ see [Volume III, Ch.02, "The Council of Unity"]), which distributes the budget within the post-scarcity economy (→ see [Volume III, Ch.03]).

### 10.8.2 Planning

Infrastructure planning is conducted with a horizon of up to 500 years — a scale unseen in other civilizations, made possible by the The-Ven computer (→ see [Volume VI, Ch.04, "Living Computation"]), which models the needs of the biosphere and society centuries ahead. The five-hundred-year plans are revised every 36 years — the senary planning cycle (→ see [Volume IV, Ch.09, "The Senary Numeral System"]).

**Critical assessment.** Critics point to two risks: slowed adaptation (a 500-year plan cannot keep up with crises) and "infrastructural ossification" (a network designed for the needs of the 24th century may hinder the 28th). Defenders answer that the senary revision and the redundancy principle leave enough freedom.

## 10.9 The Limits of Infrastructure

| Limit | Nature | Consequence |
|-------|--------|-------------|
| Speed of light | Fundamental | Space is beyond instantaneous communication |
| Magnetosphere | Physical | GR corridors are bounded by the planet |
| Living material | Biological | No network in vacuum and at T > 1,400 °C |
| Scale of the biosphere | Ecological | The carrying capacity of Théxar is bounded |
| Ban on strong AI | Ethical | The network is managed by biological nodes, not "thinking machines" |

Xha'Ruun infrastructure is perfect on the scale of a single planet and powerless beyond it: light limits space, the magnetosphere limits instantaneous transport, life limits materials. These limits are not revisited — they are accepted as fundamental (→ see [Volume I, Ch.09, "Technologies"]).

## Chapter Summary

1. Energy is based on 214 thermonuclear stations (68% of generation, ~2,300 GW, Kardashev ≈ 0.78); transmission is by resonance circuits with ~3% losses.
2. Water is distributed by "living conduits" that purify the water en route; the right to water is vested in all three species.
3. The Xha-Ven information network has three tiers (mycelium, surface, orbit); 12,400 Ven-Khal vaults hold the complete knowledge of the civilization.
4. The waste network is closed to ≥96% thanks to return standards and orbital "shepherds" that collect space debris.
5. Infrastructure is planned 500 years ahead through the The-Ven computer and built on the principle of "redundancy through diversity" — a legacy of the lesson of the fall of the First Empire.
