## Introduction

Xha'Ruun materials science is a borderline discipline: it stands exactly between geology and biology and belongs fully to neither. The wealth of Théxar's interior (iron enrichment factor 2.8, thorium 2.5, platinum 2.2 — → see [Volume I, Ch.19, "Natural Resources"]) gave the civilization an abundant raw-material base, and the principle of symbiogenesis (→ see [Volume VI, Ch.01, "Technologies of Xha'Ruun"]) determined what to do with that base: not to cast and forge, but to **grow**. A modern Xha'Ruun material is in most cases not manufactured but bred — like a plant cultivar or an animal breed. This chapter describes the six classes of materials, their production, testing, and limits of applicability.

---

## 7.1 The Six Classes of Matter

The classification of materials adopted by the Guild of Engineers in +2,480 Y.E. is senary — like everything in the Xha'Ruun measuring culture (→ see [Volume IV, Ch.09, "The Senary Numeral System"], → see [Volume I, Ch.34, "Systems of Measurement"]). The six classes divide into 36 families, and the families into 216 standard grades.

| No. | Class | Name in Xha'Ruun | Base | Method of Production |
|-----|-------|------------------|------|----------------------|
| 1 | Osseous | **Thar-Vex** | Silicon-organic | Growing |
| 2 | Crystalline | **Ven-Khal** | Silicon carbide | Growing from a melt |
| 3 | Metallic | **Tor-Khar** | Fe, Ti, Pt, REE | Smelting, alloying |
| 4 | Carbon | **Xa-Thar** | Nanotubes, graphene | Catalytic synthesis |
| 5 | Living | **Xa-Vex** | Cell colonies | Cultivation |
| 6 | Composite | **Rass-Thar** | Combinations of 1–5 | Joint growth |

The class names are formed by the standard model of two-part roots (→ see [Volume IV, Ch.08, "Lexicon and Word Formation"]): *thar* — strength, *ven* — truth/knowledge, *tor* — mountain, *xa* — life, *rass* — unity.

**The fundamental difference from terrestrial classification:** terrestrial materials science divides substances by chemical nature (metals, ceramics, polymers). The Xha'Ruun classification divides them by **relation to life** — from entirely dead metals (class 3) to entirely living colonies (class 5). Composite materials (class 6) are the summit of the hierarchy precisely because they unite the dead and the living.

## 7.2 Class 1: Osseous Materials (Thar-Vex)

The main construction material of the civilization. A silicon-organic polymer grown by specialized builder organisms on the principle of mineralization of a biological matrix — the same path by which shells and carapaces form on Théxar (→ see [Volume VII, Ch.09, "The Plant Kingdom"]).

| Grade | Density (g/cm³) | Tensile Strength (MPa) | Growth Time | Application |
|-------|-----------------|------------------------|-------------|-------------|
| Thar-Vex-1 | 1.32 | 420 | 6 days | Household goods |
| Thar-Vex-6 | 1.85 | 1,240 | 36 days | Residential structures |
| Thar-Vex-12 | 2.40 | 2,850 | 108 days | Bridges, supports |
| Thar-Vex-24 | 3.14 | 6,100 | 216 days | Orbital elevator |
| Thar-Vex-30 | 3.60 | 8,400 | 432 days | Xha-Vokh exoskeleton |

**The key property — self-healing.** The material retains a thin layer of living cells along the grain boundaries. When a crack appears, the cells migrate to the break and build up the matrix. A crack up to 2 dlan wide (0.24 m) heals over in 14–40 days at normal temperature and with sufficient nutrition.

**Feeding the material.** The living layer requires a solution of silicon salts and trace amounts of H₂S — the same hydrogen sulfide that serves as the biochemical marker of life on Théxar (→ see [Volume I, Ch.04, "Biochemistry of Life"]). Structures are threaded with a system of feeding channels, ~4% of the mass by volume. When feeding stops, the material "falls asleep": it remains strong but loses its ability to repair itself.

**Limitations:**
- Does not work below −60 °C (the cells die);
- Degrades under constant irradiation above 40 Gy/year;
- Unsuitable for contact with melts;
- Growth cannot be accelerated: doubling the rate reduces strength fourfold.

## 7.3 Class 2: Crystalline Materials (Ven-Khal)

Silicon-carbide crystals grown from a melt under a strictly controlled temperature gradient. Known above all as an information carrier (→ see [Volume VI, Ch.03, "Xha-Ven — The Biospheric Network"]), but in materials science they are valued for other properties.

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| Hardness (Guild scale) | 6 of 6 |
| Failure temperature | 2,730 °C |
| Thermal conductivity | 490 W/(m·K) |
| Transparency | 0.2–14 μm |
| Radiation resistance | Practically absolute |
| Service life | >10⁹ years |

**Three applications beyond memory:**

1. **Optical windows** — Xha-Vokh portholes, resonator lenses, light guides of bio-colonies (→ see [Volume VI, Ch.04, "Living Computation"]).
2. **Heat removal** — crystal plates draw heat from the plasma circuits of thermonuclear stations (→ see [Volume VI, Ch.01]).
3. **Resonators** — a crystal with a given set of lattice defects has a strictly defined natural frequency; on this rests all resonance technology, from GR-corridors to temple acoustics (→ see [Volume VI, Ch.02, "Quasi-Transport and Resonance Networks"], → see [Volume V, Ch.02, "Architecture"]).

**Production.** The largest crystal farms are located on the Raal plateau (Rhuval) — the same place where 48% of the planet's platinum reserves are concentrated, used as a growth catalyst (→ see [Volume I, Ch.19]). A fist-sized crystal (~1 dm³) grows for 12 years.

## 7.4 Class 3: Metallic Materials (Tor-Khar)

The only class fully inherited from the pre-industrial and industrial eras. Xha'Ruun metallurgy is older than writing: the first copper production dates to −9,200 Y.E. (→ see [Volume II, Ch.02, "Early Civilization"]).

| Metal / alloy | Share of Consumption | Main Source | Application |
|---------------|----------------------|-------------|-------------|
| Iron and steels | 46% | Kharak (45% of reserves) | Industrial tooling |
| Titanium and alloys | 18% | Rhuval, Vexar | Implants, light frames |
| Copper | 11% | Kharak, southern coast | Electrical engineering |
| Platinoids | 4% | Raal plateau | Catalysts |
| REE | 9% | Vexar (45% of reserves) | Resonance technology |
| Other | 12% | — | — |

**The paradox of metals.** Although metallurgy remains developed, the share of metals in structures is steadily falling: from 74% in +1,000 Y.E. to 19% in +4,028 Y.E. Metal is considered a "dishonest" material — it does not age visibly, it breaks suddenly, and it gives no signs of fatigue. Xha'Ruun engineering culture values materials that **warn** about their condition.

**Where metal is indispensable:**
- The inner circuits of thermonuclear reactors (temperatures above the Thar-Vex limit);
- Tools and tooling for processing other materials;
- Conductors in the few systems where electric current is retained;
- Ritual and artistic objects (→ see [Volume V, Ch.01, "Art"]).

## 7.5 Class 4: Carbon Materials (Xa-Thar)

Nanotubes and graphene structures — the only field where Xha'Ruun technology approaches what terrestrial science would consider the "normal" path of development (→ see [Volume I, Ch.09, "Technologies"]).

| Material | Tensile Strength | Feature |
|----------|------------------|---------|
| Single-wall nanotube | 130 GPa | The limit for the class |
| Nanotube bundle | 42 GPa | Industrial grade |
| Graphene membrane | 125 GPa | 1 atom thick |
| Carbon-silicon bundle | 68 GPa | Compatible with Thar-Vex |

Carbon materials are almost never used in pure form — they serve as the **reinforcing phase** in class-6 composites. The exception: the cable elements of the orbital elevator, where the carbon-silicon bundle works in a pair with the self-healing Thar-Vex-24 sheath (→ see [Volume VI, Ch.02, §2.3]).

## 7.6 Class 5: Living Materials (Xa-Vex)

Materials that remain fully functional organisms for their entire service life. The boundary between "material" and "creature" is conditional here and is the subject of constant ethical disputes (→ see [Volume V, Ch.05, "Philosophy"]).

| Material | What It Is | Lifespan | Application |
|----------|------------|----------|-------------|
| Bio-membrane | Filter film | 40 years | Sealing stations |
| Feeding mycelium | Fungal network | Unlimited | Thar-Vex channels |
| Light-film | Photosynthetic | 80 years | Energy collection, lighting |
| Thermo-skin | Heliotherm | 120 years | Building thermoregulation |
| Precipitator | Chemoautotroph | 25 years | Air and water purification |

**The bio-membrane** deserves a separate mention: one-way gas permeability makes possible orbital stations that are non-hermetic in the terrestrial sense (→ see [Volume VI, Ch.06, §6.2]). The membrane lets molecules in and does not let them out through active transport powered by the film's own metabolism.

**The ethical restriction.** The code of the Guild of Engineers prohibits using organisms with nervous tissue as materials, as well as any forms capable of learning. The boundary is drawn by the criterion of memory: what remembers cannot be a thing.

## 7.7 Class 6: Composites (Rass-Thar)

An Xha'Ruun composite is not assembled from ready components. It is **grown jointly**: the reinforcing phase (carbon, class 4) grows through the matrix (silicon-organic, class 1) in a single process, and the living layer (class 5) forms along the interfaces.

| Composite | Composition | Feature | Application |
|-----------|-------------|---------|-------------|
| Rass-Thar-A | Thar-Vex + nanotubes | Strength 12 GPa | Load-bearing structures |
| Rass-Thar-B | Thar-Vex + Ven-Khal | Optical transparency | Windows, light guides |
| Rass-Thar-C | Thar-Vex + Ti | Biocompatibility | Implants, prostheses |
| Rass-Thar-D | Mycelium + graphene | Signal conductivity | Xha-Ven nodes |
| Rass-Thar-E | All of the above | "Full composite" | Xha-Vokh hull |
| Rass-Thar-F | Kel'vash silicon + Thar-Vex | Heat resistance 1,400 °C | Reactor shells |

**Rass-Thar-F — an inter-species material.** Developed jointly with the Kel'vash after their entry into the Council of Unity in +1,200 Y.E. (→ see [Volume I, Ch.38, "The Sentient Species Kel'vash"], → see [Volume II, Ch.14, "History of the Three Species"]). The Kel'vash can form silicon-organic structures with their own metabolism at temperatures lethal to carbon-based life; in the production of Rass-Thar-F their participation is not technological but **biological** — the material is in the literal sense secreted by living Kel'vash masters. The questions of payment and voluntariness of this work are regulated by a separate section of the Treaty of the Three Species (→ see [Volume I, Ch.40, "The Treaty of the Three Species"]).

## 7.8 Testing and Standards

The Xha'Ruun testing culture rests on the concept of **honesty of material** — the ability to signal approaching failure.

| Test | What Is Measured | Standard |
|------|------------------|----------|
| Strength | Failure limit | By grade |
| Fatigue | Cycles to failure | ≥ 6×10⁶ |
| Recovery | Crack healing rate | ≥ 6 dlan/day |
| Honesty | Advance warning time | ≥ 36 days |
| Resistance | Degradation under radiation | ≤ 6% over 36 years |
| Return | Share returned to the cycle | ≥ 96% |

**The honesty test** is unique to Xha'Ruun and has no terrestrial analogue. A sample is loaded to 90% of the limit and the observer records how long before failure the material changes color, smell, or natural frequency. A material that fails without warning is not admitted to structures where living beings are present — regardless of strength. It is precisely this standard, not technical impossibility, that drove steel out of construction (→ see [Volume VI, Ch.09, "Construction"]).

**The return test** checks the closure of the cycle: at least 96% of the mass of a decommissioned material must return to production or the biosphere without residue. The standard was introduced in +1,640 Y.E. as a direct consequence of the memory of the ecological collapse that destroyed the First Empire in −2,150 Y.E. (→ see [Volume II, Ch.04, "The Era of Crisis"], → see [Volume I, Ch.11, "Ecology"]).

**The turning point — +1,812 Y.E.**, when a workshop of the Guild of Engineers in Khar-Vexar first grew a load-bearing beam instead of casting it. The event is conventionally regarded as the beginning of modern materials science; its anniversary is observed as a professional holiday of the guild (→ see [Volume V, Ch.11, "Holidays"]).

## 7.10 The Limits of the Possible

Xha'Ruun materials science runs up against several hard limits recognized as insurmountable:

- **The strength limit of matter.** The carbon nanotube (130 GPa) is the theoretical maximum for chemical bonds. There will be no stronger materials.
- **The temperature limit of the living.** No living material works above 1,400 °C; reactor circuits remain the domain of dead substances.
- **The growth-rate limit.** Growing cannot be accelerated without loss of quality; a large structure requires years, and this is a fundamental constraint on planning (→ see [Volume VI, Ch.09, "Construction"]).
- **The absence of negative-mass metamaterials.** A direct consequence of the ban on anti-gravity (→ see [Volume I, Ch.09, "Technologies"]).
- **Vacuum.** Living materials require at least traces of atmosphere; in open space only the dead or dormant phase works (→ see [Volume VI, Ch.06]).

## Chapter Summary

1. Xha'Ruun materials divide into six classes — from dead metals to fully living colonies; the classification is built on the criterion of "relation to life," not chemical nature.
2. The main construction material is the silicon-organic Thar-Vex, grown and self-healing; a crack up to 0.24 m heals over in 14–40 days.
3. The metals that dominated until +1,800 Y.E. were displaced not by the technical superiority of composites but by the "honesty of material" standard — the requirement to warn of failure in advance.
4. The Rass-Thar-F composite is the first material produced jointly by two sentient species: it is secreted by living Kel'vash.
5. The return standard (≥96% of mass into the cycle) was introduced in +1,640 Y.E. as a response to the memory of the ecological collapse of the First Empire.
