## Introduction

The cuisine of the Xha'Ruun is one of the most complete projections of the species' physiology. Six limbs determine the number of table utensils and the rhythm of cooking; the ability to distinguish the salts of heavy metals and the need for sulfur in the diet determined a flavor palette unfamiliar to carbon-based gourmets of other worlds; pheromone communication built mandatory "scent" elements into the meal, while the evidentiality of the language (→ see [Volume IV, Ch.06]) turned the description of food into a separate rhetorical genre. Gastronomy on Théxar is not only the satisfaction of a need but also an institution: the kitchen is one of the zones where knowledge (→ see [Volume V, Ch.07, "Science in Culture"]) is transmitted orally, through "culinary apprenticeship," and where the history of civilization is reproduced on the plate.

## 9.1 Physiology of Nourishment

The features of Xha'Ruun digestion (→ see [Volume I, Ch.05, "The Xha'Ruun Species"]) determine the basic framework of the cuisine.

| Parameter | Value | Culinary Consequence |
|-----------|-------|----------------------|
| Diet type | Omnivorous, protein-plant diet | Abundance of combined dishes |
| Additional taste receptor | For heavy-metal salts | "Metallic taste" — a separate category |
| Sulfur requirement | Mandatory dietary component | Sulfur-containing products are indispensable |
| Metabolism | Endothermic with heliotherapy | Warm food valued in winter, light food in heat |
| Heat treatment | Raw foods rare | Fermentation and boiling are the basic methods |
| Water balance | High requirement | Soups and drinks are a structural part of the meal |

**The key fact of physiology**: the mandatory presence of sulfur in the diet (a consequence of the role of hydrogen sulfide in the planet's metabolism, → see [Volume I, Ch.04, "Biochemistry of Life"]). Table salt (NaCl) is used but less popular than **selenium salt** (Se-salt) and sulfur condiments. A dish without a sulfur component is perceived as "unfinished" — similar to how, in other cultures, a dish without salt is considered tasteless.

## 9.2 Staple Products

### 9.2.1 Grains

| Grain | Description | Use |
|-------|-------------|-----|
| **Thar-rice** | High-protein grain, the staple cereal | Base for side dishes, porridges, "thar-bread" |
| **Khar-millet** | Small golden grain with chromophilic coloring | Porridges, drinks |
| **Ven-ear** | Cold-resistant cereal of the Polar Domain | Flour for "polar bread" |

### 9.2.2 Animal Protein

| Source | Origin | Culinary Role |
|--------|--------|---------------|
| Kharru meat | Domesticated herbivore (→ see [Volume I, Ch.18]) | Festive dishes, "long" stews |
| Thalas fish | Northern ocean (→ see [Volume I, Ch.14]) | Soups, smoking |
| Deep fauna of Gharra | Southern ocean | Salting, fermentation |
| Sulfur-beans | Legumes with high sulfur content | The main "sulfur" component for weekdays |

### 9.2.3 Vegetation

The flora of Théxar (→ see [Volume I, Ch.17]) with its chromophilic palette — from copper-red to golden — sets the color of dishes too: the **"red plate"** — copper, rust, amber tones.

- **Selenium-herb** — a piquant herb, the analogue of salt and pepper;
- **Tar-vine** — a fibrous stalk, the base for dressings;
- **Hinterland roots** — starchy root vegetables, substitutes for tubers;
- **Fermented leaves** — the base of sauces.

**The chromophilic coloring of products** is not merely decorative: the pigment chromophyll (absorption peak 570 nm) participates in light assimilation and in the kitchen carries the marking of ripeness. A golden hue of a fruit guarantees maturity; green (unripe) fruits are rarely eaten.

## 9.3 Culinary Methods

| Method | Share of Cuisine | Description |
|--------|------------------|-------------|
| Fermentation | ~40% of dishes | The key method; "maturing" food is considered an art |
| Boiling | Basic | Soups, porridges, broths |
| Stewing | Festive | "Long" dishes, up to 6 hours |
| Smoking | Fish, meat | Dry processing with tar-vine smoke |
| Baking | Hexagonal ovens | Bread, vegetables |
| Raw serving | A rarity | Only fresh marine fauna |

**Fermentation as a worldview**: ~40% of all products undergo fermentation, and this is no accident. Fermented food keeps without cold (important before the thermonuclear era), contains sulfur compounds (a physiological necessity), and "lives" — transforms over time. The cultural parallel with the debt of knowledge is obvious: **maturing is valued, speed is suspect** (→ see [Volume V, Ch.12, "Ethics and Morality"]).

## 9.4 Table Etiquette

### 9.4.1 The Six Utensils

Six limbs determine the table setting too: the diner's place includes **six utensils** — four hand implements (for the four upper limbs) and two "support" implements (for the middle ones, with which the Xha'Ruun steady their plates). The utensils are used in pairs: the upper pair of hands — cutting, the middle pair — grasping.

### 9.4.2 The Structure of the Meal

| Meal | Time | Composition |
|------|------|-------------|
| First intake | Morning (after rising) | Light: porridge, fermented drink |
| Second intake | Noon | Main: soup, a grain dish, vegetables |
| Third intake | Evening (after sunset) | Festive or family: a protein dish |

Between intakes, a "small snack" (fruits, flatbreads) is permitted — but only outside formal meals.

### 9.4.3 Rules

| Rule | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| They eat in the silence of conversation | The table is an exchange of knowledge, not only of food |
| They do not discuss the prey | Discussing the cost of a dish is bad form |
| The sulfur component is mandatory | A physiological and taste norm |
| The host tastes first | Symbolic check of quality (the "host's debt") |
| Leftovers are not thrown away | They are given "into storage" for fermentation or to those in need |

**The "host's debt"** — the rule by which the host is obliged to taste each dish first: it is both a sign of trust and a practical custom going back to prehistoric times, when testing food for poison was the leader's duty.

## 9.5 National Dishes

### 9.5.1 Festive Cuisine of Kharak

**"Khar-long"** — a festive stew of kharru meat, sulfur-beans, and hinterland roots, simmered for six hours in a hexagonal oven. Served on the days of Renewal (→ see [Volume V, Ch.11, "Holidays"]). The name plays on the "long experiment" (→ see [Volume V, Ch.07]) — the dish symbolizes patience.

### 9.5.2 Spicy Cuisine of Rhuval

**"Rhuval heat"** — a soup of fermented leaves, selenium-herb, and a drop of "sulfur dew" (a concentrate of sulfurous waters). Famous for "burning but not scalding" — the heat comes from sulfur compounds, not capsaicin.

### 9.5.3 Light Cuisine of Vexar

**"Vexar transparency"** — a cold vegetable soup of golden fruits, sulfur-beans, and chromophilic leaves, served in the heat. The philosophy of Vexar (→ see [Volume V, Ch.05, "Philosophy"]) — "transparency" — is read in the cuisine too: the layers are visible, the composition legible.

### 9.5.4 Coastal Dishes of the Island Domain

**"Thalas salt"** — Thalas fish, smoked and salted, with Thalophyta algae (→ see [Volume I, Ch.17]). The salt is sea salt; the sulfur component is the algae, rich in sulfides.

### 9.5.5 Polar Cuisine

**"Polar bread"** — a dense flatbread of ven-ear flour with the addition of fat from the deep fauna of Gharra. A caloric, ascetic dish designed for expeditions; made in reserve for months (→ see [Volume I, Ch.23, "The Cryosphere"]).

## 9.6 Beverages

| Beverage | Base | Character |
|----------|------|-----------|
| **Thar-tea** | Fermented tar-vine leaves | The main drink; served at the end of the meal |
| **Ven-kvass** | A weak fermented drink of khar-millet | Daily, refreshing |
| **Rhuval fire** | A distillate of fermented leaves | Strong, festive |
| **Sulfur dew** | Diluted condensate of hydrogen sulfide waters | Medicinal-preventive, in small doses |
| **Gharra depth** | An infusion of algae with sea salt | Salty-piquant, served with fish dishes |

The alcohol culture is moderate: a post-scarcity society does not use spirits as a commodity of pressure or profit (→ see [Volume III, Ch.03, "Economic System"]); drunkenness in public places is a social stain, not an "everyday phenomenon."

## 9.7 Cuisine and Science: Gastronomy as a Laboratory

The Xha'Ruun kitchen was historically one of the first scientific laboratories: fermentation required understanding microorganisms, sulfur-beans — chemistry, smoking — preservation.

| Discovery | Link to Cuisine | Dates |
|-----------|-----------------|-------|
| Controlled fermentation | Microorganism cultures maintained by "starters" | −4,000 |
| Selenium salt | Extraction of Se-salts from herbs | −3,200 |
| Preservation without cold | Smoking, salting, fermentation | −3,000 |
| Vitamin chemistry | Treatment of "winter weakness" with citrus fruits | +1,100 |
| Food microbiology | Identification of "living" enzymes | +2,300 |

**"Inherited starter"** — a cultural phenomenon: some culinary starters in families have been passed from generation to generation for more than a thousand years. The oldest documented starter (the "khar-mother" of the Khar-Tor district) has been maintained continuously since +1,900 Y.E. — it is registered by the Guild of Keepers as an object of cultural heritage.

## 9.8 Festive and Ritual Cuisine

Holidays (→ see [Volume V, Ch.11, "Holidays and Calendar Rites"]) are inseparable from cuisine; each rite has its mandatory dish.

| Holiday | Dish | Symbolism |
|---------|------|-----------|
| New Year (Vek'shan) | "Khar-long," thar-bread | Renewal, patience |
| Unity Day (Rass-Vek) | "Dish of consent" — six kinds on one dish | Six sides of unity |
| Remembrance Day (Mor-Ghal) | Lean "gray dish" (without a sulfur component) | Grief; a deliberate violation of the norm |
| Renewal (Nur-Vek) | Communal meals "for the whole square" | Universal truce |
| Knowledge Day (Ven-Thex) | "Scholar's snack": fruits, flatbreads, thar-tea | Lightness of mind |

The **"gray dish"** of Remembrance Day — the rarest case of a deliberate violation of the culinary norm: the dish is made without a sulfur component and is therefore taste-wise "unfinished," symbolizing the memory of the irreplaceable (the Red Rot epidemic, −2,120 – −2,105, → see [Volume II, Ch.04, "The Era of Crisis"]).

## 9.9 Culinary Apprenticeship

The transmission of culinary knowledge is an institution parallel to guild education (→ see [Volume III, Ch.07, "Education"]).

| Stage | Age | Content |
|-------|-----|---------|
| "Helper" | 6–10 | Cutting, cleaning, table setting |
| "Carrier" | 10–15 | Preparing simple dishes, caring for the starter |
| "Cook" | 15–20 | Mastering methods, working with fermentation |
| "Master of the kitchen" | 20+ | Authorship of dishes, running a household or establishment kitchen |

**Oral transmission** — is fundamental: recipes are rarely fixed in writing, and even then only as "hints" without exact proportions. The precise tuning ("by eye, by pheromone, by smell") is passed only from master to apprentice in the process of cooking together. A written recipe is considered "incomplete knowledge" — a cultural analogue of the evidentiality principle: knowledge without personal testimony requires a note that it is a record, not an experience.

## 9.10 The Cuisine of Three Species

Inter-species gastronomy is diplomacy and science at once. The physiologies of the three species are incompatible down to the chemistry of solvents: the food of the Xha'Ri (aqueous) is unsuitable for the Kel'vash (sulfuric acid as a solvent) and for the Moryn (deep-sea environment).

| Species | Medium | Food | Shared Meal |
|---------|--------|------|-------------|
| Xha'ri | Terrestrial | Grain, meat, vegetables | — |
| Kel'vash | Highlands, acidic springs | Siliceous rocks, chemoautotrophic symbionts | "Stone meal" |
| Moryn | Depths of Gharra | Chemosynthetic biota of hydrothermal vents | "Light meal" |

**"Stone meal"** — a diplomatic ritual: the Xha'Ri and the Kel'vash hold negotiations while observing each other's meal. Each side "treats" the other not with food but with spectacle and knowledge about food (composition, origin, method of acquisition). The concept of "a meal without food" entered diplomatic vocabulary (→ see [Volume II, Ch.14, "History of the Three Species"]).

The **"light meal"** with the Moryn is an analogous ritual in water: an exchange of bioluminescent "toasts" — light phrases in the "language of light" (→ see [Volume I, Ch.39, "Moryn"]).

## 9.11 Social and Economic Aspects

| Aspect | Fact |
|--------|------|
| Agriculture | ~12% of GDP; hydroponics, vertical farms (→ see [Volume III, Ch.03, "Economic System"]) |
| Public catering | The Guild of Agrarians coordinates the network of "common kitchens" in the districts |
| Food surpluses | Redirected to fermentation or into "storehouses" |
| Food security | Post-scarcity society; hunger is absent as a mass phenomenon |
| Food prohibitions | Few; cultural, not religious |

The **"common kitchens"** — an institution of public catering in every district: any meal is accessible to any resident; payment is symbolic or in labor. The common kitchens are a legacy of the recovery programs after the Dark Ages (→ see [Volume II, Ch.05, "The Era of Revival"]) and a living element of the social fabric.

## Chapter Summary

1. Xha'Ruun cuisine is determined by physiology: six limbs (six utensils), the mandatory presence of sulfur in the diet, a receptor for heavy-metal salts, warm food in winter.
2. The key method is fermentation (~40% of products); "maturing is valued, speed is suspect."
3. Table etiquette codifies culture: the "host's debt," the "meal as an exchange of knowledge," the ban on discussing cost.
4. The kitchen is a historical laboratory of science (fermentation, Se-salt, preservation) and an institution of knowledge transmission ("culinary apprenticeship," "inherited starters").
5. Festive cuisine (from "Khar-long" to the "gray dish" of Remembrance Day) and the inter-species "meal without food" reproduce the history and ethics of the civilization.
