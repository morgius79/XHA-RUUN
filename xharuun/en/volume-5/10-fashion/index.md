## Introduction

Xha'Ruun fashion is a projection of anatomy, climate, and history. Six limbs require a cut that does not restrict mobility; three eyes and tetrachromatic vision (including UV) make color and texture information visible differently to different species; pheromone sensitivity turns fabric into a chemical interface. Historically, clothing went from hides to functional textiles, but the key difference from many civilizations: fashion was never an instrument of class division. The casteless society (→ see [Volume III, Ch.06, "Social Structure"]) built clothing as a **sign of function, not status** — a craftsman and a Council member wear identical basic forms, differing only in details and quality of execution. Modern fashion is a synthesis of guild production, regional traditions, and the ethics of "transparency" (→ see [Volume V, Ch.01, "Art of Xha'Ruun"]): clothing does not hide but presents.

## 10.1 Functional Foundations

### 10.1.1 Anatomy and Cut

The six limbs of the Xha'Ruun — two lower (locomotory) and four upper (front manipulators + middle "veks") — dictate the construction of clothing.

| Element | Function | Constructional Solution |
|---------|----------|-------------------------|
| Base belt | Fixing clothing on the body | Wide belt with six loops for the upper limbs |
| "Veks-sleeve" | Freedom of the middle hands | Separate sleeve with ventilation, not restricting lift and rotation |
| "Chest flap" | Access to the chest heart | Fastener allowing quick opening of the chest area (two hearts — → see [Volume I, Ch.05]) |
| Lower belt | Support | Elastic fit that does not pinch the blood flow of the abdominal heart |

**Key principle**: clothing must not restrict the **lift of the veks** — the middle pair of arms, involved in manipulation, etiquette, and thermoreception, requires freedom. A sleeve that restricts the lift is considered "failed" and is rejected even with decorative merits.

### 10.1.2 Climate Zones

Three climatic regimes — tropical, temperate, and polar — gave rise to three types of clothing.

| Zone | Temperature | Clothing | Material |
|------|-------------|----------|----------|
| Equatorial | +28…+42°C | Minimal: base belt, light draperies | Thin plant fibers |
| Temperate | −2…+18°C | Standard: upper cloak, belt, base trousers | Chromophilic fabrics, kharru wool |
| Polar | −48…−6°C | Multi-layer: thermal underwear, insulated top, hood | Wool of deep-sea animals, synthetic insulation |

The post-scarcity society ensures climatically adequate clothing for all: "freezing in a polar city" is an impossible situation under social protection (→ see [Volume III, Ch.08, "Healthcare"]).

### 10.1.3 Thermoregulation and Heliotherapy

The Xha'Ruun skin can absorb heat through silicon-carbon channels (heliotherapy, → see [Volume I, Ch.05]). Clothing takes this into account:

- **"Light windows"** — open areas of fabric on the back and shoulders, allowing the skin to absorb light;
- **"Shadow shields"** — visors and hoods protecting from overheating;
- **"Pheromone meshes"** — areas with large-knit weave that do not block pheromone emission.

## 10.2 History of Clothing

| Era | Material | Style | Function |
|-----|----------|-------|----------|
| Pre-Civilization (−12,000 – −9,800) | Hides, plant fibers | Draping, wrapping | Protection from cold and injury |
| Early Civilization (−9,800 – −6,200) | Woven linen, leather | First belts and cloaks | Ritual distinction (priest / non-priest) |
| First Empire (−6,200 – −2,100) | Silk, wool, dyes | Class clothing, imperial standard | Demonstration of rank |
| Dark Ages (−2,100 – −750) | Coarse wool, linen | Utilitarian minimalism | Survival |
| Revival (−750 – +850) | Return to fine fabrics | Regional styles, artisanal finish | Domain identity |
| Era of Unity (+850 – present) | Synthetic and natural blends | Functional standard with variations | Guild and regional identity |

**The Imperial period** — the only era when clothing was an instrument of class division. The fall of the Empire and the ensuing Dark Ages (→ see [Volume II, Ch.04, "The Era of Crisis"]) led to a conscious rejection of "clothing of rank": the Era of Unity inherited this rejection as a principle.

## 10.3 Modern Clothing: Standards and Variations

### 10.3.1 The Basic Standard

The basic clothing set of the Xha'Ruun (for the temperate climate):

| Element | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| Base belt | Wide belt of dense fabric with six loops and a fastener |
| Upper cloak | Fabric covering the shoulders and back, with "light windows" |
| "Veks-sleeves" | Separate sleeves for the middle limbs |
| Lower trousers | Dense fabric, elastic fit |
| Footwear | Open type (toes free) or closed (for polar zones) |

The standard is not a uniform but a foundation over which variations are layered.

### 10.3.2 Guild Elements

Each of the 12 guilds (→ see [Volume III, Ch.04, "The 12 Guilds: Structure"]) has distinctive elements, but not a "uniform" in the strict sense.

| Guild | Element | Color/Material |
|-------|---------|----------------|
| Scholars | Hexagonal patch on the belt | Azure |
| Engineers | Metal belt buckle | Bronze |
| Medics | White cloak lapel | White |
| Agrarians | Green belt trim | Green |
| Builders | Reinforced veks-sleeves | Gray |
| Merchants | Narrow belt over the main one | Gold |
| Keepers | Book-symbol on the chest part | Azure with gold |
| Warriors | Flame on the shoulder | Red |
| Artists | Spiral ornament | Purple |
| Miners | Crystal buckle | Crystal |
| Navigators | Star on the collar | Silver |
| Scribes | Feather-symbol on the sleeve | Azure |

**The absence of a full uniform** is fundamental: guild affiliation is marked by a detail, not a cut. A Council member and a craftsman of the same guild wear the same mark; the difference is in the quality of fabric and execution.

## 10.4 Festive and Ritual Clothing

Holidays (→ see [Volume V, Ch.11, "Holidays and Calendar Rites"]) require special clothing.

| Holiday | Clothing | Symbolism |
|---------|----------|-----------|
| New Year (Vek'shan) | White cloak over the basic set | Renewal, purity |
| Unity Day (Rass-Vek) | Six colors on one outfit | Six sides of consent |
| Remembrance Day (Mor-Ghal) | Dark clothing without guild marks | Grief, equality before memory |
| Renewal (Nur-Vek) | The brightest festive clothing | Joy, truce |
| Knowledge Day (Ven-Thex) | Azure elements in all guilds | Priority of knowledge over guild |

**Unity Day** — the only holiday when "six colors" (by the number of limbs) are layered on one outfit: each element (belt, cloak, sleeves, trousers, footwear, accessory) is dyed its own color. This is the visual embodiment of the principle "unity without unification" (→ see [Volume III, Ch.06, "Social Structure"]).

## 10.5 Fabrics and Materials

### 10.5.1 Natural

| Material | Source | Use |
|----------|--------|-----|
| Tar-vine (fiber) | Stems of the tar-vine (→ see [Volume I, Ch.17]) | Light fabrics, summer clothing |
| Kharru wool | Domesticated herbivore | Warm fabrics, polar clothing |
| Deep-sea wool | Animals of the Gharra ocean | Waterproof fabrics |
| Chromophilic threads | Plants with high pigment content | Copper-red and golden fabrics |

### 10.5.2 Synthetic

| Material | Property | Use |
|----------|----------|-----|
| Thermoplastic | Retains heat while thin | Polar clothing |
| Pheromone mesh | Does not block pheromone communication | Everyday clothing |
| UV-permeable fabric | Passes ultraviolet for tetrachromats | Summer clothing |
| Water-repellent composite | Rain protection | Cloaks, capes |

### 10.5.3 Aesthetics of Color

The color palette (→ see [Volume V, Ch.01, "Art of Xha'Ruun"]) is oriented toward the natural hues of the planet.

| Color | Code (example) | Symbolism |
|-------|----------------|-----------|
| Gold | #c9a84c | Light of Khar'Vex |
| Azure | #5b7db5 | Sky of Théxar |
| Bronze | #8b6f4e | Earth |
| Copper | #b87333 | Warmth |
| Purple | #6b3fa0 | Night, Serrin |

**UV-invisible layers** — a practice in which part of the pattern is made with a pigment invisible in the visible spectrum but distinguishable by the Xha'Ruun in UV. This practice produces a "double image" and serves as an aesthetic device inaccessible to the Kel'vash and Moryn.

## 10.6 Clothing Production

### 10.6.1 Guild Production

The Guild of Artists (department "Fabric and Form") and the Guild of Builders (department "Materials") coordinate fabric production. The main principle: **accessibility of the basic standard to all**.

| Indicator | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| Production centers | 240 |
| Employed in fabric production | ~1.2 million |
| Accessibility of the basic set | 100% of the population |

### 10.6.2 Craft Workshops

Alongside guild production, there are workshops creating authorial pieces. The price is not a barrier to access but payment for mastery: in a post-scarcity society (→ see [Volume III, Ch.03, "Economic System"]) clothing is not a necessity good that could be used for pressure.

### 10.6.3 Individual Making

The tradition of individual tailoring survives as part of family culture. The "maternal belt" — a belt passed from the female to the children (regardless of sex) — is a practice linked to the value of knowledge transmission. An old belt is not thrown away but resewn.

## 10.7 Fashion and Ethics

### 10.7.1 The Principle of Transparency

Clothing must not "lie" (→ see [Volume V, Ch.12, "Ethics and Morality"]).

| Prohibition | Justification |
|-------------|---------------|
| Fake guild marks | Appropriating another's status is a kind of lie |
| Imitation of materials | "Wood under metal" — misleading |
| Concealment of defects | Mismatch between appearance and condition is unethical |

### 10.7.2 Ecological Production

The Law of Silence (→ see [Volume III, Ch.09, "The Law of Silence"]) regulates clothing production: the extraction of materials in protected zones is prohibited. Synthetic fabrics are developed as an ecological alternative.

### 10.7.3 The Absence of "Disposable Fashion"

The culture of handing down and resewing clothing is a legacy of the Dark Ages. Clothing is not destroyed but passed on: from older to younger, from those leaving a guild — to those entering. The practice is supported by the guilds and domain councils.

## 10.8 The Fashion of Three Species

The three species do not share a common fashion: physiological and biochemical differences make this impossible.

| Species | Materials | Style | Shared Elements |
|---------|-----------|-------|-----------------|
| Xha'Ri | Fabrics, leather | The system described above | — |
| Kel'vash | Stone, metal, acid-resistant polymers | "Armor," plates | Exchange of ornaments |
| Moryn | Bioluminescent pigments on the skin | "Living light," patterns | Light patterns on Xha'Ri fabrics for rituals |

**The exchange of ornaments** — a practice in which the Kel'vash carve patterns on plates that repeat Xha'Ri textile patterns, and the Xha'Ri apply motifs from the Kel'vash "stone book" to fabrics. This is a form of cultural dialogue without shared clothing.

**Light patterns on fabrics** — a technique in which phosphorescent pigments imitating the Moryn "language of light" are applied to Xha'Ri fabrics. It is used in ritual garments for "light meals" (→ see [Volume V, Ch.09, "Cuisine and Gastronomy"]).

## 10.9 Children's Clothing

| Age | Features |
|-----|----------|
| 0–3 (larva) | No clothing required; temperature is maintained by the environment |
| 3–8 | Minimal; base belt, light cloaks |
| 8–15 | Full set, without guild marks |
| 15+ | Guild elements, choice of style |

The guild mark is put on after choosing a guild (15–20 years). Until that moment, children wear "neutral" clothing without guild affiliation — the principle of "equal start" (→ see [Volume III, Ch.07, "Education"]).

## Chapter Summary

1. Xha'Ruun clothing is determined by anatomy (six limbs, heliotherapy, pheromone communication) and climate (three zones).
2. Historically, clothing went from hides to functional textiles; the imperial "class" period is rejected.
3. Modern clothing is a functional standard with guild and domain variations; status is not marked by cut, only by details.
4. The principle of transparency: clothing must not "lie" (fake marks, imitation of materials are prohibited).
5. The three species do not share a common fashion, but exchange ornaments and light patterns as a form of dialogue.
