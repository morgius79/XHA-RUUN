## Introduction

The sports and games of the Xha'Ruun are shaped by the biology of the species and by historical choice. Biology: three pairs of limbs (two lower, four upper), three eyes with a field of view of ~210°, four ears with precise localization, pheromone communication, and infrasonic hearing (→ see [Volume I, Ch.05, "The Xha'Ruun Species"]) — each feature of the anatomy gave rise to its own disciplines. Historical choice: a civilization that survived the collapse of the First Empire and the Dark Ages consciously built sport on the principle of **testing mastery, not superiority**. The sporting competitions of the Era of Unity are not "elimination games" but a ritualized comparison of paths of self-improvement, in which the loser is not excluded but becomes part of the overall picture. The senary structure (→ see [Volume IV, Ch.09, "The Senary Numeral System"]) permeates the scoring, field layout, and structure of games.

## 8.1 Early Forms: From Hunting to Ritual

The origins of sport lie in the hunting practices of the Pre-Civilization era (−12,000 – −9,800) and the military exercises of the First Empire (−6,150 – −2,150).

| Era | Form | Function |
|-----|------|----------|
| Pre-Civilization | Track running, stone throwing, wrestling | Training for prey and defense |
| Early Civilization | Ritual races at temples | Calendar symbolism |
| First Empire | Military games, kharru racing | Selection, demonstration of strength |
| Dark Ages | Regional folk games | Maintaining communal bonds |
| Era of Unity | Institute of Games, guild tournaments | Testing mastery, unity |

Kharru racing is the oldest sport to survive to the present day. The kharru (Xa-ruunidae, → see [Volume I, Ch.18, "Fauna of Théxar"]) is a large herbivore, domesticated around −10,200 Y.E., the first domesticated species on the planet. Kharru racing reproduces prehistoric hunting and at the same time is a full-fledged discipline of modern sport — the only one in which a contestant competes in a pair with an animal.

## 8.2 The Institute of Games

The **Great Games** (Thar-Vex-Sha, "strength-will-word") is a planetary competition held once every six years in Khar-Vexar. Established in +1,600 Y.E. by the Council of Unity as a replacement for the disbanded imperial military reviews.

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| Frequency | Once every 6 years |
| Venue | Capital arena "Six Circles," Khar-Vexar |
| Participants | ~6,000 (from all guilds, domains, and the three species) |
| Disciplines | 36 (6 × 6) |
| Duration | 12 days (2 × 6) |
| Symbolic number | 6 — completeness |

**Selection** — through domain and guild tournaments; each of the 12 guilds delegates its own six contestants to each of "its" groups of disciplines, each of the 8 domains — its own selected athletes. The three species participate on equal terms: for the Kel'vash and Moryn there are disciplines in their habitats (climbing-"silence" and light-writing-"depth").

**The prize** — not monetary and not symbolic: the winner receives the right to **one line in the General Registry of Achievements**, maintained by the Guild of Keepers. The line is recorded forever and may be supplemented by descendants. It is precisely the status in the Registry, not a medal, that is the goal of the competition — a cultural consequence of the value of knowledge: victory is recorded, not awarded (→ see [Volume V, Ch.07, "Science in Culture"]).

## 8.3 The Senary Structure of Sport

Field layout, scoring, and discipline structure are governed by base 6.

| Aspect | Senary Solution |
|--------|-----------------|
| Lanes | 6 on the arena |
| Teams | 6 players each |
| Tournament bracket | 6 groups of 6 |
| Scoring | Kept in powers of 6 until the reform (see below) |
| Finals | 6 contestants |

**The scoring reform (+3,020 Y.E.)** — a notable case of cultural inertia: senary scoring in sport (1–5, then a place-value transition) persisted longer than in science and trade, and was replaced by decimal only in the 24th century of the Era of Unity after three decades of debate. The decision was motivated by spectator ergonomics: with senary scoring, large numbers required the viewer to do mental conversion, reducing emotional engagement. Sports scoring remained the only domestic domain where the senary system gave up ground (→ see [Volume IV, Ch.09, "The Senary Numeral System"]).

## 8.4 Team Games

### 8.4.1 Khar-Tor (Six-Handed Ball)

The most popular team sport. The field is a hexagon with a side of 40 sha (about 33 m); two teams of 6 players; the ball is woven from tar-vine fibers, one dlan in diameter.

| Element | Rule |
|---------|------|
| Goal | Carry the ball into the opponent's "nest" (hexagonal niche) |
| Limbs | All six limbs may be used, except the lower limbs when standing |
| Prohibition | Contact with the opponent's body — only with the forearms of the middle hands |
| Time | 6 periods of 6 migs (≈ 6 × 3.6 s) |
| Resolution | Draws are not recorded: the sixth period is declared "open" |

**The "Open Period"** — a unique rule: on a draw, the game does not go to extra time; instead a sixth, unplanned period is introduced in which the score starts anew. The cultural logic: a competition has no "winner by time" — it has a completion that must be achieved through play, not by the clock.

### 8.4.2 Thalas-Tor (Aquatic Hexathlon)

Semi-aquatic sports are common in the Island Domain and coastal regions (→ see [Volume III, Ch.05, "The 8 Domains"]). Thalas-Tor is a relay race of six stages alternating swimming, diving, and passing objects during breathing pauses. A feature of the sport: the Xha'Ruun's three-minute breath holds (an adaptation to diving) make possible disciplines inaccessible to humanoid species with different physiology.

### 8.4.3 The Game "Consent"

**Rass-Tor** — an intellectual team game, sometimes called "the most athletic non-athletic game." Two teams of 6 negotiate the distribution of a limited resource (tokens) over six sectors of the field; the goal is to reach a configuration recognized as "consenting" (rass) by the referee before the opponent. The game directly reproduces the structure of the Council of Unity (→ see [Volume III, Ch.02, "The Council of Unity"]) and is used in educational institutions as a practice of negotiation ethics (→ see [Volume III, Ch.07, "Education"]).

## 8.5 Individual Disciplines

### 8.5.1 The Trinocular Throwing Series

The development of trinocular vision and tetrachromatic perception gave rise to throwing disciplines with precision exceeding the capabilities of one-eyed observers:

- **Six Rings** — throwing a disc at six concentric targets; points in powers of 6;
- **Werksan Flight** — throwing a "wing" (asymmetric glider) for distance and precision;
- **Shadow Shot** — shooting at a target in the UV spectrum, invisible to part of the audience (an exceptional discipline in which the result is verified instrumentally).

### 8.5.2 Infrasonic Sports

The Xha'Ruun's hearing range (8 Hz – 42 kHz, → see [Volume I, Ch.05]) makes possible competitions based on the perception of low frequencies inaccessible to most species:

- **Mor-Thash** ("roll call of the depths") — blindfolded players determine by infrasonic echo the position of moving objects;
- **Resonance Six** — holding six sound frequencies in equilibrium with the vocal cords.

### 8.5.3 The Jump of Three

An athletic discipline: a jump with three successive pushes (lower right, lower left, both). The cultural meaning — the "three pushes of the earth" correspond to the three eras of the civilization's memory (→ see [Volume II, Ch.05, "The Era of Revival"]).

### 8.5.4 The Solo Hexathlon

A six-event competition including: running 6 leagues, the Jump of Three, the wing throw, swimming 6 leagues, climbing 60 sha, and "quiet sitting" (see below). Victory is awarded by the sum of places in the six events; historically the hexathlon was a military standard of the First Empire, transformed into a civil discipline.

**"Quiet sitting"** — a puzzling discipline: participants sit motionless for a time determined by the judges from physiological indicators; the winner is not the one who lasted longest, but the one who showed the most even parameters of breathing and thermal radiation. The discipline derives from the meditative practice of the "Resonators" (→ see [Volume V, Ch.06, "Religion and Mythology"]) and demonstrates the cultural norm: **sport can test inner equilibrium as well as outer strength**.

## 8.6 Mind Games

Intellectual games hold in Xha'Ruun culture a status comparable to athletics — a consequence of the value of knowledge (→ see [Volume V, Ch.07, "Science in Culture"]).

| Game | Number of Players | Content |
|------|-------------------|---------|
| **Mor-Tor** ("path of strength") | 2 | Strategy on a hexagonal board; 6 kinds of pieces |
| **Ven-Thek** ("truth of writing") | 2–6 | Composing and refuting statements with evidentiality markers |
| **Gharra-Mor** ("depth of time") | 2 | A game on a 3-dimensional board; moves through "layers" |
| **Six Questions** | 2–6 | A question game: guessing an object with the minimum number of "observer's questions" |

**Ven-Thek** — the only known strategic game built directly on a grammatical category. A player making a move is obliged to mark his statement with a source marker: `-ven` (I know), `-sha` (I believe), `-tor` (I infer), `-rass` (I retell). An erroneous marker is treated as a lie and brings penalty points — a direct transfer of evidential ethics (→ see [Volume IV, Ch.06, "Evidentiality"]) into the game space.

## 8.7 The Three Species in Sport

Inter-species sport is one of the most visible embodiments of the Law of Consent (→ see [Volume II, Ch.14, "History of the Three Species"]).

| Species | Disciplines | Specifics |
|---------|-------------|-----------|
| **Xha'ri** | Hexathlon, khar-tor, mind games | Main participant in the planetary Games |
| **Kel'vash** | Climbing-"silence," long balance, load-carrying | Endurance competition with minimal thermal trace |
| **Moryn** | Light-writing-"depth," current-races | Bioluminescent communication instead of a sound start |

**The principle of proportionality**: in mixed disciplines, results are normalized on the physiological scales of the species — otherwise the competition would become a demonstration of anatomy. The principle of proportionality is the subject of continuous disputes between the guilds and the species colleges, and the dispute itself is considered a healthy part of the Institute of Games.

**Light-writing-"depth"** deserves special mention: a Moryn competition in which participants at a depth of 1,200–3,000 sha compose bioluminescent "phrases" of maximum complexity in minimum time. Observation is conducted from bathyscaphes and through light projectors; the judges decipher what was recorded. The discipline has been granted honorary status in the Games, since it requires the judging panel to know the "language of light" (→ see [Volume I, Ch.39, "Moryn"]).

## 8.8 Children's Games and Upbringing

The play culture of childhood is built as preparation for adult norms — counting, ethics, and the scientific method.

| Game | Age | Skill Developed |
|------|-----|-----------------|
| **Six Stones** | 3–5 | Counting in base 6 |
| **Hide and Seek with Echo** | 4–7 | Training auditory localization |
| **Question-Answer** | 5–8 | Distinguishing evidentiality markers |
| **Long Experiment** | 7–10 | Long-term planning (adaptation of an inter-species metaphor) |
| **Backyard Khar-Tor** | 8–12 | Team coordination of six limbs |

Game vocabulary passes into adult usage: "sixth period" — a situation that cannot be resolved by time; "open game" — negotiations without a predetermined outcome; "to play a long experiment" — to act on a long-term horizon (→ see [Volume V, Ch.07, "Science in Culture"]).

## 8.9 Sport and Art

Spectator sport intersects with art in several specific forms:

| Form | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **Movement drama** | Choreographic productions on sporting themes (→ see [Volume V, Ch.01, "Art of Xha'Ruun"]) |
| **Rhythmic hexathlon** | A gymnastic discipline performed to live music in senary 6/8 meters (→ see [Volume V, Ch.04, "Music"]) |
| **Athlete portrait** | A genre of painting: depicting an athlete at the moment of effort with anatomical precision |
| **Race sonification** | Sound transmission of races for blind spectators |

**Rhythmic hexathlon** — a bridge between sport and music: a complex of exercises performed to 6/8 meters, with the mandatory synchronization of the movements of six limbs with the six notes of the octave. The discipline requires equal development of musical and athletic hearing, possible with four-ear perception.

## 8.10 Sports Ethics: "Fair Play Without Winners"

The ethical core of Xha'Ruun sport is the formula worked out after the Games of +1,600 Y.E.: **"testing mastery, not superiority."**

| Norm | Content |
|------|---------|
| Recording, not awarding | Victory is entered in the Registry of Achievements |
| Draws are acceptable | Draws are recorded if completion is not achieved through play |
| Doping ban | Disruption of the metabolic baseline — "destruction of the body-instrument" |
| Insult ban | Demonstrating superiority (the "winner's dance") — entered in the code as a violation |
| Mandatory transmission | The winner is obliged to conduct a "path review" for the losers |

**The "path review"** — a post-competition procedure: the winner explains to the losers the technique, decisions, and mistakes. Refusal to review is treated as concealment of knowledge (ven-mor) and entails exclusion from the Registry (→ see [Volume V, Ch.12, "Ethics and Morality"]). Sport thus reproduces the key cultural asymmetry: concealment of knowledge is worse than defeat.

**Doping** is perceived not as cheating but as metabolic interference destroying the "body-instrument" — a concept rooted in the value of self-improvement through effort, not through substances.

## 8.11 Regional Traditions

Each domain brought its own disciplines to planetary sport (→ see [Volume III, Ch.05, "The 8 Domains"]).

| Domain | Contribution | Character |
|--------|--------------|-----------|
| Kharak-Thar | Khar-tor, arena "Six Circles" | Official standard |
| Kharak-Tor | Kharru racing | Rural tradition |
| Rhuval-Ven | Thalas-tor, boat racing | Water trade |
| Rhuval-Irkhen | Heavy athletics, load-carrying | Industrial labor |
| Vexar-Shan | Mor-Tor, mind games | Philosophical school |
| Vexar-Mor | Infrasonic sports | Energy culture |
| Island | Deep diving, light-writing | Seafaring |
| Polar | "Long March" (ice marathon) | Scientific stations |

The **"Long March"** of the Polar Domain is a multi-day crossing of the ice sheet (→ see [Volume I, Ch.23, "The Cryosphere"]) with full self-sufficiency; the discipline is considered the most ascetic and is held once every twelve years.

## Chapter Summary

1. Xha'Ruun sport is shaped by six-limbed anatomy (3 pairs of limbs, trinocular vision, infrasonic hearing) and by a historical renunciation of the military function.
2. The Great Games — a planetary competition once every 6 years, 36 disciplines, the prize being a line in the General Registry of Achievements.
3. The senary structure permeates sport: field layout, scoring, tournament structure; senary sports scoring gave way to decimal in +3,020 Y.E.
4. The principle of "testing mastery, not superiority": draws are recorded, the winner is obliged to conduct a "path review," concealment of technique is treated as concealment of knowledge.
5. Sport is an inter-species practice (the principle of proportionality) and a school of culture: children's games reproduce evidentiality, senary counting, and the debt of knowledge.
