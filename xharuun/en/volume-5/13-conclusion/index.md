## Key Ideas

1. **Culture as memory** — every element (holiday, clothing, cuisine, sport) reproduces history. Xha'Ruun culture is a mechanism of memory transmission through the body: silence, ritual, taste, movement.

2. **The scientific method as language** — the mandatory evidentiality of the language makes the scientific method a norm of speech. Culture did not "teach" the people the scientific method — it inherited a language in which one cannot speak otherwise.

3. **The asymmetry of ethics** — ignorance is safe, concealment of knowledge is grave. Losing does not lower one's standing; refusing a "path review" after a victory does. The asymmetry is shaped by the memory of catastrophes.

4. **Six as a constant** — from sports lanes to poetic lines, from notes to calendar rites — the number 6 permeates culture, echoing the six limbs and the six letters of the genetic code.

5. **Unity in diversity** — "unity without unification" is reproduced at all levels: guild marks on an identical cut, domain styles with a common language, three species exchanging ornaments.

## Culture and History

Xha'Ruun culture is a direct product of historical catastrophes. Every ethical principle is an answer to a concrete event (→ see [Volume V, Ch.12, "Ethics and Morality"]): the debt of knowledge — to the Dark Ages, the Law of Consent — to the conflicts with the Kel'vash and Moryn, the ban on self-aware AI — to the philosophical dispute of +2,000–2,400 Y.E.

---

## Culture and Language

The language and culture of the Xha'Ruun are inseparable. The evidential system (→ see [Volume IV, Ch.06]) is not only a grammatical category but also an ethical norm reproduced every time a speaker says `-ven` or `-rass`. The senary structure (→ see [Volume IV, Ch.09]) is not only a way of counting but also the structure of holidays, sport, and poetry. Xha'Ruun culture is a language that became a norm.

---

## Culture and the Three Species

The three species do not share a single culture, but they exchange its elements: the Kel'vash "stone meals," the Moryn "light meals," the exchange of ornaments in clothing. This exchange is not unification but dialogue, in which each side preserves its physiological and cultural identity (→ see [Volume II, Ch.14, "History of the Three Species"]).

Culture faces new challenges:

- **The digital age** — how will traditional institutions (the book, the square, the ritual) change?

The answers to these questions will be written by the next chapter of history (→ see [Volume II, Ch.07, "Modernity"]).

## Summary

1. Xha'Ruun culture is a mechanism of memory transmission through the body (ritual, taste, movement, silence).
2. The scientific method is a norm of speech, not a profession; the evidentiality of the language makes scientific honesty grammatical.
3. Ethics is asymmetric: concealment of knowledge is worse than theft; "I do not know" is safer than "I know."
4. Six is a cultural constant: from anatomy to poetry, from sport to the calendar.
5. The three species preserve their differences, exchanging ornaments and rituals, but not shared clothing.
